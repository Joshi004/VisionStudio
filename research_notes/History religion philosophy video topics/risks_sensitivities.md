# Risks and Sensitivities of AI-Generated Religion, Mythology and History Videos on YouTube (India-based creator, as of Nov 14, 2026)

> Not legal advice. Points marked "consult a lawyer" need an Indian media/criminal lawyer. Research cutoff: Nov 14, 2026. Pre-2024 items are marked [OLDER].

## Q1. Indian law: BNS religious-offence sections, IT Rules 2021 and the 2026 synthetic-content amendment, and recent FIRs

### Takeaway
The IPC's religious-offence sections carried over into the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) almost word for word. Section 299 (insulting religion) now names "electronic means" explicitly, and Section 196 (promoting enmity) names "electronic communication." Separately, since **20 Feb 2026**, amended IT Rules require AI content that looks real to carry a prominent label and provenance metadata, and YouTube (as a significant social media intermediary) must collect a declaration from the uploader and verify it. In 2025–26 police repeatedly registered FIRs over AI videos touching religion and history. FIRs and arrests can happen long before any conviction, so the investigation itself becomes the penalty.

### Cited Findings
**BNS provisions (in force since 1 July 2024)**
- **BNS 299** replaces IPC 295A. It punishes "deliberate and malicious intention of outraging the religious feelings of any class… by words… signs… visible representations or through electronic means" with up to 3 years, a fine, or both. The only change in wording is the addition of "through electronic means." — [Veritect IPC→BNS comparison](https://veritect.ai/old-law-new-law/hurting-religious-feelings-bns-298-302-vs-ipc-295-298); text also at [Devgan.in BNS Ch. 16](https://devgan.in/bns/chapter_16.php)
- BNS 299 is cognizable and non-bailable, triable by a first-class magistrate. A court needs prior sanction from the Central or State Government (BNSS s.217(1)(a)) before it can take cognizance. That sanction rule applies only to s.299, not to ss.298/300/301/302. — [Veritect](https://veritect.ai/old-law-new-law/hurting-religious-feelings-bns-298-302-vs-ipc-295-298)
- **BNS 196** replaces IPC 153A. It covers promoting enmity on grounds of religion, race, caste, community etc. "by words… visible representations or through electronic communication." Punishment is up to 3 years, rising to 5 years if committed in a place of worship or religious assembly. — [CJP IPC vs BNS hate-speech sections (PDF)](https://cjp.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Hate-Speech-sections-IPC-and-BNS.pdf); [LawCodeHub](https://www.lawcodehub.com/compare/ipc-hate-speech-vs-bns-196-197)
- **BNS 302** replaces IPC 298 (deliberate intent to wound a person's religious feelings; up to 1 year). **BNS 197** replaces 153B (imputations prejudicial to national integration). **BNS 353** replaces IPC 505 (statements conducing to public mischief). — [Scroll, Harsh Mander](https://scroll.in/article/1092763/harsh-mander-indias-hate-speech-poison-cant-be-legislated-away); [Devgan.in](https://devgan.in/bns/chapter_16.php)
- **BNS 152** (successor to sedition: "endangering sovereignty", including misinformation on social media) was used against social-media posters after the Nagpur violence in March 2025. — [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/mumbai/nagpur-violence-sedition-firs-curfew-9897135/)

**IT Rules 2021, as amended (G.S.R. 120(E), notified 10 Feb 2026, in force 20 Feb 2026)**
- Defines "synthetically generated information" (SGI) as audio/visual content "artificially or algorithmically created… in a manner that such information appears to be real, authentic or true, and depicts or portrays any individual or event in a manner that is, or is likely to be perceived as indistinguishable from a natural person or a real world event." — [MeitY FAQ (PDF)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/065b6deb585441b5ccdf8be42502a49c.pdf)
- **Prohibited SGI** (Rule 3(3)(a)(i)) includes SGI that violates any law, including the BNS, and SGI that "falsely depicts or portrays a natural person or real-world event by misrepresenting, in a manner that is likely to deceive, such person's identity, voice, conduct, action, statement, or such event as having occurred." — [MeitY Gazette notification (PDF)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/f55fe52418b03f58b0669f6a8bc03b6d.pdf)
- **Permitted SGI must be "prominently labelled"**: visible on screen for video, and announced at the start for audio. It must also carry "permanent metadata or other appropriate technical provenance mechanisms… including a unique identifier." Platforms must not allow labels or metadata to be removed. — [MeitY Gazette](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/f55fe52418b03f58b0669f6a8bc03b6d.pdf); [MeitY FAQ Q22](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/065b6deb585441b5ccdf8be42502a49c.pdf)
- MeitY's FAQ gives an example: a lawful AI video "should carry a visible 'synthetically generated' notice". A TTS narration should open with an audio disclosure such as "This audio is synthetically generated". — [MeitY FAQ](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/065b6deb585441b5ccdf8be42502a49c.pdf)
- **Rule 4(1A), for significant social media intermediaries such as YouTube**: before publishing, the platform must obtain a user declaration on whether the content is SGI, verify it with technical measures, and label confirmed SGI. Knowingly permitting non-compliant SGI counts as a due-diligence failure. — [MeitY FAQ Q24–26](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/065b6deb585441b5ccdf8be42502a49c.pdf)
- **Exclusions**: routine good-faith editing, accessibility/translation, and "educational/training materials… illustrative, hypothetical… or conceptual content" are not SGI, provided no false documents or records are created. "Lawful uses such as labelled satire or creative synthetic works… may be permitted so long as synthetic content is appropriately declared/labelled." — [MeitY FAQ Q6–7](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/065b6deb585441b5ccdf8be42502a49c.pdf)
- **Takedown timelines shortened**: content must come down within **3 hours** of a court order or government notice (previously 36 hours). Grievance disposal is now 7 days (was 15). Certain urgent grievances must be handled in 36 hours (was 72). — [MeitY FAQ](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/065b6deb585441b5ccdf8be42502a49c.pdf); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/centre-notifies-it-rules-amendment-to-regulate-ai-generated-and-synthetic-content-522623)
- The FAQ also mentions an earlier IT Rules amendment dated 22 Oct 2025. — [MeitY FAQ](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/065b6deb585441b5ccdf8be42502a49c.pdf)

- **Ujjain (Sept 2026)**: five content creators were booked for circulating **AI-generated videos** falsely showing a whole mosque being bulldozed, which police believe provoked clashes. 14 people were arrested. — [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/madhya-pradesh/over-20-arrested-police-search-for-content-creators-of-ai-generated-videos-that-led-to-ujjain-violence/article71524881.ece)
- **[OLDER, Apr 2023]** Ghaziabad police arrested a man for sharing an AI-generated image claimed to depict Prophet Muhammad. — [News24 Hindi](https://hindi.news24online.com/state/ghaziabad-police-arrested-man-for-making-objectionable-picture-of-prophet-muhammad-viral/209646/)

### Inferences
- The legal threshold for s.299 is "deliberate and malicious" intent. In practice, FIRs are registered on complaint or even suo motu (Ballari) before courts examine intent. Sanction is only needed before a court takes cognizance, not before an FIR or arrest. The realistic exposure for a small creator is FIRs in several states, police notices, device seizure and legal costs, even if the case is eventually quashed. **Consult a lawyer** on anticipatory bail and multi-FIR consolidation strategy before publishing anything in a high-risk category.
- A cinematic, photoreal AI video of a real historical person or event very likely meets the SGI definition ("appears real… indistinguishable from a natural person or real-world event"). Labelling is therefore effectively mandatory through YouTube's declaration flow. Clearly stylised or animated content may fall outside SGI, but how the definition applies to stylised historical dramatisation is untested. **Consult a lawyer.**
- There is a residual risk that an unlabelled or poorly labelled dramatisation of a contested historical event (for example, an invented scene of a ruler committing an atrocity) could be argued to be "prohibited SGI" that falsely depicts a real person or event "likely to deceive". That would push it beyond labelling into takedown territory. Clear on-screen framing as a dramatisation reduces, but may not remove, this risk.
- The IT Rules bind intermediaries, not creators directly. A creator's obligations flow through YouTube's terms and through general criminal law (the BNS). Content in violation can be removed within hours of a government notice.

### Gaps
- I found no reported case yet of a creator prosecuted specifically for failing to label SGI under the 2026 rules.
- I did not find specific 2024–26 FIRs over **Tipu Sultan** content in this search. Tipu remains politically contested, especially in Karnataka, but I have no sourced 2024–26 incident.
- I did not verify Supreme Court jurisprudence on s.295A/299 (e.g., *Ramji Lal Modi* (1957) on the "deliberate and malicious" threshold, or quashing standards). A lawyer should confirm the current position.
- I did not research state-specific laws (e.g., anti-conversion laws, or Odisha's proposed Jagannath temple law amendment mentioned below).

## Q2. Controversies over AI depictions of deities and religious figures, 2025–2026

### Takeaway
AI depictions of Hindu deities in casual or invented scenarios, AI animation of Sikh Gurus, and any depiction of Prophet Muhammad have each triggered police complaints or FIRs. One AI video placing Jesus "above" Hindu deities led to protests and an FIR in 2026. Even a big creator (Dhruv Rathee) deleted an AI Sikh-history video within days. A big studio's AI Mahabharat drew heavy "disrespect / AI slop" backlash, though without legal action.

### Cited Findings
- **Sikh Gurus, May 2025**: Dhruv Rathee's AI video "Banda Singh Bahadur Ki Katha" ("The Sikh Warrior Who Terrified the Mughals") depicted Guru Gobind Singh and the Sahibzadas. The SGPC president said "no film or animation depicting the Gurus or their families can be created, as the SGPC has already passed resolutions imposing a complete ban on such portrayals". He threatened legal action. — [SGPC official statement](https://sgpc.net/sgpc-objects-to-youtubers-animation-video-on-guru-sahib/)
- The acting Akal Takht jathedar condemned the video, and the DSGMC filed a complaint with Delhi Police. Manjinder Singh Sirsa demanded a 295A case and objected to "depicting Guru Gobind Singh Ji as a crying child". Rathee removed the video. — [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/chandigarh/youtuber-dhruv-rathee-video-sikh-history-objections-10015928/)
- SAD chief Sukhbir Badal said such portrayals violate the Sikh *rehat maryada*, which "prohibits visual representations of Guru Sahiban". He also criticised a "misleading narrative" and a lack of respectful language. — [ThePrint/PTI](https://theprint.in/india/ai-visuals-of-sikh-gurus-land-dhruv-rathee-in-soup-sgpc-sad-demand-immediate-takedown/2630875/)
- Rathee's statement: "some viewers feel strongly that any animated depiction of the Sikh Gurus conflicts with their beliefs… I do not want this to become a political or religious controversy." — [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/chandigarh/sgpc-demands-removal-of-ai-video-featuring-guru-gobind-singh-by-youtuber-dhruv-rathee/articleshow/121276014.cms)
- **Hindu deities, Jan 2026**: an AI video posted from cricketer Rinku Singh's account showed Shiva, Hanuman, Vishnu and Ganesha in sunglasses riding in an SUV to Western music. Karni Sena filed a police complaint ("Making our deities wear glasses and dance to English songs will not be tolerated"). Police said they were verifying before registering an FIR. — [News18](https://www.news18.com/cricket/police-complaint-filed-by-karni-sena-against-rinku-singh-over-ai-video-of-hindu-deities-ws-l-9842562.html); [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/sports/2026/01/19/police-complaint-against-rinku-singh-after-viral-facebook-video-karni-sena-slams-cricketer-s-jihadi-mindset.html)
- **Lord Jagannath, Jan 2026**: the Puri temple administration filed a police complaint over AI images and videos (for example, milk poured on the deity by a crane), calling them "fabricated… with no basis in temple tradition". The temple managing committee decided to seek a law amendment to make such content punishable. — [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/odisha/ai-videos-of-lord-jagannath-surface-police-complaint-filed/article70527952.ece)
- **Jesus and Hindu deities (interfaith ranking), June 2026**: more than 50 AI videos on a Facebook page claimed Jesus was the "father" of Shiva and Vishnu and more powerful than Hanuman. VHP and Bajrang Dal protested in Raipur, and an FIR was registered under **BNS 299** against unknown persons. — [New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Jun/28/ai-generated-videos-depicting-jesus-as-father-of-hindu-deities-spark-protests-in-chhattisgarh)
- **Prophet Muhammad**: there is an Indian AI-image arrest from 2023 [OLDER] ([News24](https://hindi.news24online.com/state/ghaziabad-police-arrested-man-for-making-objectionable-picture-of-prophet-muhammad-viral/209646/)). In Indonesia in Sept 2026, a crowd gathered at a religious leader's premises over a video alleged to insult the Prophet. He claimed it was AI-edited, and police mediated. — [iNews.id](https://www.inews.id/news/nasional/abah-setu-digeruduk-massa-gegara-diduga-hina-nabi-muhammad-ngaku-korban-ai); [IndonesiaOnline](https://indonesiaonline.co.id/abah-setu-dipanggil-polisi-video-dugaan-hina-nabi-diklaim-editan-ai/). Turkey detained a cartoonist over a Prophet caricature. — [AP](https://apnews.com/article/turkey-satirical-magazine-prophet-cartoon-a46cc0258d9f76430a84189e5c900165)
- **AI Mahabharat (JioHotstar, from Oct 2025)**: launched as the "first ever premium entertainment series fully powered by AI". The top YouTube trailer comment read "We Don't Want Fully AI Based Films…Especially Mahabharat…" (2,600+ likes), and Reddit called it "AI slop" and "disrespectful". — [C21Media](https://www.c21media.net/news/jiohotstars-ai-made-indian-mythological-microdrama-receives-mixed-response-online/). Viewers mocked anachronisms such as a modern bedside table. — [India Today](https://www.indiatoday.in/entertainment/ott/story/ai-mahabharat-trends-as-viewers-spot-modern-bedside-table-joke-chargers-missing-2812606-2025-11-03). IMDb rating was 1.6/10. — [IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt38781492/ratings/). Despite this, it reported 26.5M+ views. — [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/fresh-take/ai-mahabharat-ek-dharmayudh-storytelling-10370652/)

### Inferences
- Three patterns trigger complaints:
  - **Casual or comic "modernisation" of deities** (sunglasses, cars, Western music).
  - **Invented rituals or events** not found in tradition (the Jagannath crane).
  - **Hierarchy claims between religions** (Jesus as "father" of Hindu gods).
- For Sikh Gurus, the objection is to **any** human-form depiction, regardless of how respectful it is. For the Prophet, the prohibition on depiction is widely held in Islam and violations carry a risk of violent reaction, both in India and abroad.
- Even a high-quality, respectful AI version of Hindu epics faces an "AI is disrespectful to sacred stories" reputational headwind. This is separate from legal risk.
- Backlash amplifies with reach and the creator's perceived identity (e.g., communal framing in the Rinku Singh and Sameer MD cases). A small channel is less likely to be noticed, until a video goes viral.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024–26 India-specific incidents over AI depictions of **Buddha** or **Jesus alone**, as opposed to the interfaith-ranking case. Buddhist objections to disrespectful use of Buddha imagery exist (notably in Sri Lanka), but I did not source recent examples.
- I did not find SGPC's written resolution text, only statements referring to it.
- Whether depicting Shiva, Rama or Krishna reverentially in AI (as opposed to casually) has drawn FIRs is unclear. The incidents found all involved irreverent, invented or interfaith-hierarchy content.

## Q3. YouTube policies: hate speech, AI disclosure, monetization ("inauthentic content"), advertiser-friendly guidelines

### Takeaway
Religion is a protected attribute under YouTube's hate speech policy. Realistic AI content must be disclosed, and YouTube may auto-label AI content or penalise creators who don't disclose. Since July 2025 (clarified July 2026), YouTube has refused to monetise "inauthentic" mass-produced, template-based AI content. That is arguably the biggest platform risk for a fully AI channel. Advertiser guidelines demonetise content that portrays beliefs "in a malicious way", but religion is not itself in the "controversial issues" category.

### Cited Findings
**AI disclosure**
- Creators must disclose AI content that "makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do", "alters footage of a real event or place", "generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur", or "creates music that's the main focus of the video". — [YouTube Help: Disclosing use of GenAI content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- Non-realistic AI content (e.g., "someone riding a unicorn through a fantastical world", "AI-generated… animation… in a fully animated video") and minor edits do not require disclosure. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- "AI generated music" must be disclosed. Photorealistic AI gets a label on the player, while non-photorealistic or animated AI may be labelled in the description. YouTube says "disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- YouTube may auto-label content with C2PA metadata or content its systems detect as AI. Creators who "consistently choose not to disclose" risk removal or suspension from the Partner Program (YPP). — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)

**Hate speech**
- Protected attributes include **religion** and caste. Prohibited content includes dehumanisation, slurs and stereotypes, claims that a group is inferior, "conspiratorial claims that individuals or groups are evil, corrupt, or malicious based on their protected group status", and denial of well-documented violent events. — [YouTube Hate speech policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801939?hl=en)
- There is an exception for educational, documentary, scientific or artistic content, but "this context must appear in the images or audio of the video itself. Providing it in the title or description is insufficient." AI disclosure "is not a free pass to violate our policies." — [YouTube Hate speech policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801939?hl=en)
- Penalties: a first violation usually brings a warning, then strikes. Three strikes in 90 days can terminate the channel. YouTube also penalises content that "exposes a group with protected group status to risks of physical harm based on the local social or political context." — [YouTube Hate speech policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801939?hl=en)

**Monetization: inauthentic content**
- On 15 July 2025, YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content", clarifying that this includes "repetitive or mass-produced" content. — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Not monetizable: "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective"; "templated storylines"; and the same storyline template repeated across videos. Allowed: "using AI to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented" and AI-assisted "unique, well-researched, or creative narrative". — [YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- The July 2026 clarification defines three non-monetizable buckets: (1) generic, repetitive or template-based content; (2) "unsatisfying or off-putting" emotionally manipulative content; (3) AI personas presented as human experts on sensitive topics (health, legal, finance, **politics**). Channels with "too much" of any bucket lose YPP. — [TechCrunch, 20 Jul 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/)
- Reviewers look at the channel's main theme, most-viewed and newest videos, metadata and About section. — [YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)

**Advertiser-friendly guidelines**
- Under "Hateful & derogatory": "Portraying ideologies or beliefs in a malicious way by generalizing or disparaging" earns **no ad revenue**. Offensive material "used for education, news, or in a documentary" gets **limited** ads. Content "referencing protected groups… in a non-hurtful manner" can earn full ads. — [YouTube advertiser-friendly guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- The "Controversial issues" category covers child abuse, sexual abuse, self-harm, suicide, eating disorders, domestic abuse and abortion. **Religion is not listed.** — [YouTube advertiser-friendly guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- Violence: "dead bodies… shown in historical events like wars, as part of an educational video" can earn ads. "Dramatized violence that results in serious injury where the aftermath… is visible" and highly graphic descriptions of tragedies get limited ads. — [YouTube advertiser-friendly guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- "Sensitive events" (conflict, terrorism, mass violence) may be demonetised wholesale, though documentary context can still be monetised. — [YouTube advertiser-friendly guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)

### Inferences
- A cinematic AI history channel with realistic reenactments and AI music should **always select "Yes" on AI disclosure**. That satisfies YouTube's policy and the 2026 Indian SGI rule, and YouTube says it doesn't hurt reach or monetization.
- Brutal history (sieges, massacres, executions such as Sambhaji's) risks "limited ads" because of dramatised gore. Keep violence implied rather than shown.
- The bigger YPP risk is looking "templated": the same AI narrator, same visual style and same story arc every video. Original research, distinct scripts and a visible creator perspective matter more than the topic.
- "Conspiratorial claims that groups are evil/corrupt" can be triggered by history videos that blame a present-day religious community for historical events (e.g., "Muslims destroyed X", "Brahmins oppressed Y" framed as group guilt).

### Gaps
- I did not fetch YouTube's **misinformation / manipulated content** policy. To my knowledge it covers manipulated media posing serious risk of egregious harm, but this is unverified in this session.
- No public data shows how YouTube's India review team applies the hate speech policy to mythology content specifically.
- YouTube's response to the Indian 2026 rules (e.g., whether its "AI use" toggle is now mandatory or whether it adds provenance metadata) was not confirmed.

## Q4. Comparatively safe vs high-risk topic areas and treatments

### Takeaway
Risk is driven less by "religion vs not religion" and more by four factors:
- whether a **living devotional community in India** reveres the figure;
- whether the treatment **invents, modernises or mocks**;
- whether the topic maps onto a **current communal political fault line** (Mughal vs Maratha/Hindu, temple–mosque disputes);
- whether the video **ranks religions**.

Non-Indian ancient mythology, philosophy, engineering and daily-life history are low-risk on all four.

### Cited Findings
- Incidents found cluster around: Hindu deities in casual or modern scenarios ([News18](https://www.news18.com/cricket/police-complaint-filed-by-karni-sena-against-rinku-singh-over-ai-video-of-hindu-deities-ws-l-9842562.html)); invented rituals ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/odisha/ai-videos-of-lord-jagannath-surface-police-complaint-filed/article70527952.ece)); interfaith hierarchy ([New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Jun/28/ai-generated-videos-depicting-jesus-as-father-of-hindu-deities-spark-protests-in-chhattisgarh)); Sikh Guru depiction ([SGPC](https://sgpc.net/sgpc-objects-to-youtubers-animation-video-on-guru-sahib/)); Aurangzeb/Shivaji/Sambhaji ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/pune-news/13-booked-in-solapur-for-social-media-post-on-aurangzeb-101742070454902.html)); a living temple institution ([The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/karnataka/sowjanya-murder-case-video-youtuber-sameer-md-booked-for-offending-religious-sentiments)); and AI fabrication of current mosque/temple events ([The Hindu, Ujjain](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/madhya-pradesh/over-20-arrested-police-search-for-content-creators-of-ai-generated-videos-that-led-to-ujjain-violence/article71524881.ece)).
- YouTube explicitly allows monetizing "a unique, well-researched, or creative narrative" made with AI tools. — [YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Historical war deaths in educational context can carry full ads. — [YouTube advertiser-friendly guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)

### Inferences (risk tiering is my synthesis, not a sourced ranking)
**Lower risk** (still apply respectful, accurate treatment):
- Greek, Norse, Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Mayan/Aztec and Celtic mythology. These are not living mass religions in India. (Note: some modern pagan/Heathen communities exist, but no incidents were found.)
- Philosophy and thought experiments: Stoicism, Socrates and Plato, Zeno's paradoxes, the Ship of Theseus, the trolley problem, Confucius and Laozi as philosophy.
- Ancient science and engineering: Roman concrete, pyramids, stepwells, the Antikythera mechanism, Indus Valley drainage, Iron Pillar metallurgy.
- Daily life in ancient civilisations, lost cities (Pompeii, Angkor, Mohenjo-daro, Hampi as architecture), trade routes, and history of inventions.
- Teachings of texts presented as ideas without depicting the founder (e.g., Bhagavad Gita concepts, Buddhist ethics, Stoic texts). Use abstract or landscape visuals rather than an AI "Krishna" or AI "Buddha".

**Medium risk** (needs care, sourcing, and possibly avoiding figure depiction):
- Reverent depiction of Hindu epics (Ramayana/Mahabharata). Low legal risk if faithful, but there is a strong reputational "AI disrespects sacred stories" headwind, as the AI Mahabharat showed. Avoid comic or modern touches and invented scenes. State which version you follow (e.g., Valmiki vs Tulsidas).
- Buddha and Jesus depicted reverently. Common in global art and less legally fraught in India, but avoid sexualised, comic or commercial use.
- Ancient Indian empires with less present-day communal charge (Maurya, Gupta, Chola, Vijayanagara as architecture and administration), presented factually.

**High risk** (avoid or treat with legal review):
- **Any depiction of Prophet Muhammad**, including faceless or symbolic depictions; also his family and companions.
- **Any human-form depiction of the Sikh Gurus**, Sahibzadas or their families. Sikh history can only be told without depicting them (e.g., text, calligraphy, landscapes, gurdwara architecture), and even then the narrative accuracy is scrutinised.
- Mughal vs Maratha/Rajput/Hindu history (Aurangzeb, Shivaji, Sambhaji), Tipu Sultan, temple-destruction narratives, Partition, Ayodhya, Kashi/Mathura, Somnath. Both "glorifying" and "villainising" framings have drawn FIRs.
- Deities in casual, comic, modern, romantic or invented scenarios; "what if" deity crossovers; deity "power rankings".
- Interfaith comparisons that rank religions, or claim one religion's figures are derived from or subordinate to another's.
- Living gurus, temple institutions or sect heads; caste-origin narratives (e.g., varna origin stories framed as group superiority).
- Photoreal AI "reconstructions" of real historical figures doing or saying things not in the record, especially atrocities. This is both SGI-deception risk and hate-speech framing risk.

## Q5. Best practices for handling sacred material respectfully

### Takeaway
Sourced evidence on what "respected creators" do is thin. The clearest documented lessons come from failures:
- remove depictions that the community forbids;
- don't modernise or mock deities;
- don't invent rituals;
- don't rank faiths;
- put context inside the video itself, not just in the description.

The practices below combine those sourced lessons with clearly marked inferences.

### Cited Findings
- When challenged, Rathee removed the video and explicitly acknowledged the community's view that "any animated depiction of the Sikh Gurus conflicts with their beliefs". This quick-takedown response defused escalation. — [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/chandigarh/sgpc-demands-removal-of-ai-video-featuring-guru-gobind-singh-by-youtuber-dhruv-rathee/articleshow/121276014.cms)
- Sukhbir Badal urged creators to "exercise utmost caution and sensitivity while dealing with Guru Sahiban or Sikh history" and said "accurate historical representation [is] non-negotiable". He also flagged "want of respectful language" (honorifics). — [ThePrint/PTI](https://theprint.in/india/ai-visuals-of-sikh-gurus-land-dhruv-rathee-in-soup-sgpc-sad-demand-immediate-takedown/2630875/)
- Experts criticised the Jagannath AI videos as having "no basis in temple tradition". Fidelity to tradition is the yardstick devotees apply. — [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/odisha/ai-videos-of-lord-jagannath-surface-police-complaint-filed/article70527952.ece)
- Indian Express's critique of AI Mahabharat: "the problem is not that AI was used. The problem is that the taste wasn't". It called for "actual writers" and editors who "know how to say 'no'". — [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/fresh-take/ai-mahabharat-ek-dharmayudh-storytelling-10370652/)
- YouTube requires educational or documentary context to appear in the video's images or audio, not just the description. — [YouTube Hate speech policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801939?hl=en)
- MeitY's example disclosures: a visible "synthetically generated" notice on video, and an opening audio line for synthetic narration. — [MeitY FAQ](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/065b6deb585441b5ccdf8be42502a49c.pdf)

### Inferences (practical checklist; not sourced as industry standard)
1. **Labelling**: toggle YouTube's "AI use = Yes". Add an on-screen "AI-generated / dramatised reconstruction" card at the start, and consider a spoken opening line, which matches MeitY's audio-disclosure example. Keep tool metadata/C2PA intact; don't strip it.
2. **Cite the source text and version on screen**, e.g., "Based on Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda" or "Translation: Emily Wilson's Odyssey". Note where traditions differ instead of picking one as "the truth".
3. **Don't depict** the Prophet, the Sikh Gurus or their families. Use light, calligraphy, architecture, landscapes or text. For other sacred figures, consider symbolic or non-face depiction when uncertain.
4. **No modernisation, comedy or invented scenes** for deities or revered figures. Use honorifics where the community expects them (Guru Sahib, Chhatrapati, Prophet with ﷺ/PBUH if mentioned).
5. **History framing**: attribute claims to named historians or primary sources. Present contested questions as contested. Avoid assigning guilt to present-day communities. Keep violence implied.
6. **Thumbnails and titles**: avoid "exposed", "dark truth", "real face of [religion/figure]" and ranking language ("X vs Y: who is more powerful"). These framings attract complaints and the advertiser "malicious portrayal" bucket.
7. **Pre-publish review** for any Indian-religion or Indian-history episode: get a cultural or community reader where possible. A lawyer should review high-risk topics.
8. **Have a takedown and response plan**: monitor comments in the first 48 hours, be willing to unlist quickly if a community body objects, and keep research notes and source files as evidence of good faith (relevant to the "deliberate and malicious" intent test).
9. **Avoid YPP "inauthentic content" risk**: vary narrative structure, add a distinct editorial voice, and avoid a single repeated template.

### Gaps
- I found no published style guide or code of conduct from respected mythology/history YouTubers (e.g., channels like Kings and Generals, Overly Sarcastic Productions, or Indian channels such as Epified) on handling sacred material. This would need separate research.
- I found no authoritative guidance from Islamic bodies in India on AI imagery specifically, or a formal Buddhist or Christian Indian body statement on AI depictions, in this search.
- Whether on-screen disclaimers have any legal weight under BNS 299 in India is untested. **Consult a lawyer.**
