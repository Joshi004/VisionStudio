## 1. 3dfx: why the late-1990s market leader collapsed, and what Nvidia did differently

### Takeaway
3dfx had the best technology and the strongest brand around 1997–98. It lost mainly for two reasons. First, in December 1998 it bought the board maker STB, which turned its own customers into competitors who then switched to Nvidia chips. Second, it could not keep up with the six-month product cycle that PC makers demanded. Nvidia stayed a chip-only supplier to every board maker, shipped on a fast and regular schedule, and backed Microsoft's DirectX standard. In December 2000 Nvidia bought 3dfx's assets for about $70M.

### Cited Findings
- **Peak:** 3dfx was founded in 1994, and by 1998 its Voodoo chips "dominated the retail market for video cards." Things started going wrong in 1999: "The company had trouble getting new products out and Nvidia, a well-oiled manufacturing machine, introduced chips that outperformed the Voodoos." — [Forbes, Dec 18, 2000](https://www.forbes.com/2000/12/18/12183dfx.html)
- **Retail versus PC makers:** 3dfx led in retail, but "that segment accounts for just 10% of the overall market." Most of the market was chips sold to PC makers (OEMs). — [Forbes, Dec 2000](https://www.forbes.com/2000/12/18/12183dfx.html)
- **The STB purchase:** 3dfx agreed to buy STB Systems on Dec 14, 1998, for $141M. The deal closed in May 1999. — [Level2 Ventures](https://level2.vc/news/a-short-story-of-3dfx); [The Silicon Underground](https://dfarq.homeip.net/why-3dfx-failed-in-the-year-2000/); [EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/ballard-steps-down-as-head-of-struggling-graphics-chip-maker-3dfx/)
- **Why STB was a problem:**
  - STB had been Nvidia's largest customer, and Nvidia cut it off after the deal. — [EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/ballard-steps-down-as-head-of-struggling-graphics-chip-maker-3dfx/); [Silicon Underground](https://dfarq.homeip.net/why-3dfx-failed-in-the-year-2000/)
  - "As a result, former customers began using Nvidia chips, thereby giving 3dfx more competitors." — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/2000/12/18/12183dfx.html)
  - Blogger Dave Farquhar's reading: once 3dfx made its own boards, "Nvidia's chips didn't have to be the best. If Nvidia's chips were competitive at all, every other maker of graphics cards would rather buy chips from them rather than from a competitor." — [Silicon Underground](https://dfarq.homeip.net/why-3dfx-failed-in-the-year-2000/)
- **Manufacturing and quality:** STB's factory in Juarez, Mexico "was unable to compete on cost and quality" with the Asian contract manufacturers that built Nvidia's boards. Its quality-control problems drove some system builders to Nvidia-based cards. — [Level2 Ventures](https://level2.vc/news/a-short-story-of-3dfx); [Silicon Underground](https://dfarq.homeip.net/why-3dfx-failed-in-the-year-2000/)
- **Product cycles:** The OEM business required "the ability to consistently produce new products within the six-month cycle required by PC manufacturers; 3dfx did not have the methodology or mindset to operate in this business model." — [Level2 Ventures](https://level2.vc/news/a-short-story-of-3dfx)
- **Nvidia's matching discipline:** Tae Kim's *The Nvidia Way* (as quoted in a review) says that by the end of 1999 Nvidia had organized design and production around a "Three Teams, Two Seasons" strategy, alongside its "Speed of Light" philosophy. — [Rational Flaneur review of *The Nvidia Way*](https://rationalflaneur.com/the-nvidia-way-meets-the-intelligent-fanatic/)
- **Technology bets 3dfx lost:**
  - 3dfx assumed gamers would not want 32-bit color because it would drop frame rates. Nvidia's RIVA TNT pursued it anyway.
  - Developers moved away from 3dfx's own Glide software interface toward Microsoft's DirectX and OpenGL. — [Level2 Ventures](https://level2.vc/news/a-short-story-of-3dfx)
- **Late, weak products:** The delayed "Napalm" chip arrived after Nvidia had already shipped a newer DirectX 7 part. Voodoo 5 "was already significantly inferior to the competition in both quality and price." — [Level2 Ventures](https://level2.vc/news/a-short-story-of-3dfx)
- **Draining the cash:** 3dfx bought GigaPixel for $186M in March 2000, which "left the company stripped of its financial reserves." — [Level2 Ventures](https://level2.vc/news/a-short-story-of-3dfx)
- **The end:**
  - 3dfx stopped making boards on November 16, 2000.
  - On December 15, 2000, with creditors starting bankruptcy proceedings, it agreed to sell its assets to Nvidia. — [Silicon Underground](https://dfarq.homeip.net/why-3dfx-failed-in-the-year-2000/)
  - The price was $70M in cash plus 1 million Nvidia shares. Nvidia also lent 3dfx $15M, and the two companies settled their patent lawsuits. — [WIRED, Dec 2000](https://www.wired.com/2000/12/3dfx-fall-good-voodoo-for-nvidia/)
- **Nvidia's model at the time:** Nvidia made chips and sold them to board makers such as Creative Labs, Hercules, and ELSA. It was also Microsoft's partner on the Xbox chipset, "a deal 3Dfx came very close to securing." Nvidia did not buy the 3dfx board business. — [WIRED, Dec 2000](https://www.wired.com/2000/12/3dfx-fall-good-voodoo-for-nvidia/)
- **Contemporary view:** Scott Miller, CEO of 3D Realms (the Duke Nukem studio), said in 2000: "because of the STB purchase, I think they lost focus on chip innovation and maintaining their technical lead." — [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/2000/12/3dfx-fall-good-voodoo-for-nvidia/)
- **Irony (secondary source):** According to *The Nvidia Way*, as summarized in a review, 3dfx had at one point planned to buy Nvidia out of bankruptcy after Nvidia's first chips failed. Nvidia later bought 3dfx's patents and hired about 100 of its engineers. — [Review of *The Nvidia Way* (cryptonews.net republication)](https://cryptonews.net/news/finance/31237886/). Unverified against the book itself.

### Inferences
- The story frames well as "the leader who tried to own everything versus the supplier who sold to everyone." 3dfx tried to capture the board makers' profit and lost the board makers. Nvidia's later habit of selling through many partners, and later through every cloud provider, has the same shape.
- Speed beat brilliance. 3dfx's technology was not obviously worse in 1998. Its delivery schedule was.

### Gaps
- No primary 3dfx filings were checked. The exact date and court of the formal bankruptcy filing (believed to be 2002) were not verified.
- The claim that 3dfx planned to buy Nvidia rests on a secondary review of Tae Kim's book, not on the book's text.

## 2. Intel: money and factories, the $20B Nvidia idea, Larrabee, missing mobile and AI, and Intel in 2024–2026

### Takeaway
Intel had far more money and its own factories, and it still missed the GPU in three ways:
- Its board turned down a reported proposal from Paul Otellini in 2005 to buy Nvidia for up to $20B.
- It bet instead on Larrabee, an in-house GPU made from many small x86 processor cores, and cancelled it as a graphics product in December 2009.
- It lost Mellanox to Nvidia in a 2019 bidding contest.

Intel also passed on the original iPhone chip. By 2024 it had lost $18.8B in a year. In August 2025 the US government took a 9.9% stake, and in September 2025 Nvidia agreed to invest $5B (closed December 2025). As of November 2026, Intel's AI accelerators are still a minor factor. Its turnaround depends on its factory business, which signed its first outside customer for its most advanced 18A process in November 2026.

### Cited Findings
**The 2005 Nvidia idea (reported, anonymous sources)**
- *NYT, Oct 24, 2024 (Steve Lohr and Don Clark):* In 2005, CEO Paul Otellini "presented the board with a startling idea: Buy Nvidia... The price tag: as much as $20 billion." Some Intel executives believed graphics chips "could eventually take on important new jobs in data centers." "But the board resisted, according to two people familiar with the" discussion. — [New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/10/24/technology/intel-ai-chips-mistakes.html?smid=url-share&unlocked_article_code=1.Uk4.08OJ.SCT6kgtDjvcz)
- The board's objections, as reported:
  - It would have been Intel's most expensive acquisition.
  - "Intel has a poor record of absorbing companies."
  - Otellini backed off, and one attendee called it "a fateful moment." — [PCGamesN summarizing NYT](https://www.pcgamesn.com/intel/nvidia-buyout-report); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/intels-former-ceo-reportedly-wanted-to-buy-nvidia-for-usd20-billion-in-2005-nvidia-is-worth-over-usd3-trillion-today)
- Scale: Intel was worth about $150B at the time. The $20B figure was nearly four times the $5.4B AMD paid for ATI. Otellini died in 2017. — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/intel/nvidia-buyout-report)
- Instead, the board backed Larrabee, an in-house graphics project led by Pat Gelsinger, who later became Intel's CEO. — [Quartz summarizing NYT](https://qz.com/intel-former-ceo-tried-buy-nvidia-ai-chips-openai-1851680111); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/intels-former-ceo-reportedly-wanted-to-buy-nvidia-for-usd20-billion-in-2005-nvidia-is-worth-over-usd3-trillion-today)

**Larrabee: announced 2007, cancelled as a graphics card in December 2009**
- Reuters, Dec 6, 2009: Intel "scrapped plans to launch an advanced graphics chip based on its novel Larrabee design, after concluding that delays in the project would make it uncompetitive." An Intel spokesman said "silicon and software development are behind." The first chip was kept as a development platform for software and high-performance computing. — [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-intel/intel-scraps-graphics-chip-based-on-larrabee-idUSTRE5B51QR20091206/?type=technologyNews)
- Why it failed, in Intel's own words: Tom Piazza said at Intel's developer forum in September 2010, "it's impractical to try to do all the functions in software... we ran into a performance per watt issue," and "Naturally a rasterizer wants to be fixed function." — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/graphics-cards/computing-components/processors/intel-larrabee-was-impractical-716960); [PC Perspective](https://pcper.com/2010/09/intel-admits-larabee-was-impractical-and-power-hungry/)
- Analyst view (Real World Tech, 2009): Intel lacked "a decade of experience developing high performance drivers," and "every month of delays is equivalent to losing 3.9% performance" relative to Nvidia and ATI. — [Real World Tech](https://www.realworldtech.com/larrabee-deferred/)

**Missing mobile**
- Otellini told The Atlantic in May 2013 that Apple wanted a chip for the original iPhone "at a certain price... and not a nickel more and that price was below our forecasted cost... in hindsight, the forecasted cost was wrong and the volume was 100x what anyone thought." He added: "My gut told me to say yes." — [The Atlantic, May 2013](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2013/05/paul-otellinis-intel-can-the-company-that-built-the-future-survive-it/275825/); [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2013/5/16/4337954/intel-could-have-been-inside-the-original-iphone-says-outgoing-ceo)
- Caveat: The Verge noted it is questionable whether Intel's chips of that era could have met iPhone power needs. — [The Verge, 2013](https://www.theverge.com/2013/5/16/4337954/intel-could-have-been-inside-the-original-iphone-says-outgoing-ceo)

**Losing Mellanox to Nvidia (2019)**
- In January 2019, Israeli media reported that Intel had bid $5.5–6B for Mellanox. — [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-mellanox-m-a-intel/intel-offered-up-to-6-billion-for-israels-mellanox-reports-idUSKCN1PO0O8/?feedName=technologyNews&feedType=RSS)
- On March 11, 2019, Nvidia won with $125 per share in cash, an enterprise value of about $6.9B. — [Nvidia](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-to-acquire-mellanox-for-6-9-billion); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2019/03/11/nvidia-intel-mellanox-deal/)

**Intel 2024–2026**
- *Full-year 2024:* revenue of $53.1B and a net loss of $18.8B. — [Intel press release, Jan 2025](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1726/intel-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-financial). It was Intel's worst loss since 1986. — [Japan Herald/syndicated](https://www.japanherald.com/news/278529858/trump-unveils-usd8-9-billion-intel-rescue-us-to-own-9-9-equity-stake)

The stake is passive, with no board seat. — [Intel press release](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1748/intel-and-trump-administration-reach-historic-agreement-to); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/us-take-10-equity-stake-intel-trumps-latest-corporate-move-2025-08-22/)
- *Nvidia's investment in Intel (verified):*
  - Announced Sept 18, 2025: $5B of Intel common stock at $23.28 per share.
  - Intel will build Nvidia-custom x86 CPUs for data centers, and PC chips that combine an Intel CPU with Nvidia RTX GPU chiplets, connected by NVLink. — [Nvidia press release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2025/NVIDIA-and-Intel-to-Develop-AI-Infrastructure-and-Personal-Computing-Products/default.aspx)
  - Closed Dec 26, 2025: 214,776,632 shares for $5.0B, under an agreement dated Sept 15, 2025. — [Intel 8-K](https://www.intc.com/filings-reports/all-sec-filings/content/0000050863-25-000204/intc-20251226.htm); [Reuters, Dec 29, 2025](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/nvidia-takes-5-billion-stake-intel-under-september-agreement-2025-12-29/)
  - Reuters called it "a major financial lifeline" that made Nvidia "one of Intel's largest shareholders." — [Reuters, Sept 18, 2025](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/nvidia-bets-big-intel-with-5-billion-stake-chip-partnership-2025-09-18/)
- *Intel's AI chips (Tom's Hardware, 2026):*
  - Gaudi 3 is shipping.
  - Falcon Shores was cancelled for commercial release and is used internally only.
  - Crescent Island, an inference GPU, is due to begin customer sampling in the second half of 2026.
  - Jaguar Shores is expected in 2027 at the earliest. Tom's notes Intel's "track record in AI acceleration has been consistently weak" on software. — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/intel-chip-roadmap-2026-2028)

  - April 22, 2026: Elon Musk said Tesla plans to use Intel's 14A process for its "Terafab" project. Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan has said Intel would exit leading-edge manufacturing if it could not land an outside customer. — [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/tesla-ceo-musk-says-company-plans-use-intels-14a-process-terafab-2026-04-22/)
  - October 2026: Intel's stock fell 45%, and Intel moved some Oregon capacity from 18A to the older Intel 3 process. The Ohio factory is "nearly five years behind schedule." — [CNBC, Nov 2, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/02/intel-foundry-faces-critical-test-after-stocks-45percent-plunge-in-october.html)
  - November 18, 2026: Tan said Intel "just signed the first customer" for 18A, without naming it. The customer is on the 18A-P variant. — [CNBC, Nov 18, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/intel-first-18a-foundry-customer.html)

### Inferences
- Three missed chances make a clean storyline: buying Nvidia in 2005, the iPhone chip around 2006, and Mellanox in 2019. In each case Intel's financial caution or its x86-first worldview won out. Within about 20 years, Nvidia went from Intel's reported acquisition target to the investor rescuing it.
- Larrabee shows a cultural point. Intel assumed that its x86 processor design plus its factory lead could win anywhere. Graphics turned out to need special-purpose hardware and a decade of driver software, exactly the area where Nvidia had invested.
- My estimate of the Nvidia stake: 214.8M shares is roughly 4–5% of Intel after the government stake. This is not a sourced figure, so confirm the exact percentage before using it.

### Gaps
- I could not access the full NYT article. Details beyond the reported quotes (for example, who attended the board meeting) are unverified. Neither Intel nor Nvidia has publicly confirmed the 2005 discussion.
- I did not verify Intel's exact AI-chip revenue or market share for 2026, or the name of the first 18A customer.
- I did not research Intel's Nvidia chipset license dispute and its 2011 settlement, a possible side story.

## 3. ATI and AMD: the 2006 ATI deal, the reported Nvidia merger talks, and why OpenCL and ROCm lagged CUDA

### Takeaway
AMD bought ATI for $5.4B in 2006. Former AMD employees later said AMD first explored a deal with Nvidia, which fell apart because Jensen Huang insisted on being CEO of the combined company. That account is reported but not confirmed by either company. AMD's GPUs have often been competitive as hardware, but its software for general-purpose GPU computing (OpenCL, and later ROCm) has trailed CUDA for close to 20 years. As of 2026, SemiAnalysis says ROCm is improving fast but still falls behind when AI models run across many linked machines, which is how frontier AI labs actually use their chips. AMD's biggest opening is a 6-gigawatt OpenAI deal signed in October 2025, with first deliveries due in the second half of 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **The ATI deal:** a $5.4B cash-and-stock deal, confirmed in 2006. — [Tom's Hardware (20th anniversary piece)](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/amd-confirmed-usd5-4-billion-ati-acquisition-20-years-ago-today-deal-to-reinvent-our-industry-paved-the-way-for-radeon-gpu-innovation-apus-and-games-console-domination)
- **Nvidia first (reported):** Forbes reported in Feb 2012, citing "former AMD employees," that AMD "approached graphics processor designer Nvidia about an acquisition before snapping up Nvidia rival ATI in 2006." — [Forbes, Feb 22, 2012](https://www.forbes.com/sites/briancaulfield/2012/02/22/amd-talked-with-nvidia-about-acquisition-before-grabbing-ati/)
- **The CEO condition:** "Jen-Hsun Huang reportedly insisted that he become chief executive of the combined company," and AMD CEO Hector Ruiz chose ATI instead. — [Tom's Hardware, 2012](https://www.tomshardware.com/news/AMD-ATI-Nvidia-GPU-Tegra,14795.html)
- **A second recollection:**
  - Former AMD engineer Hemant Mohapatra recalled the merger talks. He said Huang would not sell unless made CEO, and tied this to Huang's long-term strategy around the proprietary CUDA platform.
  - He also said AMD "never thought of them (Nvidia) in the same league as Arm/Intel," and that ATI then had more overall market share. — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/insider-says-nvidia-almost-sold-half-of-itself-to-amd-but-jensen-huang-wanted-to-be-ceo-of-the-merged-company)
  - Flag: one person's recollection, years later. Note that CUDA launched in 2006, so this may be hindsight framing.
- **Why CUDA won (Stratechery, 2020):** "CUDA is free, but that is because the integration is so tight. CUDA only works with Nvidia video cards, in large part because many of the routines are hand-tuned and optimized." — [Stratechery, "Nvidia's Integration Dreams"](https://stratechery.com/2020/nvidias-integration-dreams/)
- **ROCm in 2026 (SemiAnalysis):**
  - It praised AMD for moving stable ROCm support into the popular vLLM inference software in January 2026.
  - But "CUDA parity has not yet been demonstrated."
  - The chief internal complaint is "a persistent lack of stable GPU clusters for internal software development teams" and for automated testing.
  - It still updated its view to "a great chance of success" if AMD fixes those risks. — [SemiAnalysis, "Can AMD break the CUDA Moat?" (2026)](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/can-amd-break-the-cuda-moat-amd-advancing)
- **SemiAnalysis benchmarks:** AMD's MI355X performs competitively when only some optimizations are switched on. With all three major techniques labs use combined (splitting the "prefill" and "decode" stages of inference, wide expert parallelism, and FP4 low-precision math), "AMD's performance is currently not competitive with Nvidia's." — [SDxCentral summarizing SemiAnalysis InferenceX](https://www.sdxcentral.com/news/nvidia-pulls-ahead-as-amds-software-stack-falls-short-report/)
- **Public evidence of the testing gap (Aug 2026):** On the vLLM-Omni project's GitHub, a contributor counted "over 59+ tests currently missing from ROCm," blamed on "lack of AMD provided CI GPUs." — [GitHub vllm-omni issue #5731](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm-omni/issues/5731)
- **AMD's counter-strategy (2026):**
  - ROCm is "100% open source, up to but not including firmware."
  - AMD is betting on Triton, a chip-neutral programming language, as "the great equalizer."
  - It plans a release every six weeks. — [Nacionale summarizing EE Times](https://www.nacionale.com/en/articles/can-amd-catch-nvidia-rocm-vs-cuda-amd-stock-up-jensen-huang-resigns) (secondary aggregator)
- **AI writing the porting code (reported):** Anthropic co-founder Tom Brown said its model "Claude Mythos" wrote or converted most of the software code needed for AMD chips. — [BusinessKorea (2026)](https://www.businesskorea.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=281548) (single secondary source)
- **The OpenAI deal (Oct 6, 2025):**
  - 6 gigawatts of AMD Instinct GPUs over multiple generations, with the first 1 GW of MI450 starting in the second half of 2026.
  - OpenAI received a warrant for up to 160M AMD shares at $0.01 each. It vests in stages tied to deployments and to AMD stock-price targets rising to $600. — [AMD press release](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1260/amd-and-openai-announce-strategic-partnership-to-deploy-6-gigawatts-of-amd-gpus); [AMD 8-K](https://ir.amd.com/financial-information/sec-filings/content/0001193125-25-230895/0001193125-25-230895.pdf)
  - AMD shares rose more than 34% on the news. — [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/amd-signs-ai-chip-supply-deal-with-openai-gives-it-option-take-10-stake-2025-10-06/)
- **Recent numbers:** AMD's Data Center segment revenue (which includes server CPUs) was $6.718B for the quarter ended June 27, 2026, up 107%. — [Safeguard.sh roundup](https://safeguard.sh/resources/blog/ai-chip-market-share-2026-nvidia-custom-silicon-training-vs-inference) (secondary)
- **Market reaction:** AMD shares rose about 6% on the day Huang resigned (Nov 12, 2026). — [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resigns-in-blow-to-ai-boom-5021980)

### Inferences
- AMD's problem has rarely been the chip itself. It is the years of software, testing, and developer habit around the chip. Nvidia treated software as the product, while AMD treated it as a supporting feature for longer.
- The 2006 fork is a strong documentary moment. Had the merger happened with Huang as CEO, Nvidia would have become part of a CPU company. Instead, Nvidia stayed independent and launched CUDA the same year.

### Gaps
- I found no primary historical sources on why OpenCL (the cross-vendor standard created in 2008–09) failed to catch CUDA. A follow-up should look for AnandTech coverage or developer histories.
- Neither AMD nor Nvidia has confirmed the merger talks. Dates and price were never reported.
- I could not confirm whether OpenAI's first 1 GW MI450 cluster was running as of November 2026.

## 4. Google TPUs, Amazon Trainium, and other custom chips: what they are and why they still mostly haven't displaced Nvidia (late 2026)

### Takeaway
Custom chips (also called ASICs) are designed for a single customer or job, usually with Broadcom or Marvell as design partner, and are built by TSMC. By late 2026 they are Nvidia's most serious competition:
- Google released four TPU systems in 2026 and now sells TPUs outside its own cloud.
- Amazon's chip business has passed a $25B annual run rate, and Anthropic has committed to it for ten years.
- Estimates of Nvidia's share of AI-chip revenue fell from about 87% (2024) to roughly 70–80% (2026).

Nvidia still grew revenue 103% year over year in its quarter ending October 2026. The same cloud companies building their own chips remain among its biggest customers, and the overall market is growing faster than any rival can take share. Share is shrinking; absolute dominance is not, yet.

### Cited Findings
**Google TPUs**
- Google built TPUs "more than a decade ago, alongside Broadcom," originally for its own search computing. It "has now begun selling them outside of its cloud-computing operation." — [Bloomberg via Yahoo Finance, Nov 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/google-escalates-nvidia-rivalry-specialized-123000247.html)
- Broadcom has co-designed seven generations of TPUs since 2014. — [Tom's Hardware, May 2026](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/custom-ai-asics-examined-from-broadcom-to-mtia)
- Nov 9, 2026: Google announced TPU 8tx (for confidential and secure inference) and TPU 8i Max (for very long inputs). Together with TPU 8t and 8i, "This is the first time we have introduced four TPU systems in a single year." — [Google blog, Nov 9, 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/tpu-8i-max-and-8tx/)
- Bloomberg Intelligence estimates:
  - TPU sales will reach $135B by 2028, up from an estimated $12B in 2026.
  - TPUs "cost about 30% less than Nvidia's GPUs but do a competitive job in terms of throughput." — [Bloomberg via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/google-escalates-nvidia-rivalry-specialized-123000247.html) (analyst estimate, not a company figure)
- Attacking the CUDA lock-in: Google open-sourced "TorchTPU" in October 2026, which lets PyTorch code and Hugging Face models run natively on TPUs. — [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/business/Industry/google-launches-two-ai-chips-as-it-seeks-to-challenge-nvidias-dominance/article71686071.ece)
- Benchmarks (secondary report of SemiAnalysis):

## 5. Nvidia's own failures (NV1, GeForce FX, the 2008 laptop chips, Tegra, Arm, crypto)

### Takeaway
Nvidia nearly died at least once and stumbled repeatedly:
- **NV1 and the Sega chip (1995–96):** a wrong technical bet, survived thanks to Sega's goodwill.
- **GeForce FX (2003):** lost the performance crown to ATI.
- **Laptop chips (2008):** a $196M charge for chips that failed in the field.
- **Smartphones (2011–2015):** lost to Qualcomm.
- **Arm (2020–2022):** a deal killed by regulators, costing a $1.25B fee.
- **Crypto (2018 and 2022):** two crypto-driven inventory hangovers, plus a 2022 SEC penalty for not disclosing crypto's role in 2017 sales.

The recurring pattern: Nvidia admitted the mistake fairly fast, cut losses, and moved its effort to the next market.

### Cited Findings
**NV1 and the Sega near-death experience (1995–1997)**
- NV1 (1995) drew graphics with curved surfaces ("quadratic texture mapping") instead of the triangles the industry adopted. Microsoft's DirectX then made triangles the standard. — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/103083-how-dreamcast-almost-bankrupted-nvidia-before-sega-executive.html); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-sega-1995-5-million-rescue)
- Huang (NTU commencement speech, May 2023): "After one year of development, we realized our architecture was the wrong strategy. It was technically poor." Finishing the Sega console chip meant "inferior technology, [been] incompatible with Windows"; not finishing meant running out of money. "Either way, we would be out of business." — [CNBC, Mar 30, 2024](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2024/03/30/nvidia-ceo-these-soft-skills-saved-my-2-trillion-company.html)
- Huang to Sequoia: "we would finish the project and die, or not finish the project and die right away." Sega kept paying "some 5 million dollars, I think it was... It was all the money that we had." — [Time Extension quoting Sequoia interview](https://www.timeextension.com/features/flashback-how-the-kindness-of-sega-saved-nvidia-from-going-under)
- Sega's then-US chief, Shoichiro Irimajiri, recalled in Sept 2026 that he persuaded Sega to turn leftover development funds into a $5M stake, as a "parting gift." He described Huang as "honest, passionate and deeply knowledgeable." — [Japan Times, Sept 12, 2026](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/09/12/companies/sega-nvidia-investment/)
- The turnaround: RIVA 128 (1997), a triangle-based DirectX chip, "sold more than 1 million units in four months." — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2024/03/30/nvidia-ceo-these-soft-skills-saved-my-2-trillion-company.html)
- Sega later sold its stake for about $15M. — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/103083-how-dreamcast-almost-bankrupted-nvidia-before-sega-executive.html)

## 6. The moat in plain terms (CUDA and developers, complete systems and Mellanox, yearly cadence, TSMC), plus the late-2026 leadership shock

### Takeaway
Nvidia's moat has four layers:
1. **CUDA (2006) and its developers.** Nvidia says more than 7.5 million developers use CUDA and its tools. Nearly 20 years of code, tools, and trained engineers assume Nvidia hardware.
2. **Complete systems.** Nvidia sells whole data-center racks, built from GPUs, CPUs, NVLink connections, and networking from Mellanox (bought for about $6.9–7B in 2019–20), not just chips.
3. **A yearly product rhythm.** Announced in June 2024 and now delivering (Vera Rubin shipped in Q3 FY27).
4. **Deep supply relationships,** above all with TSMC, where Nvidia is the largest customer for the CoWoS advanced packaging its chips need.

Analysts such as Ben Thompson argue that the CUDA layer has weakened, because most AI developers now work on top of large language models rather than CUDA, and the biggest buyers can afford to build alternatives. The new moat is system-level: making thousands of chips work as one machine. A new risk emerged in November 2026: the founder who embodied the culture resigned.

### Cited Findings
**CUDA and developers**
- From Nvidia's FY2026 annual report (10-K, fiscal year ended Jan 25, 2026):
  - "With our introduction of CUDA in 2006, we opened the parallel processing capabilities of our GPU to a broad range of compute-intensive applications."
  - "There are over 7.5 million developers worldwide using CUDA and our other software tools."
  - "More than half of our engineers work on software."
  - Over $76.7B invested in research and development since the company's founding. — [Nvidia 10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)
- The developer count was 4.7 million two years earlier, per the FY2024 10-K. — [Moat Explorer, citing 10-Ks](https://moatexplorer.com/nvidia/the-moat/cuda-software-moat/) (secondary)
- The 7.5M count includes "other software tools," so it is broader than people who write CUDA code. No independent count exists. — [Software Lifecycle](https://www.software-lifecycle.com/profiles/nvidia)
- Nvidia's GPUs and networking power over 78% of the TOP500 supercomputers, and its ecosystem includes about 6,000 applications (FY2026 10-K). — [10-K summary](https://smart-goblin.com/NVDA/10-K/2026-01-25-0001045810-26-000021/sections) (secondary)
- The developer network effect, in Thompson's words: "you're just more likely to be able to hire low level engineers if your stack is on Nvidia." — [Stratechery, 2025](https://stratechery.com/2025/google-nvidia-and-openai/)
- Thompson's core thesis: "the most important thing to understand about Nvidia is that it is not a hardware company, and not a software company: it is a company that integrates both." — [Stratechery, "Nvidia Waves and Moats," 2024](https://stratechery.com/2024/nvidia-waves-and-moats/)
- The counter-argument (Thompson, 2024): "Nvidia's CUDA moat has been diminished; the vast majority of development in AI is no longer happening with CUDA libraries, but rather on top of LLMs." — [Stratechery, 2024](https://stratechery.com/2024/ai-integration-and-modularization/)

- Huang to Reuters: Nvidia bought Mellanox to help stitch servers "together into one giant computer... - April 27, 2020: the deal closed at a "transaction value of $7 billion" after China's approval. Huang called it "a homerun deal." Mellanox's Israel operation had about 2,500 employees. — [Nvidia, Apr 27, 2020](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-completes-acquisition-of-mellanox-creating-major-force-driving-next-gen-data-centers); [Jensen's letter](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/building-the-new-nvidia-together/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/04/27/nvidia-ceo-calls-mellanox-acquisition-a-homerun-deal.html)
- The 10-K says Mellanox "enabled our platforms to be data center scale." Its Blackwell design connects "36 Grace CPUs and 72 Blackwell GPUs in a data center scale, liquid-cooled design." — [Nvidia 10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)

That means splitting AI work across many machines, which turns inference into "a distributed-systems problem rather than a single-kernel one." — [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/can-amd-break-the-cuda-moat-amd-advancing)

**Yearly product cadence**

## 7. Jensen Huang's "zero-billion-dollar markets": creating markets before they exist

### Takeaway
Huang's stated philosophy is to go after problems so new that the market is "zero billion dollars." There are no customers yet, and therefore no competitors. CUDA (from 2006) and AI (2012–2015) were such bets. CUDA nearly broke Nvidia financially, because it added cost to every chip while there were no applications to justify it. The bet paid off only when deep learning arrived. In 2024 he named robotics as the next one.

### Cited Findings
- Huang to Stripe's Patrick Collison (Stripe Sessions, 2024):
  - "I'd rather be a market maker, market creator than a market taker... I don't love thinking about share."
  - A zero-billion-dollar market is something that "hasn't been done before, it's incredibly hard to do. That market is probably zero billion dollars in size." — [Stripe Sessions 2024 transcript](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang)
- The cost of CUDA, in his words:
  - "it cost so much to go after that zero-billion-dollar market it actually crushed the $1 billion market we were enjoying... CUDA added a ton of cost into our chips. But there were no applications... our gross margins get crushed."
  - Nvidia's market value fell to "like a billion dollars or something like that." — [Stripe Sessions 2024](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang)
  - Flag: the market-value figure is Huang's offhand recollection and was not verified against historical data. It may understate the actual low.
- Caltech commencement (2024): "we decided to build something where we are sure there are no customers... Because one of the things you can definitely guarantee is where there are no customers, there are also no competitors." He was referring to robotics. — [Nvidia blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/jensen-huang-caltech-commencement-address/); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/jensen-huang-tells-caltech-grads-to-pursue-zero-billion-dollar-markets-hopes-to-inspire-the-next-big-tech-leaders)
- Nvidia board member Mark Stevens (Sequoia "Crucible Moments" podcast): "The market for AI chips in 2012, 2014, 15. It was a zero billion dollar market... There was no guarantee that AI would ever really emerge." — [Sequoia Capital](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/crucible-moments-nvidia)
- Andrew Ng's recollection of how word spread: "I remember at Stanford when my students were telling me, 'Hey Andrew, there's this thing called CUDA'." — [Sequoia Capital](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/crucible-moments-nvidia)
- Nvidia's own framing (FY2026 10-K): "In 2012, the AlexNet neural network, trained on NVIDIA GPUs, won the ImageNet computer image recognition competition, marking the 'Big Bang' moment of AI." — [Nvidia 10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)
- Thompson's view on Nvidia's critics: "they have earned every dollar they've gotten through 25 years of taking massive risks... Nvidia almost went under trying to build CUDA at a time when no one understood what they were doing." — [Sharp Tech with Ben Thompson (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1abSx642vwI) (podcast transcript, informal)

### Inferences
- This is the single best answer to "why Nvidia and not someone else." Rivals optimized for existing, measurable markets. Intel had its x86 processors, AMD its CPUs plus graphics, 3dfx its retail gamers, Qualcomm its phones. Nvidia repeatedly spent money on a market that did not exist yet, so when AI arrived, Nvidia was the only one already standing there with hardware, software, and trained developers.
- Survivorship bias caveat for the script: the same philosophy produced misses. Tegra and smartphones were an existing market, not a zero-billion one, and Nvidia lost it. Robotics and self-driving cars have so far been slower to pay off. The documentary should present the philosophy as a high-risk bet that happened to hit big, not a guaranteed formula.
