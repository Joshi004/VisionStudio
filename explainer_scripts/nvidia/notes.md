# Nvidia explainer: notes

`script.txt` holds only spoken words, for a word-for-word voice check. About 2,680 words, so roughly 16 to 17 minutes at a brisk narrator pace.

Full research with sources: `reports/stories/Nvidia rise story research.md`. Raw notes: `reports/stories/research_notes/Nvidia rise story research/`.

## Story shape

1. Hook: almost six trillion dollars, a gaming-chip company, a founder suddenly gone. The question: what did Nvidia do that others didn't?
2. Jensen's childhood: the Kentucky school, cleaning toilets, Denny's.
3. Founding at Denny's (1993).
4. First near-death: the NV1 chip fails, and Sega pays anyway after Jensen tells the truth.
5. One shot: the chip simulator, RIVA 128 (1997), "thirty days from going out of business".
6. Beating 3dfx: sell to everyone, ship every six months.
7. The CUDA bet: the professor-and-stadium analogy, the hidden tax on gamers.
8. Second near-death: 2008, and Wall Street hates CUDA. Setup: Nvidia refuses Hinton a free card.
9. Turning point: AlexNet on two gaming cards (2012). Payoff: Jensen hand-delivers a supercomputer to OpenAI (2016).
10. Mellanox: "the whole car".
11. ChatGPT and the trillion-dollar run.
12. Answer: what Nvidia did that others didn't, plus an honest note about luck.
13. Twist: Jensen resigns (November 2026), a record quarter, the stock falls.
14. Close: the full journey, "Can Nvidia still be Nvidia without Jensen?", the lesson.

## Sensitive section (resignation)

- Fact: Nvidia's SEC filing and press release say he resigned on November 11–12, 2026, effective immediately, and Colette Kress is interim CEO.
- The reasons are reported allegations only. The script says he admitted an affair, that news reports raised national security concerns, and that the US Justice Department has reached no conclusion. Keep it that way. Do not name the private individuals, and do not use "spy" on screen as if it were fact.

## Facts that will go stale

## Hedged or dropped on purpose

- The 1996 layoffs are given as "at least half": sources range from half to 65 percent.
- The founding money is "about two million dollars" from investors. The popular "$40,000 of their own money" has no primary source.
- The Intel 2005 buyout idea is said to be "reportedly", since it rests on anonymous sources in the NYT.
- Dropped: Jensen's own "fell to $1.5 billion" memory (market data shows about $4B) and "$500M a year on CUDA" (unverified).
- Cut for length but verified, if you want to add it back: Google's cat-recognition AI used 16,000 processor cores (about $1M of hardware). A Stanford and Nvidia team matched that scale with 12 Nvidia chips for about $20K.
