Most graduation speeches are forgotten by dinner time. A few are still quoted years later. So what makes a line stick?

In 2005, Steve Jobs stood in front of Stanford's graduating class and gave them one warning: "Your time is limited, so don't waste it living someone else's life."

Nine years later, at the University of Texas, Navy admiral William McRaven offered a much smaller first step: "If you want to change the world, start off by making your bed."

One line is about your whole life. The other is about tomorrow morning. Both are short, simple, and easy to act on.
