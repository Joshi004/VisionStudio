# Beat the ASI divers to Dwarka

The next video should be a cinematic investigation of Dwarka, the submerged city tradition links to Krishna. Working title: **"Krishna's Lost City: What Divers Found Under Dwarka, and Why They're Going Back"**. Release it as an 8–10 minute film plus three 60–90 second Shorts, before the Archaeological Survey of India's (ASI) planned December dive and Gita Jayanti on 20 December. It ranks first for three reasons:

- It joins the biggest Indian epic moment in years to a hard archaeology news hook.
- It shows something no camera has ever filmed.
- The creator can depict the city rather than the deity, which keeps religious risk manageable.

Four more topics follow in ranked order:

- **2. Odyssey POV Shorts**, timed to the film's 17 November digital release, for global reach.
- **3. "I'm an AI. Do I have a soul?"**, a cinematic philosopher debate timed to Gita Jayanti and the current AI-consciousness argument.
- **4. "I spent 24 hours in Harappa, 2500 BCE"**, which launches a recurring time-traveller host who becomes the channel's backbone.
- **5. A reverent Ramayana explainer** on what Valmiki says happens after the film ends.

Viewers will know every video is AI. YouTube has auto-labelled photorealistic AI since May 2026, and Indian rules have required prominent labels since February 2026. So the AI has to be the premise: points of view, reconstructions and debates among the dead that cameras could never capture. Each video also needs visible sources and no anachronisms, because AI callouts in comments measurably cut small channels' views. Target global English viewers with India-rooted stories rather than the small English-only Indian audience: third-party estimates put US history ad revenue at roughly 7–15 times India's. Launch on a new channel. Stay away from Sikh Gurus, depictions of the Prophet, "modernised" deities, rankings of religions, Mughal-versus-Maratha or Rajput conflict (including the tempting 500th anniversary of the Battle of Khanwa), and photoreal depictions of modern real people.

Rank reflects overall value, not strict publishing order. The Odyssey window opens on 17 November, so ship those Shorts while the Dwarka film is in production.

**Dwarka wins on timing and scarcity.** The ASI's underwater exploration at Dwarka and Bet Dwarka "could begin by December", subject to approval. It would be the **first proper underwater search since 2007**, and the ASI has done underwater fieldwork only **11 times since 1981**. The 2007 dig yielded terracotta, beads, bangles, copper rings, iron ingots and pottery ([New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Nov/13/asi-set-to-approve-deep-dive-for-exploration-at-dwarka-bet-dwarka)). That gives the creator a natural structure: what was found, how a coastal city drowns, and what the divers are looking for now. It also sets up a follow-up video if the dive produces news.

The surrounding demand is huge. *Ramayana: Part 1* grossed more than **₹500 crore worldwide in its opening weekend**, the highest-grossing Diwali release ever ([DNA India](https://www.dnaindia.com/bollywood/report-ramayana-box-office-collection-ranbir-kapoor-yash-film-beats-tiger-3-to-become-biggest-diwali-blockbuster-3223801)). Gita Jayanti falls on **20 December 2026** ([Drik Panchang](https://www.drikpanchang.com/festivals/gita-jayanti/gita-jayanti-date-time.html?year=2026)).

The format plays to AI's proven reconstruction template. A ruin opens on a "how is this possible?" curiosity gap, the structure is rebuilt on screen, and a golden-hour reveal pays it off ([Alici.ai](https://alici.ai/formulas/media/stonerebornx/10758645-a78c-599a-860c-b650c427e8a5-virupaksha-temple-hampi-reconstruction-ai-video)). The key creative rule is to keep "tradition says" and "divers found" visibly separate on screen. For devotees, Dwarka's historicity is a matter of faith. A "debunk" framing would provoke them, and a "proof" framing would overclaim beyond the evidence. Introduce the channel's recurring host here as an obviously fictional explorer. Do not make the host a fake ASI official, because invented depictions of real institutions edge toward deceptive synthetic content under Indian rules.

**The Odyssey is the global wave to catch this week.** Nolan's film has grossed **$1.804 billion** and goes to digital and 4K on **17 November 2026**. Its critic and audience scores diverge sharply: Metacritic critics give 88, but users give only 5.7 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Odyssey_(2026_film)); [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-odyssey-2026/)). Translator Emily Wilson argues the film misses key Homeric themes ([AP News](https://apnews.com/article/odyssey-christopher-nolan-greek-translators-ea67da1d543d021773824bd7e8de8abe)). The Oscar calendar adds later spikes: shortlists on 15 December, nominations on 21 January and the ceremony on 14 March ([Oscars.org](https://www.oscars.org/99th-oscars-and-academy-key-dates)).

Second-person "POV: you wake up in…" history is the most viral AI-native format on record. Vendor-tracked top posts reached **69.9M** (Black Plague) and **44.4M** (pyramid builder) views ([MakeViral](https://www.makeviral.ai/tools/pov-video-generator)). The format's beats map onto Homer's episodes: an on-screen place and year, one sensory detail, the people, the danger, then a cliffhanger. Keep each Short standalone: Cyclops, Sirens, Circe. Greek myth carries no Indian legal exposure and reaches the highest-paying audience. It ranks second, not first, because the category is crowded and under scrutiny: WIRED ran "Professor Fact Checks Ancient Greece Slop" on 13 November 2026 ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/video/watch/fact-check-ancient-greece)). As a general precaution that the notes do not cover, avoid film footage and any resemblance to the cast. Base everything on Homer.

**The AI-soul debate turns the channel's AI nature into the premise.** The argument is live right now:

- Pope Leo XIV's encyclical *Magnifica Humanitas* insists AIs "do not feel joy or pain" ([Jefferson Public Radio](https://www.ijpr.org/science-technology/2026-11-13/inside-the-box-the-soul-of-the-machine)).
- Anthropic invited 15 Christian leaders to guide Claude's "moral and spiritual development" ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-ai/anthropic-claude-ai-consciousness-religious-leaders-10909349/)).
- The Washington Post ran "Artificial intelligence raises an ancient religious question" on 9 November ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2026/11/09/artificial-intelligence-raises-an-ancient-religious-question/)).

AI philosopher debates already work with fairly simple visuals. Clarified Mind averages about **210K views per video**, and "Machiavelli debates Marcus Aurelius' Stoicism" reached 497K ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/clarifiedmind)). A cinematic, lip-synced version is an open gap. The breakout pattern is a clash that forces a verdict, not a calm conversation. So let an AI character cross-examine Socrates, Descartes and Spinoza as lip-synced characters, and end on "where they finally agree".

Present the Gita's *atman* and the Buddhist *anatta* (no-self) through text, landscape and narration rather than an AI Krishna or Buddha. Never rank the traditions. "Five Religions Decide on Absurd Trolley Problems" drew 433K views abroad, but interfaith ranking is exactly what triggered a 2026 police case (an FIR) in India. Spinoza's 350th death anniversary brings conferences in Heidelberg (February) and Bologna (March 2027) ([PhilEvents](https://philevents.org/event/show/154493); [University of Bologna](https://site.unibo.it/sivenatura/it/eventi/spinoza-350-nature-power-freedom)), which give a ready sequel.

**Harappa is the evergreen backbone, not the next spike.** The AI "time-travel vlogger" is 2026's breakout global format. Chloe VS History reached **15M YouTube views**, with a 4M-view Titanic episode, because "people get attached to a particular character" ([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/may/26/we-can-stitch-together-our-past-the-ai-generated-time-travellers-vlogging-from-history)). Her Tudor London 1536 vlog hit **3.0M** in 12 minutes, yet no Indian setting appears among the top AI historical films tracked by glu.tv ([glu.tv](https://glu.tv/best-ai-historical-films/)).

That absence suggests an English-language gap, but it is an inference, not a measured gap. Oxford historian Adam Smith urges creators toward lesser-known events "rather than the really predictable things, like Vesuvius or the plague" ([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/may/26/we-can-stitch-together-our-past-the-ai-generated-time-travellers-vlogging-from-history)). The ASI's Rakhigarhi burials are now under ancient-DNA study ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2276489&lang=1&reg=3)), so results could land at any time. Stick to daily life (drains, beads, trade) and stay out of ancestry and migration claims. The same host should carry later episodes in Pataliputra, a Chola port and the Vijayanagara market, each as a self-contained story.

**The Ramayana has the most raw Indian demand but the strongest headwind.** "ramayana explained" was among YouTube's fastest-rising religion topics in the four weeks to 9 November ([TrendReport](https://trendreport.com/reports/religion-and-spirituality-youtube-trends-week-2026-11-09/)). Part 1 covers only the first three books (kandas) of Valmiki's text. Part 2 is reportedly pushed to Diwali 2028 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana:_Part_1); [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/bollywood/ramayana-part-1-box-office-collection-day-4-live-updates-ranbir-kapoor-film-earns-rs-500-crore-10955435/)), which leaves a long tail of "what happens next" interest. Release dates also conflict across sources (5–8 November depending on market), and an aggregator still lists Part 2 for 2027.

Two things push this topic to fifth. First, Hindi incumbents dominate the space: Tilak alone has 40.7M subscribers ([IndiTubeDb](https://www.inditubedb.com/guides/best-youtube-channels-ramayana-ramayan-indian-mythology-2026)). Second, there is the "AI disrespects sacred stories" reaction. JioHotstar's AI Mahabharat drew a top trailer comment of "We Don't Want Fully AI Based Films…Especially Mahabharat" ([C21Media](https://www.c21media.net/news/jiohotstars-ai-made-indian-mythological-microdrama-receives-mixed-response-online/)). ## AI earns its keep only where cameras never went

The creator has to plan for an audience that knows it is watching AI. A 2026 SSRN study found that an AI mention appearing in a video's top comments **cuts views by about 10%, with the effect concentrated in smaller channels**. In experiments, an AI label cut click selection by 23%, and an AI callout during viewing cut watch time by 14% ([Fong, Lam & Natan, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6885980)). Disclosure itself is not the killer. A 617-person experiment found labels reduce perceived authenticity but not trust ([Frontiers in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1975796)), and hiding AI "evoked feelings of deception" ([Umeå University](https://umu.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:2069063/FULLTEXT01.pdf)).

Hiding is impossible anyway. Since May 2026 YouTube auto-applies a prominent AI label under the player and as an overlay on Shorts ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/)). So the strategy is to make AI the reason to watch, so that "it's AI" stops working as a gotcha in the comments. POV viewers wrote "The only right way to use ai" ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91280871/this-ai-trend-lets-tiktok-users-relive-historys-best-and-worst-moments)).

AI's unique value in this zone is **putting the viewer or a character inside a scene that can never be filmed**. That means a drowned city, Odysseus's deck, Socrates cross-examining a machine, or a Harappan street at dawn. Stock footage cannot do this, and a talking head can only describe it. The creator's pipeline is unusually well matched. Consistent faces and environments are what a recurring host needs, and lip-synced dialogue is what turns a philosopher debate from narrated slides into drama. Shots of 2–10 seconds fit the "new beat every 2–3 seconds" rhythm Shorts reward ([Prepublish](https://prepublish.ai/guides/youtube-shorts-retention)).

What turns AI into "slop" is well documented:

- **Anachronisms**, such as sunglasses in Rome ([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/may/26/we-can-stitch-together-our-past-the-ai-generated-time-travellers-vlogging-from-history)) and a modern bedside table in Hastinapur ([India Today](https://www.indiatoday.in/entertainment/ott/story/ai-mahabharat-trends-as-viewers-spot-modern-bedside-table-joke-chargers-missing-2812606-2025-11-03)).
- **The "black box" problem** of no sources, historians' most common complaint ([BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cy87076pdw3o)).
- **Generic regional looks.** Early AI Hampi images were faulted for resembling north Indian temples ([New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/states/karnataka/2023/Jul/23/ai-images-of-hampis-past-now-a-big-hit-2597603.html)).

The fixes are concrete: put sources on screen and in descriptions, use period- and region-correct architecture and costume, and give the host human-written humour and personality. The Elizabeth I creator, whose first video got 6M views in a week, warns that without human detail "people are going to scroll past it" ([Sharjah24](https://sharjah24.ae/en/Articles/2026/11/13/AI-creator-shares-storytelling-tips-at-SIBF-2026)). Character attachment also buys tolerance: Chloe's fans defended her against historians' fact-checks ([Olive Higham](https://olivehigham.substack.com/p/genai-is-producing-historical-slop)).

Retention is won in the first scene. In one 322-Short dataset, the top quarter of videos kept **95% of viewers through scene one, against 44%** for weaker tiers ([Blue Fermion](https://bluefermion.com/a/n8n-youtube)). Three hook types work for history: the correction, the absurd detail and the consequence chain ([MaqtAi](https://maqtai.com/blog/history-shorts-channel-people-finish/)). Dramatic irony is another cheap, strong open loop: the viewer knows the city drowns, the character does not.

Structure the channel as an anthology, not a long serial. Serial follow-ups can outperform one-offs (median 250K vs 97K views on one AI channel), but episodes decay by 90% or more by part 5 ([BlewUp](https://blewup.co/cases/nobody-gets-in/)). YouTube's "inauthentic content" rules also bar monetization of "characters… put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)). Each episode needs its own story shape, not just a new backdrop.

## Global viewers pay 7–15 times more, so tell Indian stories to the world

The Indian English-only audience for this genre is thin:

- Indian Gen Z prefers local languages for video (56%) ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/marketing-news/new-google-and-kantar-report-reveals-how-indias-youth-engage-with-news-146268.html)).
- Mythology's giant channels are Hindi ([IndiTubeDb](https://www.inditubedb.com/guides/best-youtube-channels-ramayana-ramayan-indian-mythology-2026)).
- Small English Indian-history Shorts channels log as few as **87–460 views per Short** ([Chronicles Of India](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCjQzLp3bnVMpwxvQ3IEAcyw)).

Money points the same way. Third-party estimates put long-form history RPM (revenue per 1,000 views) at **$7–14 for US viewers versus $0.5–1.2 for Indian viewers** ([Vaatik](https://vaatik.com/blog/youtube-rpm-by-country-tier-list)). Shorts pay only a few cents per thousand views anywhere ([YTMoneyCalculator](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/average-youtube-rpm/)). RPM follows where the viewers are, not where the creator lives ([TubeMind](https://tubemind.io/youtube-rpm-in-india)). These are vendor models, not verified YouTube Studio data, but they agree on the direction. The creator's current niche, Indian viewers watching Shorts, sits in the lowest-paying combination.

The recommendation is **India-rooted, world-facing**: write every script for a curious viewer who has never heard of Dwarka or Harappa. Urban Indian English speakers and the diaspora will still watch, and global viewers get novelty they cannot find from Western AI channels. Deep English-language Indian history can find a large audience. Odd Compass's first Chola video drew about 960K views ([Homegrown](https://homegrown.co.in/homegrown-creators/odd-compass-captivating-animated-shorts-bring-indian-history-to-life-in-vivid-detail)). No data measures the diaspora's share of English mythology viewing, so the first three videos should be treated as an experiment. Mix one global-native topic (the Odyssey) with India-rooted ones and compare where views come from. Later, YouTube's multi-language audio tracks could open Hindi audiences without a second channel: 77% of Indian 14–24-year-olds watched translated content last year ([Google India](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/platforms/2025s-biggest-trends-on-youtube-that-gave-india-a-new-shared-vernacular/)). That would need the currently English-only pipeline to be extended.

**Put this on a new channel.** YouTube judges monetization eligibility at the channel level, looking at its main theme, most-viewed and newest videos, and metadata ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)). Mixing Indian finance with mythology muddies that signal and the audience. There is also a risk to the finance channel itself. YouTube's July 2026 clarification names "AI-generated podcast hosts offering financial guidance" as non-monetizable, and channels with "too much" of such content lose monetization ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)). If the finance characters come across as advisers, the creator should keep that exposure on its own channel and reframe those videos as clearly fictional stories rather than advice.

The argument for reusing the finance channel is its existing subscribers. Those subscribers came for finance, though, and in one dataset 95% of winning Shorts' first-week views came from the Shorts feed rather than from subscribers ([Blue Fermion](https://bluefermion.com/a/n8n-youtube)). The cost of a new channel is real: it has to re-qualify for monetization from zero.

## The red lines: Gurus, the Prophet, Mughal-era fault lines and modernised deities

This section is not legal advice; high-risk topics need an Indian media lawyer.

Indian criminal law already reaches online video:

- **Section 299 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)**, the old IPC 295A, punishes deliberately outraging religious feelings "through electronic means" with **up to three years**. The offence is cognizable and non-bailable ([Veritect](https://veritect.ai/old-law-new-law/hurting-religious-feelings-bns-298-302-vs-ipc-295-298)).
- **BNS Section 196** covers promoting enmity between groups.

The amended IT Rules, in force since **20 February 2026**, require AI video that looks real to carry a prominent label and provenance metadata. They also cut government-ordered takedowns to **three hours** ([MeitY FAQ](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/065b6deb585441b5ccdf8be42502a49c.pdf)). The real exposure for a small creator is the first information report (FIR) itself, not a conviction. Police registered a case on their own initiative against YouTuber Sameer MD over an AI-heavy video ([The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/karnataka/sowjanya-murder-case-video-youtuber-sameer-md-booked-for-offending-religious-sentiments)). In Ujjain in September 2026, AI videos falsely showing a mosque being bulldozed led to cases against creators and arrests ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/madhya-pradesh/over-20-arrested-police-search-for-content-creators-of-ai-generated-videos-that-led-to-ujjain-violence/article71524881.ece)).

| Deities in casual, modern or invented scenes | Police complaint over AI gods in sunglasses ([News18](https://www.news18.com/cricket/police-complaint-filed-by-karni-sena-against-rinku-singh-over-ai-video-of-hindu-deities-ws-l-9842562.html)); Jagannath temple complaint over invented rituals ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/odisha/ai-videos-of-lord-jagannath-surface-police-complaint-filed/article70527952.ece)) | Faithful, cited, reverent; depict places rather than gods where possible |

Two tempting anniversaries deserve an explicit pass for now:

- **Khanwa 500** is the strongest round-number Indian anniversary in the window. Rana Sanga's legacy, however, was already a political flashpoint in 2025 ([The Perfect Voice](https://www.theperfectvoice.in/history/khanwa-1527--the-clash-that-could-have-stopped-the-mughals/article-11176)).
- **The Mahad Satyagraha centenary** (20 March 2027) and the Manusmriti-burning centenary (from 25 December 2026) are being commemorated across parties ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/mumbai/chavdar-lake-satyagraha-centenary-mahad-ambedkar-legacy-politics-2026-10594314/)). They would require a lip-synced AI Ambedkar, a photographed modern figure, inside a caste debate. That is the worst combination of likeness risk and communal risk.

Platform policy adds three practical constraints:

- **Context must sit inside the video.** YouTube's hate-speech policy accepts educational context only if it "appear[s] in the images or audio of the video itself", not in the title or description ([YouTube Hate speech policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801939?hl=en)).
- **Disclose AI on every upload.** Turn on the "AI use" toggle, add an opening on-screen card reading "AI-generated dramatised reconstruction", and keep provenance metadata intact.
- **Keep violence implied.** Dramatised violence with visible aftermath gets limited ads ([YouTube advertiser guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)).

Operationally, cite the version of the text used on screen (for example, "Valmiki, Aranya Kanda"). Get a cultural reader for any Indian-religion episode, watch comments closely for the first 48 hours, and be ready to unlist quickly if a community body objects. Rathee's fast takedown defused his case ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/chandigarh/sgpc-demands-removal-of-ai-video-featuring-guru-gobind-singh-by-youtuber-dhruv-rathee/articleshow/121276014.cms)). Keep research notes as evidence of good faith, since Section 299 turns on "deliberate and malicious" intent.

## Conclusion

The mandatory AI label looks like a handicap, but it sorts topics cleanly. Subjects where a real camera could have existed invite unfavourable comparison with human work. Subjects where no camera ever could, such as a drowned city, Homer's sea or a machine questioning Socrates, make the label a promise rather than a confession. That same filter happens to steer the creator away from India's most dangerous terrain. The topics that most need photoreal "reconstructions" of real people (Mughal-era conflict, modern reformers) are the ones with the heaviest legal and communal exposure. The safest ground is also where AI adds the most: deep antiquity, lost places and ideas.

The scarce input is no longer visuals but research and script. YouTube's monetization rules, historians' complaints and the evidence on viewer distrust all reward visible sourcing, a distinct story shape for each episode and a host with real personality. Two things remain untested. Demand for English-language Indian topics is inferred from a gap, not measured, and there is no retention data for long AI videos built on dialogue. Treat Dwarka, the Odyssey Shorts and the AI-soul debate as a three-video experiment. Judge them on how many viewers survive scene one, where the views come from geographically, and how often "AI" appears in the top comments before committing the channel to the Harappa-style host anthology.
