## 1. Thinking Machines Lab: founding, people, money, products, policy, releases, reception, news

### Takeaway
Thinking Machines Lab (TML) is a San Francisco public benefit corporation launched on 18 February 2025 by former OpenAI CTO Mira Murati, who is its founder and CEO (confirmed). It raised a $2B seed at a $12B valuation in mid-2025, was reported in November 2026 to be closing a $5B round at a $40B pre-money valuation (not confirmed by the company as of 19 Nov 2026), and went from a fine-tuning service (Tinker, Oct 2025) to shipping four Apache 2.0 open-weights models in 2026, while losing three of its six co-founders.

**"A Safe Path to Open Weights" policy (31 Jul 2026)**
- Abstract: "Safe open-weight models are public goods, as they put AI development and safety work in many hands and make training choices inspectable. Open models also carry real misuse risks, and release is irreversible... By iteratively choosing the most open option the evidence supports while building up the ecosystem's resilience, we can move toward openness safely." — [TML, 31 Jul 2026](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/a-safe-path-to-open-weights/)
- "we can carefully stage releases with options such as monitored API access, hosted fine-tuning, and vetting defenders." and "each stage should widen access only when the evidence supports it." — [TML, 31 Jul 2026](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/a-safe-path-to-open-weights/)
- On Inkling: "we concluded that releasing Inkling was not likely to add material risk beyond what existing open-weight models already pose." Tested via internal evals, "external testing by four independent organizations, and a fine-tuning study to elicit worst-case capabilities." — [TML, 31 Jul 2026](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/a-safe-path-to-open-weights/)
- Applied to Inkling 2 Small (5 Nov 2026): "Inkling 2 Small will initially be available through Tinker, with closed weights, following the approach we outlined in a safe path to open weights. We intend to make the full weights available under an Apache 2.0 license next week after the completion of external safety testing and red-teaming." — [TML, 5 Nov 2026](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-small/)

## 2. Alibaba Qwen: position in open models, people, licences, 2026 cadence

### Takeaway
Qwen is the most-downloaded and most-built-upon open-weights family on Hugging Face in 2026 (roughly half of tracked downloads and ~150k–230k derivative repos, depending on who counts and when), but that is a downloads lead, not a usage lead on paid API traffic. The team lost its technical lead Lin Junyang in March 2026 and got a new head, Liu Dayiheng, in September. Alibaba ships small models under Apache 2.0, put its 2.4T flagship out in August under a custom revenue-gated licence, and kept the November flagship Qwen4-Max closed.

### Cited Findings

**Downloads, derivatives, share (dated)**
- ATOM Report (arXiv, data to March 2026): "Qwen surpassed Llama in cumulative downloads in September 2025 (325.4M vs. 323.7M) and by March 2026 reached 942.1M, thus nearly doubling the downloads of the Llama models (476.0M)." "Qwen's share of new fine-tunes and adaptations rose from 1% in January 2024 to 69% by February 2026". — [ATOM Report, arXiv 2604.07190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07190)

"Even among the 28,531 GGUF conversions of Qwen models on the Hub, Qwen published only 54." — [Hugging Face blog, Summer 2026](https://huggingface.co/blog/state-of-open-models-summer-2026) (exact post date not captured; data covers first seven months of 2026).
- Hugging Face, Spring 2026: Qwen family "constituting more ... ,000 derivative models. ## 4. Looped / recurrent-depth transformers: what, history, trade-offs, monitorability, "first?", analogies

### Takeaway
A looped transformer re-runs some of its own layers several times for each token, so it "thinks" more steps internally without storing more weights. The idea dates to Universal Transformers (2018) and was revived in 2025 by Geiping et al.'s recurrent-depth model (Huginn) and ByteDance's Ouro. Looping boosts multi-step reasoning but not memorised facts, and it raises a monitoring concern because the extra reasoning isn't written down. Inkling 2 Mini is not the first released looped model, but TML itself only claims "some of the first", and it is plausibly the largest and most capable looped model shipped for general use so far.

### Cited Findings

**What looping is (plain language from sources)**
- TML: "Inkling 2 Mini is a looped transformer, running the middle layers of the model three times for every token. This allows the model to apply more reasoning and reach the right answer with fewer tokens". — [TML, 20 Nov 2026](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
- TML design: "Inkling 2 Mini has 24 layers, divided into four blocks of six layers each. The two middle blocks run three times, while the first and last blocks each run once." Motivation: "the middle layers of the model carry more abstract reasoning, while the first and last few layers devote more space to translating between embeddings and internal representations." — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
- Model card: "The middle 12 layers run three times with shared weights, giving 48 layer applications per token from 24 layers of weights." "each extra pass through the looped layers adds about 1.0B effective active parameters... there are 5.4B effective active parameters per token." — [TML model card](https://thinkingmachines.ai/model-card/inkling-2-mini/)
- Hugging Face blog: "the model acts as if it had 48 (6+ 3*12 + 6) logical layers with just 24 physical ones." "Each pass therefore refines what the previous one computed." — [Hugging Face blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/inkling2mini)
- Sebastian Raschka: "The main idea in a looped transformer is that it applies the same layers (i.e., the transformer block) several times. The looped transformer method is often also called recurrent depth or looped depth sharing." "the looped depth, in a sense, changes the trade-off between parameter count and computation." — [Raschka, LLM Architecture Gallery](https://sebastianraschka.com/llm-architecture-gallery/looped-depth-sharing/)

**Research history**
- Universal Transformers (Dehghani et al., 2018, arXiv 1807.03819): "the UT does not recur over positions in the sequence, but over consecutive revisions of the vector representations of each position (i.e., over 'depth')." "UTs can have variable depth (number of per-symbol processing steps)." — [arXiv 1807.03819](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819). TML cites it directly as the basis of its design. — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
- Geiping et al., "Scaling up Test-Time Compute with Latent Reasoning: A Recurrent Depth Approach" (arXiv 2502.05171, Feb 2025): "Our model works by iterating a recurrent block, thereby unrolling to arbitrary depth at test-time. This stands in contrast to mainstream reasoning models that scale up compute by producing more tokens." "We scale a proof-of-concept model to 3.5 billion parameters and 800 billion tokens... up to a computation load equivalent to 50 billion parameters." Released model: huggingface.co/tomg-group-umd/huginn-0125. — [arXiv 2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171)
- Geiping et al. on the idea's lineage: "this idea is foundational to machine learning and has been (re-)discovered in every decade, for example as recurrent neural networks, diffusion models, and as universal or looped transformers." Architecture: prelude, recurrent core, coda; "by looping the core we can put an indefinite amount of verses in our song." — [arXiv 2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171)
- Ouro (ByteDance et al., arXiv 2510.25741, 29 Oct 2025; authors include Yoshua Bengio): "named after the recursive Ouroboros, a family of pre-trained Looped Language Models (LoopLM)"; "Ouro 1.4B and 2.6B models enjoy superior performance that match the results of up to 12B SOTA LLMs"; "this advantage stems not from increased knowledge capacity, but from superior knowledge manipulation capabilities." — [arXiv 2510.25741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741). Released 29 Oct 2025 as "Four Apache-2.0 checkpoints". — [Ouro project page](https://ouro-llm.github.io/). HF cards state: "This model is intended for research purposes only." — [ByteDance/Ouro-1.4B](https://huggingface.co/ByteDance/Ouro-1.4B)
- Raschka lists further lineage: Mixture-of-Recursions (2025, token-level routing of recursion depth), Ouro (Oct 2025), and "Nanbeige 4.2 (2026), [which] simplifies the looped transformer idea by repeating the same 22-layer stack". — [Raschka](https://sebastianraschka.com/llm-architecture-gallery/looped-depth-sharing/). HF blog: "Architectures such as Nanbeige have explored looping before." — [Hugging Face blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/inkling2mini)
- TML also uses "attention residuals" from "Attention Residuals, Kimi Team: Guangyu Chen et al., 2026" to pass a "message" between loops. — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)

**Compute vs memory trade-off**
- TML: 26.4B total, 3.4B active, "and 5.4B weight activations per token to account for looping"; "~20GB" total memory footprint. — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
- Training efficiency: "The non-looping model cost 7.5% less compute to train, but the looping version was over 50% more compute-efficient: the non-looping scaling law predicts it would take over 1.5× the looping version's training compute to match its held-out loss." — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
- Cost side: Raschka: "the activation memory and KV-cache memory also grow with the number of passes, depending on the implementation." — [Raschka](https://sebastianraschka.com/llm-architecture-gallery/looped-depth-sharing/). vLLM: each pass has its own KV cache, so 12 core layers produce 48 caches (per search summary of vLLM blog; verify on page). — [vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-11-20-inkling2-mini). TML's speed chart footnote: decoding speed "including repeated reads across loops." — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
- Ouro: "LoopLM scales by deepening its internal computational graph rather than extending the output sequence, avoiding context-length bloat." — [arXiv 2510.25741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741)

**Reasoning yes, stored knowledge no**
- TML: "While looping improves the model's reasoning, it doesn't enable its weights to store more facts—knowledge is mostly a function of total parameter count." "knowledge-intensive benchmarks doesn't improve with more passes, such as AA-Omniscience, SimpleQA Verified, SimpleVQA, and WorldVQA." [sic] — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
- TML: "more loop passes primarily benefit multi-hop reasoning rather than recall... Closed-book factual QA is nearly invariant to loop passes". — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
- TML workaround: "Inkling 2 Mini overcomes the knowledge gap by efficiently using search and tools." — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
- Independent support: Ouro's advantage "stems not from increased knowledge capacity, but from superior knowledge manipulation capabilities." — [arXiv 2510.25741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741)

**Monitorability concern**
- TML: "Looping allows the model to reason longer without writing its reasoning down, which matters for monitoring". "By running the same layers multiple times, looped models increase their serial depth without adding more parameters, meaning that they do more computation per token internally before verbalizing their thought processes via CoT. Reasoning that never appears in the CoT can't be caught by a CoT monitor, which has led to concerns that looped transformers could be harder to oversee." — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
- TML's findings (preliminary, their own tests): "looping increases what these models can do without a verbalized chain of thought, but at this scale we do not see a meaningful reduction in the monitorability of their reasoning." No-CoT time horizon "rises from 16 seconds at one pass to 37 seconds at three". "Reasoning at 5 passes was less than half as long as at 1 pass (1,661 vs. 3,703 tokens on average), but no less legible". — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
- TML caveat: a model "trained with RL at a higher pass count... might compress its reasoning more aggressively." — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
- TML released a research variant "TML-d24-varloop-research" (1–5 passes trained) and is working with NDIF and Goodfire on interpretability. — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
- Geiping et al. frame the same property positively: the approach "can capture types of reasoning that are not easily represented in words." — [arXiv 2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171)

**Is Inkling 2 Mini "one of the first released looped production models"?**
- TML's exact claim: "We're excited to offer some of the first models with looped architecture available to researchers." — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
- Earlier released looped models exist: Huginn-0125 (3.5B, Feb 2025) — [arXiv 2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171); Ouro 1.4B/2.6B (Apache 2.0, 29 Oct 2025, "research purposes only") — [Ouro](https://ouro-llm.github.io/); Nanbeige 4.2 (2026) — [Raschka](https://sebastianraschka.com/llm-architecture-gallery/looped-depth-sharing/).
- Ecosystem readiness: vLLM headline "Inkling 2 Mini on vLLM: Day-0 Support for a Looped Model"; Ouro got native vLLM support in v0.11.1 (18 Nov 2025). — [vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-11-20-inkling2-mini); [Ouro](https://ouro-llm.github.io/)

**Candidate everyday analogies (my assessment; not sourced claims)**
Source-grounded anchors: UT's "consecutive revisions" of each token's representation; HF's "Each pass therefore refines what the previous one computed"; Geiping's "put an indefinite amount of verses in our song"; Ouro's Ouroboros (snake eating its tail).
1. **Rereading and redrafting an essay** (same writer, same brain, three drafts before handing it in). *Accurate*: same weights reused, each pass refines the previous one (matches UT "revisions" and HF "refines"). Best overall pick. Caveat: the drafts happen silently, which conveniently also illustrates the monitorability point.
2. **Doing mental arithmetic in your head vs on paper.** *Accurate for the monitorability point*: looping lets more steps happen "in the head" before anything is written; CoT is the paper. Pair with #1.
3. **A small kitchen with one excellent chef who passes the dish through the same station three times** vs a big kitchen with more stations. *Mostly accurate* for compute-vs-memory (fewer stations = less space/weights, more time per dish). Weakness: suggests strictly sequential repeats of identical work; in reality later passes get a "message" from earlier passes.
4. **Thinking harder doesn't teach you new facts** (you can mull over a maths problem for an hour and solve it, but no amount of thinking will tell you the capital of a country you never learned). *Accurate* for "reasoning yes, knowledge no" (TML: knowledge "mostly a function of total parameter count"). Very usable on screen.
5. **A song with a repeated chorus/verses** (Geiping's own metaphor). *Partly accurate*: captures reuse of the same block; less intuitive about why repeats help.
6. **A washing machine rinse cycle run three times.** *Weak*: implies each pass does the same thing to a passive object; misses the refining/building-on-previous aspect. Avoid.
7. **Ouroboros snake.** *Visual only*; good for b-roll, not for explanation.
