# Nvidia Bet Early, Survived Everything, Then Lost Jensen

Nvidia became a company that earned **$115.8 billion in revenue and $65.3 billion in profit in a single quarter** (ended October 25, 2026) because, over three decades, it repeatedly paid for markets before they existed and survived long enough for one of those bets to land ([Nvidia Q3 FY27](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)). The story has three near-deaths: in 1996 a wrong-headed first chip left it weeks from missing payroll; in 2008 its costly CUDA software bet, a defective-chip charge and the financial crisis cut its value by roughly three-quarters; and in November 2026 its only CEO, co-founder Jensen Huang, resigned. What separated Nvidia from richer rivals was not one invention but a pattern: it sold chips to everyone instead of competing with its customers, shipped on a relentless clock, put CUDA on every gaming card for a decade while almost nobody used it, and then turned itself from a chip seller into a builder of whole AI data centers. When two University of Toronto researchers trained AlexNet on two $500 gaming cards in 2012, the entire AI field was already standing on Nvidia's software, and when ChatGPT arrived a decade later, Nvidia was the only company with the chips, software, networking and developers ready. As of November 20, 2026, the business is at a record, but the stock is down about 41% from its October 6 peak of roughly $5.8 trillion, Nvidia is the fourth most valuable US company, custom chips from Google and Amazon are eroding its share, and the board is investigating the circumstances of Huang's exit. For a documentary, the arc is now complete and tragic in the classical sense: the founder's paranoia built the company, and his departure is the biggest open question about its future.

## Chapter 1: A nine-year-old cleaned toilets at a Kentucky reform school (1963–1993)

Jen-Hsun Huang was born in Taipei on **February 17, 1963**, the son of a refinery chemical engineer and a schoolteacher, and spent part of his childhood in Thailand ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang)). Around **1973**, amid social unrest, his parents sent him and his older brother to the United States alone; in Huang's words, "it was probably safer for the kids to go to the United States and then for them to follow" ([NPR](https://thin.npr.org/147162496)). An uncle enrolled the boys at **Oneida Baptist Institute** in Kentucky, believing it was a prestigious boarding school. It was, as a later school president told NPR, regarded locally as "a religious reform school" that took expelled children ([NPR](https://thin.npr.org/147162496); [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)). Every student had a job, and nine-year-old Jensen's was cleaning the bathrooms ([NPR](https://thin.npr.org/147162496)).

The most vivid Oneida scenes come in two grades of evidence. A childhood friend, Ben Bays, independently describes the bullying: Huang was "an undersized Asian immigrant with long hair and heavily accented English... He was a perfect target," and local boys shook a rickety swinging footbridge to try to throw him off, yet "he just shook it off" and by year's end was leading those same kids on adventures ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)). The story of a knife-scarred 17-year-old roommate whom Jensen taught to read, in exchange for bench-press lessons and "a hundred pushups every night," rests on Huang's own retelling, and its details have shifted between versions ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution); [Wired, 2002](https://www.wired.com/2002/07/nvidia/)). Huang tells Oneida warmly ("I loved the time I was there"), while others describe real hardship, and that gap between suffering and fondness is itself a storytelling beat ([NPR](https://thin.npr.org/147162496)). In 2019 he gave $2 million for a building at the school ([Kentucky Lantern](https://kentuckylantern.com/2026/11/09/what-do-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-and-clay-countys-oneida-baptist-institute-have-in-common/)).

Reunited with his parents in Oregon, he skipped two grades, graduated high school at 16 and became a nationally ranked table-tennis player ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)). His first job, at 15, was at **Denny's**: dishwasher, then busboy, then waiter ([Wikipedia, citing Fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang)). He earned an electrical engineering degree at **Oregon State in 1984**, where he met his wife Lori as a lab partner ("I asked her if she wanted to see my homework"), designed chips at AMD and then LSI Logic, and earned a Stanford master's at night in 1992 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang); [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-leather-jacket-wife-daughter-style-2023-10)). At LSI he worked with two Sun Microsystems engineers, **Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem**, on Sun's graphics hardware, which set up the founding.

**On-screen beat:** the Denny's loop is fully documented. He washed dishes at Denny's, founded Nvidia in a Denny's booth, and in 2023 Denny's put up a plaque honoring that founding ([NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-dennys-trillion/)).

## Chapter 2: Three engineers bet on the wrong shape and nearly died (1993–1996)

Priem and Malachowsky wanted to leave Sun after leadership shelved Priem's next-generation graphics design, and they recruited Huang, who was reluctant to leave LSI until he was convinced the 3D video-game market was big enough ([Joey Zhou notes on *The Nvidia Way*](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04)). The three planned the company in a Denny's in San Jose; Huang's explanation was simple: "It had all the coffee you could drink and no one could kick you out" ([El País](https://english.elpais.com/economy-and-business/2024-03-04/inside-nvidias-museum-of-the-future.html)). Nvidia was **incorporated in April 1993** ([Nvidia Investor FAQ](https://investor.nvidia.com/investor-resources/faqs/default.aspx)) with a goal Huang later described as "transforming the PC into an accelerated PC," because Windows then drew everything in software ([Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)). The name riffs on the Latin *invidia*, envy; Priem wanted rivals "green with envy" ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)).

The money came through a phone call. LSI's CEO Wilf Corrigan "called up Don Valentine and told Don, invest in this kid," Huang recalls, adding, "I didn't know how to write a business plan" ([Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)). Sequoia and Sutter Hill partners joined the board in **June 1993** ([IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt)); Tae Kim's book puts the first round at about $2 million and reports Valentine's warning: "If you lose my money, I will kill you" ([Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04)).

The first product, **NV1 (May 1995)**, was an all-in-one graphics, video and audio chip that drew 3D scenes with curved surfaces rather than triangles. Nvidia's own prospectus admits it was built "in the absence of industry standards with the goal of establishing the Company's proprietary NV technology as a 3D graphics standard" ([IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt)). Then Microsoft's DirectX standardized on triangles. Huang: "the API called DirectX is the architecture that everybody else uses except us... the rest of the industry, now some 50 companies, are all over us" ([Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/flashback-how-the-kindness-of-sega-saved-nvidia-from-going-under)). NV1 brought in just **$1.18 million in 1995 at a −40% gross margin**, meaning Nvidia lost money on every chip it sold ([IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt)).

Worse, Nvidia was building the same doomed architecture into a chip for Sega's next console. Huang faced a choice he summarized as: "we would finish the project and die, or not finish the project and die right away" ([Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/flashback-how-the-kindness-of-sega-saved-nvidia-from-going-under)). He went to Sega's Shoichiro Irimajiri, admitted Nvidia could not deliver, and asked to be released from the contract while still being paid. Sega approved about **$5 million**: "It was all the money that we had," Huang says ([Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/flashback-how-the-kindness-of-sega-saved-nvidia-from-going-under)). Irimajiri's own 2026 account frames it as converting leftover development funds into Nvidia shares as "a parting gift": "I came to like Jensen Huang's character, and bet everything on him" ([Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/09/12/companies/sega-nvidia-investment/)). Sega itself told Inc. it approved $5 million "around 1995 or 1996" but no longer has records showing whether it was a contract payment or equity ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/georgia-fearn/jensen-huang-sega-shoichiro-irimajiri-nvidia-technology-failed-5-million/91376429)). Huang then cut staff deeply; Stephen Witt puts it at "from more than 100 to a skeleton roster of 35 engineers," while a consultant who arrived in 1996, David Kirk, demanded a hand-delivered paper check every week because he doubted the company would last ([Inc. excerpt of *The Thinking Machine*](https://www.inc.com/stephen-witt/how-jensen-huang-kicked-off-a-mad-dash-to-save-nvidia-in-its-early-years/91173298)).

**On-screen beat:** an honest CEO walks into a client's office and says "if we finish this for you, we die." The client pays anyway. In September 2026 the two men, Irimajiri then 86, reunited at a Sega arcade in Tokyo ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/nvidias-jensen-huang-reunites-with-sega-exec-who-saved-the-company-with-5m-investment/)).

## Chapter 3: One shot at a perfect chip turned 1997 into a resurrection (1997–1999)

With months of cash left, Nvidia designed **RIVA 128** to fully support Microsoft's Direct3D, aiming to build "the biggest chip that anybody had ever imagined building" at a price competitors would not dare ([Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)). There was no money for the normal process of making test chips, fixing bugs and re-making them, so Huang bought a hardware emulator from IKOS, a company he says was shutting down that very day ([Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)), reportedly for about $1 million ([Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04)). The machine was so big that staff "shoved aside the Ping-Pong table to make space for it in the break room" ([Inc. excerpt](https://www.inc.com/stephen-witt/how-jensen-huang-kicked-off-a-mad-dash-to-save-nvidia-in-its-early-years/91173298)). Simulating the chip was so slow that Huang estimates "an hour per frame" for Windows to draw on screen ([Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)). The logic was existential: "if we only had six months and you get to tape out just one time, then obviously you're going to tape out a perfect chip" ([Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)). It was not quite perfect, since it supported only 8 of 32 DirectX blend modes, but it worked.

RIVA 128 began shipping in **August 1997**, manufactured by SGS-Thomson ([IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt)). By January 12, 1998, Nvidia had shipped **over one million** units, and analyst Jon Peddie called it "the surprise of 1997," estimating a 21% share of its segment "in only 4 months of shipments" ([Nvidia press release, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20110326212747/http:/www.nvidia.com/object/IO_20020114_4455.html)). Revenue jumped from **$3.9 million (1996) to $29.1 million (1997)**, with the first profitable quarter ending December 31, 1997 ([IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt)). The New Yorker reports that when the chip shipped, Nvidia had only one month of payroll left, and that "for years to come he opened staff presentations with the words 'Our company is thirty days from going out of business'" ([Business Insider, quoting The New Yorker](https://markets.businessinsider.com/news/stocks/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo-profile-ai-stock-market-nvda-price-2023-11)). Huang still called 1997 "probably Nvidia's best moment... running out of time, we're running out of money, and for a lot of employees, running out of hope" ([Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)).

Nvidia went public on **January 22, 1999** at $12 a share, raising $42 million ([SEC 424B4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt)), and closed its first day at $19.6875, a market value of about **$626 million** ([WSJ, Jan 25, 1999](https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB917224371262043000)). On **August 31, 1999**, it unveiled **GeForce 256** as "the world's first graphics processing unit (GPU)," with about 23 million transistors ([GeForce 256 press release](https://mmgik.org/images/almanach/agp/przypisy/NVIDIA%20LAUNCHES%20THE%20WORLDS%20FIRST%20GRAPHICS%20PROCESSING%20UNIT%20GEFORCE%20256.pdf)). The innovation was moving the geometry math (where each corner of each shape goes and how light hits it) from the PC's main processor onto the graphics chip. The term "GPU" was also brilliant branding: Nvidia wrote a definition that only its own chip met ([Nvidia GeForce 256 page, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20060324123500/http:/www.nvidia.com/page/geforce256.html)).

## Chapter 4: A fast, boring supplier beat the glamorous 3D king (1999–2003)

In the late 1990s, **3dfx** and its Voodoo cards "dominated the retail market," but retail was only about 10% of the market; most chips went to PC makers ([Forbes, Dec 2000](https://www.forbes.com/2000/12/18/12183dfx.html)). In December 1998 3dfx bought the board maker STB for $141 million ([EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/ballard-steps-down-as-head-of-struggling-graphics-chip-maker-3dfx/)), turning itself into a competitor of the very card makers that bought its chips. "As a result, former customers began using Nvidia chips" ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/2000/12/18/12183dfx.html)). The 3D Realms CEO put it bluntly in 2000: "because of the STB purchase, I think they lost focus on chip innovation" ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/2000/12/3dfx-fall-good-voodoo-for-nvidia/)). 3dfx also could not keep up with the six-month cycle PC makers demanded, and its delayed chips arrived after Nvidia had already moved on ([Level2 Ventures](https://level2.vc/news/a-short-story-of-3dfx)).

Nvidia's answer was organizational. Huang asked why no graphics company could hold a lead for more than two years when PC makers refreshed twice a year and a chip took 18 months to design ([Askeladden review of *The Nvidia Way*](https://askeladdencapital.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-review-notes-and-analysis/)). The result, per Tae Kim, was "one 18-month architecture and then... two faster derivatives every six months," the "Three Teams, Two Seasons" system ([Motley Fool interview with Tae Kim](https://www.fool.com/investing/2025/01/29/interview-tae-kim-author-of-the-nvidia-way/)). On **December 15, 2000**, Nvidia agreed to buy 3dfx's graphics assets for **$70 million in cash and 1 million shares** ([Nvidia press release, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20190330171444/http:/www.nvidia.com/object/IO_20010612_6602.html)), closing April 18, 2001 ([Nvidia 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287001500581/d8k.txt)). Meanwhile, Microsoft chose Nvidia for the original Xbox and paid a **$200 million advance** in April 2000 ([EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/nvidia-receives-200-million-advance-payment-from-microsoft/); [Nvidia 8-K, 2002](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000095016802001118/d8k.htm)), nearly five times what the IPO had raised a year earlier.

The winning streak did not make Nvidia invincible. The **GeForce FX** (2003) lost the performance crown to ATI's Radeon 9700; German magazine c't called its cooler "like a mid-sized hairdryer" ([heise](https://www.heise.de/news/Gemischte-Ergebnisse-bei-ersten-Tests-von-Nvidias-GeForce-FX-Update-73695.html)), and Nvidia mocked its own "Dustbuster" in a parody video ([How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/nvidias-most-iconic-gpu-was-dubbed-the-dustbuster-for-a-reason/)). Rather than fire the product managers, Huang made them present every decision behind the fiasco to a few hundred colleagues ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)), an early form of the public "failure reviews" that became Nvidia culture.

## Chapter 5: Wall Street hated the decade-long CUDA bet (2004–2012)

In the early 2000s, a few researchers discovered that gaming chips, built to run tiny programs on "a million pixels, sixty times per second," were also extraordinary at math ([NVIDIA Technical Blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/inside-the-programming-evolution-of-gpu-computing/)). Using them was miserable. Stanford graduate student **Ian Buck** said that "it required a PhD in computer graphics to be able to port an application to the GPU" ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/reviews/ian-buck-nvidia,2393.html)). Buck had once chained 32 GeForce cards to play Quake across a wall of eight projectors ("It was the first gaming rig in 8K resolution"), then built a language called Brook to make GPUs programmable ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)). Nvidia hired him around 2004–2005: "At the time, it was just myself and one other engineer" ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/reviews/ian-buck-nvidia,2393.html)).

On **November 8, 2006**, Nvidia unveiled **CUDA** alongside the GeForce 8800, "the industry's first C-compiler development environment for the GPU" ([Nvidia press release, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20200703112618/http:/www.nvidia.com/object/IO_37226.html)); a public SDK followed in February 2007 and version 1.0 in June 2007 ([CUDA 1.0 release](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/cuda-1-0-released/982)). The fateful decision was to put it on every GeForce card, not just expensive professional ones. "We were democratizing supercomputing," Huang said ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)), and later explained: "Install base defines an architecture... Everything else is secondary" ([Lex Fridman #494](https://lexfridman.podhood.com/eca4cb06-5e04-43fd-b967-6fa4eea152cf)). The cost, in his own recollection, was brutal: "we increased our cost by fifty percent... and we were a thirty-five percent gross margin company... GeForce would carry the burden of CUDA, and none of the gamers would appreciate it," and it "took a decade" to pay off ([Lex Fridman #494](https://lexfridman.podhood.com/eca4cb06-5e04-43fd-b967-6fa4eea152cf)). Nvidia never broke out CUDA's cost, but total R&D rose from **$553.5 million in FY2007 to $908.9 million in FY2010** ([FY2009 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581009000013/fy2009form10k.htm); [FY2010 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581010000006/0001045810-10-000006.txt)). Nvidia marketed CUDA to oil prospectors, stock traders and biologists, and even helped General Mills simulate cooking frozen pizza; AI wasn't a target because "there didn't seem to be much of a market" ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)).

"When CUDA was released, in late 2006, Wall Street reacted with dismay" ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)). Then 2008 stacked three blows. On July 2 Nvidia cut its forecast and flagged a charge for defective laptop chips; the stock fell **30.7% in one day** ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/2008/07/03/nvidia-semiconductors-update-markets-equity-cx_mp_0703markets23.html)). The final charge was **$196 million**, nearly eight times what Nvidia had reserved for all warranties ([Nvidia Q2 FY09 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581008000018/q209form8kpressrelease.htm); [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1567281/bad-graphics-chips-cost-nvidia-196m.html)). Fiscal 2009 revenue fell to $3.42 billion with a net loss of $30 million, yet R&D still rose 24% ([Nvidia Q4 FY09 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581009000005/pressrelease.htm)). Market value sank from about **$19 billion at end-2007 to about $3.5–4 billion in late November 2008** ([FinHacker](https://www.finhacker.cz/en/stocks/nvidia-market-cap/); [StatMuse](https://www.statmuse.com/money/ask/nvidia-stock-market-cap-2008)). CUDA downloads peaked in 2009 and then fell for three years, board members feared corporate raiders, and a director who joined in 2013 called it "a distinctly flat, stagnant company" ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)). When activist fund Starboard Value took a stake in 2013, the board worried it would force cuts to CUDA ([CNBC excerpt of *The Nvidia Way*](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/12/05/nvidia-and-starboard-value-excerpt-from-tae-kims-book-on-nvidia.html)).

Nvidia kept seeding the ground anyway. Chief scientist David Kirk co-taught one of the first university courses in GPU programming at Illinois in spring 2007 ([Illinois ECE](https://ece.illinois.edu/news/2522)), and in June 2008 Illinois became the first "CUDA Center of Excellence" with a $500,000 gift, on condition that it teach CUDA ([NVIDIA/UIUC release](https://www.ks.uiuc.edu/Publications/pdfs/2008-06-30-nvidia.com-NVIDIA_Appoints_First_CUDA_Center_of_Excellence.pdf)). The irony is that it fumbled the field that would matter most. In 2009 Geoffrey Hinton emailed Nvidia: "Look, I just told a thousand machine-learning researchers they should go and buy Nvidia cards. Can you send me a free one?... They said no" ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)).

During the same years, the rivals took different forks. In 2005, Intel CEO Paul Otellini reportedly proposed buying Nvidia for as much as $20 billion and the board resisted, backing its own Larrabee graphics project instead ([New York Times, Oct 24, 2024](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/10/24/technology/intel-ai-chips-mistakes.html?smid=url-share&unlocked_article_code=1.Uk4.08OJ.SCT6kgtDjvcz)); Intel scrapped Larrabee as a graphics product in December 2009 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-intel/intel-scraps-graphics-chip-based-on-larrabee-idUSTRE5B51QR20091206/?type=technologyNews)). In 2006 AMD bought ATI for $5.4 billion ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/amd-confirmed-usd5-4-billion-ati-acquisition-20-years-ago-today-deal-to-reinvent-our-industry-paved-the-way-for-radeon-gpu-innovation-apus-and-games-console-domination)) after, according to former AMD employees, talks with Nvidia stalled because Huang insisted on running the combined company ([Forbes, 2012](https://www.forbes.com/sites/briancaulfield/2012/02/22/amd-talked-with-nvidia-about-acquisition-before-grabbing-ati/); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/news/AMD-ATI-Nvidia-GPU-Tegra,14795.html)). Nvidia's own detour in this era, smartphones, failed: it bought modem maker Icera for $367 million in 2011 and wound it down in 2015, with Huang conceding that "the phone marketplace has commoditized really, really fast" ([Fierce Network](https://www.fierce-network.com/wireless/nvidia-to-exit-icera-cellular-baseband-business); [Rethink Research](https://rethinkresearch.biz/articles/nvidia-confirms-its-smartphone-exit-with-icera-shutdown/)).

## Chapter 6: Two gaming cards in a bedroom started the AI era (2012–2016)

In 2012, Alex Krizhevsky and Ilya Sutskever, Hinton's students, "bought two GeForce cards from Amazon" ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)). Their neural network, **AlexNet**, trained for "between five and six days... on two GTX 580 3GB GPUs" and won the ImageNet image-recognition contest with a **15.3% error rate versus 26.2%** for the runner-up ([AlexNet paper](http://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~ilya/pubs/2012/imgnet.pdf)). That was a jump of more than ten points when the previous year's improvement had been about two ([Dive into Deep Learning](https://d2l.smola.org/chapter_convolutional-modern/alexnet.html)). Hinton's telling is pure documentary material: "He had the two G.P.U. boards whirring in his bedroom... Actually, it was his parents who paid for the quite considerable electricity costs." He called it "a kind of Big Bang moment," and Sutskever said, "G.P.U.s showed up and it felt like a miracle" ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)). Hinton also explained why Nvidia won by default: "To do machine learning without CUDA would have just been too much trouble" ([same](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)).

A second demonstration made the economics obvious. Google had trained a famous neural network that learned to recognize cats on YouTube using **16,000 CPU cores in 1,000 machines**; a Stanford team with Nvidia's Bryan Catanzaro matched that scale with **just 3 machines** holding 12 Nvidia GPUs ([Coates et al., ICML 2013](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/coates13.pdf)), at roughly **$20,000 versus $1 million** ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2013/06/18/google-brain-king-slashes-cost-of-ai-gear/1409139)).

Huang moved fast. In 2013 he bet the company on AI ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)). Nvidia VP Greg Estes recalled: "He sent out an e-mail on Friday evening saying everything is going to deep learning, and that we were no longer a graphics company... By Monday morning, we were an A.I. company. Literally, it was that fast." Huang told Catanzaro to imagine all 8,000 employees marched into the parking lot and to pick anyone he wanted for his team ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)). On **September 7, 2014**, Nvidia released **cuDNN**, a free library packaging the hand-tuned math Krizhevsky had written himself, so AI frameworks could use GPUs without CUDA expertise ([NVIDIA Developer Forums](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/announcing-nvidia-reg-cudnn-ndash-gpu-accelerated-machine-learning/34741)). In April 2016 it announced **DGX-1**, billed as "250 servers in a box," at **$129,000** ([Fortune, 2016](https://fortune.com/2016/08/15/elon-musk-artificial-intelligence-openai-nvidia-supercomputer/)). In August 2016 Huang personally delivered the first one to a young nonprofit called OpenAI, with a handwritten note: "To Elon & the OpenAI Team! To the future of computing and humanity. I present you the World's First DGX-1!" Elon Musk opened the box with a box cutter ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2016/08/15/elon-musk-artificial-intelligence-openai-nvidia-supercomputer/); [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)).

**On-screen beat:** in 2009 Nvidia refused Hinton a free card. Seven years later, its CEO hand-delivered a $129,000 AI supercomputer as a gift to an AI lab.

## Chapter 7: From chip seller to builder of "one giant computer" (2017–2022)

Google's transformer architecture appeared in 2017, and OpenAI's GPT models were trained on Nvidia supercomputers ([The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)). GPT-3 (2020) used **10,000 Nvidia V100 GPUs for 14.8 days** ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/tJAD2LG9uweeEfjwq/estimating-efficiency-improvements-in-llm-pre-training)). Nvidia's key strategic move in this period was recognizing that AI models were outgrowing single chips. On **March 11, 2019**, it outbid Intel for the Israeli networking firm **Mellanox**, at about **$6.9 billion** ([Nvidia](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-to-acquire-mellanox-for-6-9-billion); [Reuters on Intel's bid](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-mellanox-m-a-intel/intel-offered-up-to-6-billion-for-israels-mellanox-reports-idUSKCN1PO0O8/?feedName=technologyNews&feedType=RSS)), so it could stitch servers "together into one giant computer" ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-mellanox-m-a-nvidia/nvidia-to-buy-israels-mellanox-for-6-8-billion-in-data-center-push-idUSKBN1QS197/)). Huang later called it "a homerun deal" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/04/27/nvidia-ceo-calls-mellanox-acquisition-a-homerun-deal.html)). Its attempt to buy chip designer Arm, announced September 2020, collapsed in February 2022 under regulatory opposition, costing Nvidia a **$1.25 billion** prepayment, though it kept an Arm license and later built its own Arm-based CPUs ([Nvidia](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-and-softbank-group-announce-termination-of-nvidias-acquisition-of-arm-limited); [UK CMA](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/nvidia-abandons-takeover-of-arm-during-cma-investigation)).

The other lesson of this era was that chips that can do anything attract buyers you did not plan for. Crypto miners inflated gaming sales twice. In November 2018 Huang admitted "the crypto hangover lasted longer than we expected" as shares fell about 19% the next day ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-nvidia-results/nvidia-forecasts-revenue-below-estimates-shares-slump-17-percent-idUSKCN1NK2ZF/?il=0); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/11/16/nvidia-stock-falls-on-revenue-and-guidance-miss.html)). In May 2022 the SEC fined Nvidia **$5.5 million** for not disclosing that crypto mining had driven a significant part of its 2017 gaming growth ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2022-79)), and the 2022 crypto bust forced a $1.34 billion inventory-related charge ([Nvidia 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581022000147/nvda-20220731.htm)). At the end of 2022, Nvidia was worth **$359.5 billion** ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/nvda/market-cap/)).

## Chapter 8: ChatGPT made Nvidia the most valuable company on Earth (Nov 2022–Oct 2026)

The ChatGPT effect reached Nvidia's books about six months after the chatbot launched. On **May 24, 2023**, Nvidia forecast quarterly revenue more than 50% above Wall Street estimates ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/nvidia-forecasts-second-quarter-revenue-above-estimates-2023-05-24/)); the next day the stock rose 24%, adding about **$184 billion** in a single session ([Reuters via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/nvidia-close-becoming-first-trillion-094234567.html)), and Nvidia crossed **$1 trillion on May 30, 2023** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/list_of_public_corporations_by_market_capitalization)). An H100 chip was estimated at $20,000–40,000 ([Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/tech/hoppers-blackwells-and-rubins-a-field-guide-to-the-complicated-world-of/)), and buyers were rationed. Oracle's Larry Ellison described a dinner at Nobu with Musk and Huang as "me and Elon begging Jensen for GPUs. Please take our money; no, take more of it" ([Fortune, Sep 2024](https://fortune.com/2024/09/16/larry-ellison-elon-musk-begged-nvidias-jensen-huang-more-gpus-fancy-sushi-dinner/)).

The numbers are almost cartoonish. Revenue went from **$27.0 billion in FY2023 to $215.9 billion in FY2026**, and net income from **$4.4 billion to $120.1 billion** ([Nvidia Q4 FY24](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2024/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-2024/); [Nvidia Q4 FY26](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-2026/default.aspx)). Nvidia shifted to a yearly product cadence, announced at Computex in June 2024: "Our company has a one-year rhythm" ([Nvidia blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/computex-2024-jensen-huang/)), shipping Blackwell, then Blackwell Ultra, then Vera Rubin, whose racks are estimated at $5–9 million each ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/price-of-nvidias-vera-rubin-nvl72-racks-skyrockets-to-as-much-as-usd8-8-million-apiece-but-server-makers-margins-will-be-tight-nvidia-is-moving-closer-to-shipping-entire-full-scale-systems); [Plugin Capital](https://plugin-capital.com/2026/06/15/how-much-would-it-cost-to-build-a-gw-level-vera-rubin-data-center/)). It passed $3 trillion on June 5, 2024 and became the world's most valuable company on June 18, 2024 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/nvidias-stock-market-value-hits-3-trillion-first-time-2024-06-05/); [Quartz](https://qz.com/nvidia-timeline-gaming-chips-ai-giant)), then became the **first company ever to reach $4 trillion (July 9, 2025) and $5 trillion (October 29, 2025)** ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/nvidia-becomes-first-company-clinch-4-trillion-market-value-2025-07-09/); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/nvidia-poised-record-5-trillion-market-valuation-2025-10-29/)).

The rise was never smooth. On **January 27, 2025**, the Chinese AI lab DeepSeek's efficient model triggered a 17% drop that erased about **$589–593 billion** of Nvidia's value in one day, the largest single-day loss for any company in history ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/chinas-deepseek-sets-off-ai-market-rout-2025-01-27/); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-01-27/asml-sinks-as-china-ai-startup-triggers-panic-in-tech-stocks)). In April 2025 US export rules on its China chip forced a $4.5 billion charge, and Huang said "the $50 billion China market is effectively closed to us" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/28/nvidia-expects-to-lose-billions-in-revenue-due-to-h20-chip-licensing-requirements/)). Nvidia increasingly acted as financier as well as supplier: it agreed to invest **$5 billion in Intel** in September 2025, a once-unthinkable role reversal ([Nvidia](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2025/NVIDIA-and-Intel-to-Develop-AI-Infrastructure-and-Personal-Computing-Products/default.aspx); [Intel 8-K](https://www.intc.com/filings-reports/all-sec-filings/content/0000050863-25-000204/intc-20251226.htm)), and a headline "up to $100 billion" OpenAI deal shrank to a **$30 billion** equity investment in February 2026 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/04/nvidia-huang-openai-investment.html); [Reuters via MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/openai-s-110-billion-funding-round-draws-investment-from-amazon-nvidia-softbank-ce7e5cdfdb8aff23)), feeding worries about "circular" deals in which suppliers fund their own customers ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-ai-circular-deals/)). The peak came on **October 6, 2026: about $5.79 trillion** at $243.37 a share, with analysts talking about the first $6 trillion company ([ET Now](https://www.etnownews.com/markets/from-5-79-trillion-to-4-32-trillion-nvidia-loses-1-47-trillion-in-market-value-since-october-peak-as-jensen-huang-probe-weighs-on-sentiment-article-156488585); [Forbes via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/jensen-huang-lost-quarter-wealth-173124517.html)).

## What Nvidia did that others didn't

The honest answer is that Nvidia combined several unglamorous habits that each rival had only some of, and it kept them for 30 years. Its competitors had more money (Intel), comparable hardware (ATI/AMD) or better early technology (3dfx). None had all of the following at once.

### It sold to everyone instead of competing with its customers

3dfx bought a board maker and turned its customers into rivals; Nvidia stayed a chip supplier to every card maker, so "if Nvidia's chips were competitive at all, every other maker of graphics cards would rather buy chips from them" ([Silicon Underground](https://dfarq.homeip.net/why-3dfx-failed-in-the-year-2000/)). The same shape repeats today: Nvidia sells to Amazon, Google and Microsoft even as they design competing chips, and Amazon's CEO says "I don't foresee a day where we're not partners with Nvidia" ([CNBC transcript, Nov 17, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/cnbc-exclusive-transcript-amazon-president-ceo-andy-jassy-speaks-with-cnbcs-scott-wapner-on-halftime-report-today-.html)).

### It shipped on a clock while rivals chased perfection

3dfx "wanted perfection in every product that we shipped," according to its former marketing executive, and slipped schedules ([Américo Dias on *The Nvidia Way*](https://americo.dias.pt/posts/nvidia-way-lessons/)). Intel's Larrabee fell behind, with one analyst estimating that "every month of delays is equivalent to losing 3.9% performance" against Nvidia and ATI ([Real World Tech](https://www.realworldtech.com/larrabee-deferred/)). Nvidia's six-month cadence in 1999 and its one-year rhythm from 2024 are the same idea, which Huang summed up as "The competition will always be shooting behind the duck" ([Américo Dias](https://americo.dias.pt/posts/nvidia-way-lessons/)).

### It paid for a market that did not exist yet

This is the single best answer to "why Nvidia?" Huang calls these "zero-billion-dollar markets": "where there are no customers, there are also no competitors" ([Nvidia blog, Caltech 2024](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/jensen-huang-caltech-commencement-address/)). Putting CUDA on every gaming card cost margin and investor patience for years, but it meant that by 2012 hundreds of millions of cheap GPUs could run AI code, and students were already learning it. Andrew Ng remembers students telling him, "Hey Andrew, there's this thing called CUDA" ([Sequoia, *Crucible Moments*](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/crucible-moments-nvidia)). The cross-vendor alternative, OpenCL, stalled in committee politics and was "strategically hobbled" on Nvidia's own hardware ([Modular](https://www.modular.com/blog/democratizing-ai-compute-part-5-what-about-cuda-c-alternatives)); AMD's ROCm arrived around 2016, and as of 2026 SemiAnalysis says "CUDA parity has not yet been demonstrated" ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/can-amd-break-the-cuda-moat-amd-advancing)).

### It treated software as the product

"More than half of our engineers work on software," Nvidia's FY2026 annual report says, and it claims over 7.5 million developers use CUDA and its tools ([Nvidia 10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)). Ben Thompson's summary is that Nvidia "is not a hardware company, and not a software company: it is a company that integrates both" ([Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2024/nvidia-waves-and-moats/)). Libraries such as cuDNN, the university courses and the free hardware to labs were the unglamorous work that made the ecosystem sticky.

### It built the whole machine, not just the chip

Mellanox networking and NVLink let Nvidia sell entire liquid-cooled racks of 72 GPUs that behave as one computer ([Nvidia 10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)). SemiAnalysis now frames Nvidia's moat as making AI work spread across many machines, a "distributed-systems problem rather than a single-kernel one" ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/can-amd-break-the-cuda-moat-amd-advancing)). Nvidia is also TSMC's largest customer for the advanced CoWoS packaging its chips require ([DIGITIMES](https://apps.digitimes.com/news/a20241025VL210/cowos-demand-packaging-2025-capacity.html)).

### It ran on paranoia, flat structure and fast admissions of failure

Huang had about 60 direct reports, held no one-on-ones and gave feedback in public: "Feedback is learning. For what reason are you the only person who should learn this?" ([Stripe Sessions 2024](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang)). Employees send "Top 5" emails so he can catch weak signals early ([Tae Kim](https://taekim.substack.com/p/nvidias-unique-culture)). The "30 days from going out of business" mindset lasted 33 years: "The feeling doesn't change" ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-joe-rogan-2025-12)). Nvidia also cut losses quickly on NV1, the GeForce FX, Tegra phones and Arm. The darker side is well documented too: Witt's book describes "the Wrath of Huang," including a public berating of a chip architect that lasted up to two hours ([Telegraph review](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/books/non-fiction/review-the-thinking-machine-stephen-witt/)). Turnover nonetheless stayed unusually low, at 2.5% in FY2025 ([Nvidia Sustainability Report FY2025](https://images.nvidia.com/aem-dam/Solutions/documents/NVIDIA-Sustainability-Report-Fiscal-Year-2025.pdf)).

### The honest caveat: luck and survivorship

The same philosophy also produced misses, and the decisive AI breakthroughs came from academics using gaming cards, not from an Nvidia plan; Nvidia even refused Hinton's request. Gamers unknowingly subsidized CUDA for a decade, and crypto miners twice inflated demand. The fair framing for viewers is a high-risk bet that kept the company alive long enough to be standing in the right place, not a guaranteed formula. Many of the most quotable stories also come from Huang's own retellings decades later, and they shift in the details.

## Where it stands today (Nov 20, 2026)

### The business is at a record

In the quarter ended October 25, 2026 (reported November 17), Nvidia posted revenue of **$115.8 billion (+103% year over year)**, Data Center revenue of **$107.6 billion**, gross margin of **74.8%** and net income of **$65.3 billion**, and guided to **$130 billion** for the next quarter. It called the Vera Rubin rack ramp "the fastest product ramp in the company's history" ([Nvidia Q3 FY27](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)). Revenue for the first nine months of the fiscal year, $293.7 billion, already exceeds all of fiscal 2026 ([same](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)). Nvidia had about 42,000 employees as of January 2026 ([Nvidia 10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)).

### The stock is collapsing anyway

Despite that report, Nvidia fell to about **$3.27 trillion** intraday on November 20, down about 41% from its October peak, slipping behind Alphabet to become the **fourth most valuable US company** behind Apple, Microsoft and Alphabet ([Forbes via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/jensen-huang-lost-quarter-wealth-173124517.html); [Reuters via StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Nvidias+share+slide+knocks+it+below+Alphabet+in+valuation/27380111.html)). It was on course for a seventh straight weekly decline, which Reuters said would be its longest losing streak since 2008 ([Reuters via StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Nvidias+share+slide+knocks+it+below+Alphabet+in+valuation/27380111.html)). Its forward price-to-earnings ratio had fallen to 10.84, "by far the lowest among the tech megacaps" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html)). The causes overlap: AI-spending fears after a rough October earnings season, a November 16 US rule closing a loophole for Chinese firms renting Nvidia chips abroad, and the leadership crisis ([Forbes via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/jensen-huang-lost-quarter-wealth-173124517.html); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html)).

### Jensen Huang resigned on November 11–12, 2026

**The facts.** Nvidia's 8-K filing states that on November 11, 2026, the board "accepted the resignation of Jen-Hsun Huang, as NVIDIA's President, Chief Executive Officer and member of the Board, effective immediately." CFO **Colette Kress** became interim CEO, and director **Harvey C. Jones**, on the board since 1993, became its first-ever chair ([Nvidia 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000082/nvda-20261111.htm); [Nvidia press release, Nov 12, 2026](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition)). The filing itself does not give a reason ([Software Lifecycle](https://www.software-lifecycle.com/profiles/nvidia)). Jones later said Huang resigned after disclosing behavior "inconsistent with the company's code of conduct" and that the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html)). Huang had been CEO since the 1993 founding, roughly 33.5 years. Weeks earlier, in October, he had said he hoped to keep running the company for 20 more years ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/12/nx-s1-6037833/jensen-huang-nvidia-stepped-down)).

**The reported allegations, which are unproven.** According to NPR's summary of New York Times reporting, which NPR said it had not independently confirmed, the Times reported in late October 2026 that Huang had a close relationship with a woman at a Singapore-based company that bought Nvidia chips, and that US intelligence officials believed she had been recruited by Chinese intelligence. In a November 8 email to staff, he admitted being unfaithful to his wife. The Times later reported a second affair, with an Nvidia executive who reported to him ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/12/nx-s1-6037833/jensen-huang-nvidia-stepped-down)). Beijing denies that the woman is a spy, and US Attorney General Todd Blanche said the Justice Department "has not yet reached a conclusion" ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resigns-in-blow-to-ai-boom-5021980)). Separately, the Justice and Commerce Departments confirmed they had been investigating the Singapore firm for export violations since 2024, and Nvidia had put it on a "no sell" list in June 2025 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html)). Any script should present the espionage link strictly as reported allegations and should not name the private individuals involved.

**What is happening now.** Independent directors, working with a former federal prosecutor, are continuing to investigate ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/nvidia-investigation-jensen-huang-continue-departure-sources-say-rcna609136)). Law firm Wachtell Lipton is examining whether national security or company secrets were compromised, Heidrick & Struggles is running the CEO search, and on November 17 the board created a committee on national security and export controls ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html)). Huang said he made "serious mistakes" and would be "honored" to return if the board wanted it; Musk called for his return and President Trump said he would be open to it ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html)). Huang and General Counsel Tim Teter are due before congressional committees in early December ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nvidia-falls-below-alphabet-to-become-fourth-most-valuable-us-company-5042158)). Names floated as permanent successors, all speculation, include Ian Buck, who built CUDA, AMD's Lisa Su and Arm's Rene Haas ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/colette-kress-the-cfo-who-challenged-huangs-bets-is-nvidias-interim-ceo-5036760)). Huang's net worth fell from about $204.5 billion in early October to roughly $119–127.5 billion on November 20 ([MythPost, citing Forbes](https://www.mythpost.com/money-deals/jensen-huang-net-worth-2026/); [Forbes profile](https://www.forbes.com/profile/jensen-huang/)). Witt's verdict is the line a documentary might close on: "There's no obvious replacement. It's not even clear Nvidia can be the same kind of company without Jensen" ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/12/nx-s1-6037833/jensen-huang-nvidia-stepped-down)).

### The moat is narrowing at the top

Estimates of Nvidia's share of AI-chip revenue fell from about 87% in 2024 to roughly 70–80% in 2026; these are analyst estimates, not audited figures ([Silicon Analysts](https://siliconanalysts.com/analysis/nvidia-ai-accelerator-market-share-2024-2026); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/custom-ai-asics-examined-from-broadcom-to-mtia)). Google launched four TPU systems in 2026 and now sells TPUs outside its own cloud ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/tpu-8i-max-and-8tx/); [Bloomberg via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/google-escalates-nvidia-rivalry-specialized-123000247.html)). Amazon's chip business has passed a $25 billion annual revenue run rate ([CNBC transcript](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/cnbc-exclusive-transcript-amazon-president-ceo-andy-jassy-speaks-with-cnbcs-scott-wapner-on-halftime-report-today-.html)), and AMD signed a 6-gigawatt deal with OpenAI ([AMD](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1260/amd-and-openai-announce-strategic-partnership-to-deploy-6-gigawatts-of-amd-gpus)). Customer concentration is high: in FY2026 one direct customer accounted for 22% of revenue and another for 14% ([Nvidia 10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)). Nvidia also carried $118 billion in guarantees tied to customers' data-center financing as of October 25, 2026 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html)). The fair late-2026 framing is that Nvidia is no longer the only option for the biggest buyers, but it is still by far the biggest seller.

## Disputed or weak claims: avoid or hedge

- **"$40,000 of their own money" to found Nvidia.** It appears only in secondary sources with no primary citation ([Business Plus](https://businessplus.ie/tech/nvidia-jensen-huang/)). A summary of Tae Kim's book says each founder contributed $200 ([ursummary](https://ursummary.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-summary/)). Safer: "a few hundred dollars of their own, then about $2 million from Sequoia and Sutter Hill, according to Tae Kim."
- **"70–90 rival graphics companies."** No source uses this range. Huang has variously said about 30, about 50, 60 and "one of a hundred" ([Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang); [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/flashback-how-the-kindness-of-sega-saved-nvidia-from-going-under); [Berkeley Haas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9hzVdV63scU)), and Witt says at least 35. Safer: "by Huang's own count, somewhere between 30 and 100 companies," and say Nvidia is the only *independent* survivor, since ATI lives on inside AMD.
- **"Nvidia spent $500 million a year on CUDA."** It traces only to a low-quality biography site ([hub.cissychen.com](https://hub.cissychen.com/biographies/huang-day6.en.html)). Book summaries attribute about $475–500 million to developing the G80 chip over four years ([getAbstract](https://journal.getabstract.com/en/review/the-nvidia-saga/)), and Nvidia never broke out CUDA spending. Use Huang's own "+50% cost" line or the total R&D figures instead.
- **Huang's claim that Nvidia fell to "one and a half billion dollars."** That is his recollection ([Lex Fridman #494](https://lexfridman.podhood.com/eca4cb06-5e04-43fd-b967-6fa4eea152cf)); at Stripe he said "like a billion dollars" ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang)). Market data shows a low of about $3.5–4 billion in late 2008. His "six, seven billion" pre-CUDA figure is also low, against about $13 billion at end-2006 and $19 billion at end-2007 ([FinHacker](https://www.finhacker.cz/en/stocks/nvidia-market-cap/)).
- **TSMC made RIVA 128.** Nvidia's own 1998 press release says SGS-Thomson fabricated it ([press release](https://web.archive.org/web/20110326212747/http:/www.nvidia.com/object/IO_20020114_4455.html)). Huang's Rogan retelling of going "straight to production at TSMC" conflicts with this ([Rosetta transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/joerogan/joe-rogan-experience-2422-jensen-huang)).
- **The emulator cost.** Kim says about $1 million; Huang on Rogan and a Medium post say "half" of a roughly $1 million bank balance. Huang's claim that IKOS was "shutting down" that day is single-source.
- **Sega's $5 million.** Whether it was a contract payment or a share purchase is unclear, and Sega has no records ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/georgia-fearn/jensen-huang-sega-shoichiro-irimajiri-nvidia-technology-failed-5-million/91376429)). The sale price of Sega's stake is unverified: TechSpot says about $15 million, and VGC's "[for $300m]" is an editorial insertion. The date varies from 1995 to 1996. A Kim summary mentions a separate $1 million prototype payment.
- **Layoff size in 1996.** Estimates range from "half" (New Yorker) to "60%, leaving about 40" (Kim) to "more than 100 to 35" (Witt). No filing confirms any of them.
- **"30 days of cash."** It is a motto, not an audited figure after 1997. The New Yorker's "one month of payroll" at RIVA 128's launch is the documented version.
- **The name.** "NV = next version" (Huang) versus "new venture" (Witt) is genuinely ambiguous. The "NVision was taken by a toilet-paper maker" story appears in The New Yorker but is otherwise weakly sourced.
- **IPO misstatements.** One blog gives a $73.5 million market cap and some summaries call $19.69 the IPO price; both are wrong. The offer price was $12 and the first-day close $19.6875.
- **"First GPU."** This is Nvidia's own marketing definition. 3D cards existed before GeForce 256.
- **The Intel $20 billion idea (2005)** rests on anonymous sources in the NYT and is unconfirmed by either company. **The AMD merger talks with Huang demanding to be CEO** rest on former AMD employees' recollections.
- **The CUDA launch year.** It was announced in November 2006 and the public SDK followed in 2007; both are defensible. Buck joined in 2004 or 2005 depending on the source; use "around 2004–2005."
- **Google's "cat" network.** It used 16,000 CPU cores; Nvidia's blog says "2,000 CPUs." Say "16,000 processor cores."
- **AlexNet "in a bedroom."** This comes from Hinton's quote, not the paper. Attribute it to him.
- **The Mythbusters GPU demo** is reported as 1,100 or 2,100 paintball barrels. Check the original video before stating a number.
- **The GPT-4 training cluster (~25,000 A100s)** is an unconfirmed estimate.
- **Vera Rubin "$20 billion in its first quarter"** comes from Tom's Hardware and Zacks, not Nvidia's release. Rack prices ($5–9 million) are estimates; Nvidia publishes no list prices.
- **Market share figures** (87% in 2024, 70–80% in 2026, JPMorgan's "28% of future contracts") are analyst estimates with inconsistent definitions, not audited data.
- **"7.5 million CUDA developers"** is a company figure that includes "other software tools."
- **The China-revenue claim** that 20% of FY2026 compute revenue was "driven by China" comes from short seller Culper Research, and Nvidia denies diversion.
- **Direct reports.** Huang said 60 in 2024; an analyst said 70 in 2026. The number varies by date.
- **Quote misattributions.** "Ample doses of pain and suffering" was said at the Stanford SIEPR Economic Summit (March 1, 2024), not the GSB "View From The Top." "If I had known how hard it would be, I wouldn't have started Nvidia" is a paraphrase; his words were "I wouldn't do it... a million times harder."
- **Scandal timeline.** One Reuters-derived note dates the first NYT report to October 2025, while NPR says late October 2026. The 2026 date fits the sequence of events. Further relationships reported by some outlets are unconfirmed, and the espionage link is an allegation on which the DOJ has reached no conclusion.

**Writer's suggestions (not from sources; label as narration, not fact)**
- **Professor vs. stadium of schoolkids:** a CPU is a brilliant math professor; a GPU is a stadium of thousands of kids who each know only arithmetic. To solve one hard proof, hire the professor. To add a million pairs of numbers, hire the stadium.
- **The hidden tax on every gamer:** for a decade, every gamer buying a GeForce card paid for a feature almost none of them used, and that hidden tax built the runway AI later landed on.
- **The QWERTY keyboard plus the whole kitchen (the moat):** everyone already learned Nvidia's keyboard (CUDA), Nvidia sells the whole kitchen rather than just the oven (racks, networking, software), and it installs a new kitchen every year. Rivals can match one appliance, but rarely the whole kitchen.
- **Selling shovels to every miner, not digging for gold:** 3dfx tried to own the whole gold mine and lost its customers; Nvidia sold shovels to every miner, from card makers in 1999 to cloud giants in 2026.

## Conclusion

The most useful revision to the popular Nvidia myth is that its defining trait was not genius foresight but the ability to survive long, expensive bets. Huang did not predict AlexNet; Nvidia refused Hinton a free card. What it did was keep CUDA on every chip through a stock collapse and three years of falling downloads, so that when outsiders discovered the use, the only viable platform was already in their bedrooms. That reframes the rivals' failures as failures of patience and posture: 3dfx competed with its own customers, Intel trusted its existing x86 franchise, and AMD treated software as a supporting feature. None of them was simply out-engineered.

November 2026 shows that the same traits cut both ways. A company deliberately built so that information flowed to one person, and so that one person's paranoia set the pace, now has record earnings and no founder. The documentary's closing question is not whether Nvidia's chips stay good. It is whether a culture designed around Jensen Huang can keep "shooting ahead of the duck" without him, while Google, Amazon and AMD use open software such as TorchTPU, Triton and ROCm to attack the CUDA lock-in that made the moat in the first place.
