# Nvidia's Founding and Near-Death Early Years (1993 to about 2002)

Source-quality legend used below:
- **[PRIMARY]**: SEC filings, Nvidia press releases of the time, or Jensen Huang / Shoichiro Irimajiri speaking on the record.
- **[BOOK]**: Tae Kim, *The Nvidia Way* (2024) or Stephen Witt, *The Thinking Machine* (2025). The Kim material is mostly taken from online summaries and reviews, not the book itself; Witt is quoted from a published excerpt.
- **[SECONDARY]**: Journalism or blogs repeating other accounts.

---

## 1. Founders, the Denny's meeting, money, the problem they set out to solve, and the name

### Takeaway
Three engineers, Jensen Huang (then at LSI Logic) and Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem (both from Sun Microsystems), planned Nvidia in booths at a Denny's in East San Jose. They incorporated it in April 1993 to bring 3D graphics, mainly for video games, to ordinary PCs. Sequoia and Sutter Hill then put in about $2M. The often-repeated "$40,000 of their own money" is not well sourced. Kim's book reportedly says each founder put in $200.

### Cited Findings
**Date and place**
- Nvidia was incorporated in California in April 1993 and reincorporated in Delaware in April 1998. [PRIMARY] — [Nvidia Investor FAQ](https://investor.nvidia.com/investor-resources/faqs/default.aspx); also in the [1999 IPO prospectus (SEC 424B4)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt)
- The exact incorporation date is given as April 5, 1993. [BOOK] — [Nvidia Way summary (ursummary)](https://ursummary.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-summary/); [Silicon Underground](https://dfarq.homeip.net/nvidias-ipo-on-january-22-1999/)
- Nvidia's own blog says that in 1993 the three "met at Denny's to discuss creating a chip that would enable realistic 3D graphics on personal computers." Huang lived nearby with his wife and kids. Nvidia and Denny's later unveiled a plaque at that Silicon Valley (San Jose) Denny's. [PRIMARY] — [NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-dennys-trillion/)
- Huang on why Denny's: "It had all the coffee you could drink and no one could kick you out." [PRIMARY quote via press] — [El País](https://english.elpais.com/economy-and-business/2024-03-04/inside-nvidias-museum-of-the-future.html)
- Kim's book reportedly places the meetings in **late 1992**, at a Denny's in **East San Jose**, over bottomless coffee. [BOOK] — [Joey Zhou notes on The Nvidia Way](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04)
- Personal hook: Huang worked at Denny's as a teenager, as a dishwasher and then a busboy or waiter. He has said he had his first hamburger and first milkshake at Denny's. [PRIMARY] — [NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-dennys-trillion/). Kim reportedly says this was a **Portland** Denny's. — [ursummary](https://ursummary.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-summary/)
- The "30th birthday" detail: Huang turned 30 in February 1993. A tabloid-style source says the idea came "during his 30th birthday" at Denny's. [SECONDARY, weak] — [Business Plus](https://businessplus.ie/tech/nvidia-jensen-huang/)

**Who the founders were and the problem**
- Priem and Malachowsky had built Sun's GX graphics accelerator. When Sun's leadership shelved Priem's next-generation design, they decided to leave and asked Huang, who managed LSI Logic's Sun account, to help them. [BOOK] — [Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04); [ursummary](https://ursummary.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-summary/)
- Huang was reluctant to leave LSI. He reportedly wanted proof that the startup could reach at least $50M in annual sales, and he became convinced by the size of the 3D video-game market. [BOOK] — [Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04)
- Huang: "If it wasn't because of Chris and Curtis convincing me to do Nvidia, I would still be at LSI Logic today. I'm certain of it." [PRIMARY] — [Acquired podcast, 2023](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)
- Huang's framing: in 1993 "we were the only consumer 3D graphics company ever created. We were focused on transforming the PC into an accelerated PC because at the time, Windows was really a software-rendered system." [PRIMARY] — [Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)
- Priem's personal motivation was a flight simulator that no PC graphics card of the time could run. [BOOK] — [Askeladden Capital review](https://askeladdencapital.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-review-notes-and-analysis/)
- Roles: Huang was CEO and handled business; Priem was CTO and chip architect; Malachowsky ran engineering and implementation. They worked out of Priem's townhouse before moving to an office in Sunnyvale. [BOOK] — [Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04)

**Money**
- **"$40,000 of their own money"**: this appears widely in popular press, but I found it only in secondary sources with no primary citation. [SECONDARY] — [Business Plus](https://businessplus.ie/tech/nvidia-jensen-huang/)
- **Conflicting claim:** a summary of Kim's book says Nvidia was incorporated "with each cofounder contributing $200." [BOOK] — [ursummary](https://ursummary.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-summary/)
- First venture money was **$2 million** from Sequoia Capital (Don Valentine) and Sutter Hill Ventures (Tench Coxe). [BOOK] — [ursummary](https://ursummary.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-summary/); [Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04). Quartz says "$10 million... from Sequoia Capital and Sutter Hill" to develop NV1, which probably counts later rounds too. [SECONDARY] — [Quartz](https://qz.com/nvidia-history-origin-ai-chip-jensen-huang-1851372906)
- The IPO prospectus confirms that Sequoia's Mark Stevens and Sutter Hill's Tench Coxe had been directors since **June 1993**. Before the IPO, Sequoia entities held 13.2%, Huang 13.1% (3.1M shares), and Malachowsky and Priem 13.0% each (3,062,500 shares). [PRIMARY] — [IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt)
- How the funding happened: LSI Logic CEO Wilf Corrigan called Don Valentine. In Huang's words: "he called up Don Valentine and told Don, invest in this kid." Huang also said: "I didn't know how to write a business plan." [PRIMARY] — [Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)
- Anecdotes from the pitch: Valentine told Priem "Pick one. Otherwise, you're going to fail because you don't know who you are," and closed with "If you lose my money, I will kill you." Kleiner Perkins and Apple reportedly passed. Kleiner wanted Nvidia to make its own circuit boards, and Malachowsky refused: "Why would we compete on a resistor?" [BOOK; single-source anecdotes] — [Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04); [Askeladden](https://askeladdencapital.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-review-notes-and-analysis/)

**The name (three competing versions of what "NV" stood for)**
- Version A, from Huang himself: "We named all of our files NV, as in 'next version'," then extended it to Nvidia from the Latin *invidia* (envy). [PRIMARY quote] — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/15/how-to-say-nvidias-name-and-what-does-it-mean-anyhow/)
- Version B, from Witt: lawyer Jim Gaither wrote "NV" for "**new venture**" as a placeholder at incorporation. Witt says this was a coincidence, because Priem and Malachowsky were already calling their chip NV1. [BOOK] — [Witt excerpt PDF](https://www.opsjournal.org/DocumentLibrary/Uploads/Jensen%20Huang_final.pdf)
- Version C: the name honors the first chip, NV1. [BOOK summary] — [Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04)
- The founders allegedly first wanted "NVision," but it was taken by a toilet-paper maker. [SECONDARY, single weak source; unverified] — [Business Plus](https://businessplus.ie/tech/nvidia-jensen-huang/)
- Pronunciation is "en-VID-ee-uh." [SECONDARY] — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/15/how-to-say-nvidias-name-and-what-does-it-mean-anyhow/)

### Inferences
- The safest script line is: "Three engineers. A Denny's booth. Bottomless coffee. 1993." All of the well-sourced accounts agree on that.
- "$40,000" looks like an internet fact. Use "a few hundred dollars of their own money, then $2 million from Sequoia and Sutter Hill" only with an "according to Tae Kim" attribution, or avoid the founders' own-money figure entirely.
- For the name, "NV" plus the Latin *invidia* (envy) is solid. Present "next version" vs "new venture" as a fun ambiguity, or just use Huang's own version.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for the $40,000 figure. I could not check Kim's $200 claim against the book text itself.
- The exact Denny's address and the exact dates of the meetings are not confirmed in primary sources (only "San Jose / East San Jose").

## 2. NV1 (1995), Sega, the DirectX shock, layoffs, and Irimajiri's $5 million

### Takeaway
NV1 (May 1995) was an all-in-one multimedia chip that drew 3D with curved or four-sided surfaces ("forward texture mapping" of quadratic patches) instead of triangles. When Microsoft's DirectX standardized on triangles, NV1 and the Sega console chip being built on the same design (NV2) were stranded. Huang told Sega that Nvidia could not deliver, asked to be let out of the contract while still being paid, and Sega's Shoichiro Irimajiri approved about $5M anyway. Staff were cut roughly in half or more. The exact size of the cut and the amount and form of Sega's payment vary by source.

### Cited Findings
**NV1 facts**
- The SEC prospectus states: NV1 was "introduced... in May 1995"; it "provided 3D graphics, video and audio," was "targeted primarily to the game console market," and was built "in the absence of industry standards with the goal of establishing the Company's proprietary NV technology as a 3D graphics standard." It also says: "By the end of 1996, the PC industry had broadly adopted Microsoft's Direct3D and SGI's OpenGL." Nvidia "stopped selling the NV1 in the first quarter of 1996" and "ceased development of the NV2, a product designed for a game console platform." [PRIMARY] — [IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt)
- Revenue was tiny: $1.18M in 1995 and $3.9M in 1996, all from NV1. Diamond Multimedia, which sold NV1 as the "Diamond Edge 3D" card, accounted for 86% and 82% of revenue in those years. Gross margin on product revenue was **-40% in 1995**, so Nvidia lost money on every chip. [PRIMARY] — [IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt)
- Huang on the technology: "NV1 and NV2 were based on forward texture mapping, no triangles but curves, and tessellated the curves... turns out to have been completely the wrong answer." [PRIMARY] — [Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)
- Kim, as summarized: NV1 used quadrilaterals instead of triangles and also included wavetable audio meant to replace the Sound Blaster card. It bet on memory staying expensive ($50/MB), but memory fell to about $5/MB. *Doom* ran poorly on it, and about **250,000 chips were returned**. Huang later called it a "Swiss Army knife" that nobody wanted to pay for. [BOOK] — [ursummary](https://ursummary.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-summary/); [Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04)
- NV1 powered the first PC version of Sega's *Virtua Fighter*. [SECONDARY] — [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/nvidias-jensen-huang-reunites-with-sega-exec-who-saved-the-company-with-5m-investment/)
- Huang, from a Sequoia interview: "This is 1995, Microsoft had come out with Windows 95, and the API called DirectX is the architecture that everybody else uses except us... the rest of the industry, now some 50 companies, are all over us." [PRIMARY] — [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/flashback-how-the-kindness-of-sega-saved-nvidia-from-going-under)
- Huang has also claimed that Nvidia's original API, "Direct NV," came before DirectX: "In 1993, we had Direct NV (Direct Nvidia), which in 1995 became DirectX." This is his own claim and is not independently verified. [PRIMARY, single-source] — [Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)

**The Sega dilemma and the ask**
- Huang: "I was confronted with a situation where we would finish the project and die, or not finish the project and die right away." [PRIMARY] — [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/flashback-how-the-kindness-of-sega-saved-nvidia-from-going-under)
- Huang described the meeting this way: "I told him our circumstance is that if we finish this game console for you, our company would be out of business... I would like him to let us off of our contract... but pay us in full... He thought about it for a couple days... and said, I'd like to help you... it was some 5 million dollars, I think it was... It was all the money that we had." [PRIMARY] — [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/features/flashback-how-the-kindness-of-sega-saved-nvidia-from-going-under)
- In a 2025 version, Huang says he told Irimajiri that "if they invested that money in us, it was likely to be lost." He also says Sega sold its stake once Nvidia went public ("because they thought it was a miracle") and that it would be worth "about a trillion dollars today." VGC's bracketed "[for $300m]" is an editorial insertion and has not been verified. [PRIMARY quote, figure uncertain] — [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/nvidias-jensen-huang-reunites-with-sega-exec-who-saved-the-company-with-5m-investment/)
- **Irimajiri's own account (2026):** He was then head of Sega's **U.S. arm** and told Nvidia the contract would be terminated. Huang said Nvidia would go bankrupt without it. Irimajiri proposed that Sega use the remaining development funds to **buy Nvidia shares** as "a parting gift," and he persuaded Sega to put in $5M. In his words: "I came to like Jensen Huang's character, and bet everything on him." [PRIMARY] — [Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/09/12/companies/sega-nvidia-investment/)
- Irimajiri to Inc.: Huang was "a genuinely honest, straightforward individual who never played games and never misrepresented what he believed." Sega confirmed to Inc. that it approved the $5M "around 1995 or 1996." It said precise records are no longer available and did not clarify whether the money was contract payments, equity, or both. [PRIMARY] — [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/georgia-fearn/jensen-huang-sega-shoichiro-irimajiri-nvidia-technology-failed-5-million/91376429)
- In September 2026, Huang reunited with Irimajiri, then 86, at Sega's Akihabara arcade in Tokyo. [SECONDARY] — [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/nvidias-jensen-huang-reunites-with-sega-exec-who-saved-the-company-with-5m-investment/); WSJ profile "[The 84-Year-Old Man Who Saved Nvidia](https://www.wsj.com/business/nvidia-stock-jensen-huang-sega-irimajiri-chips-ai-906247db)"
- **Conflict:** a summary of Kim's book says "Sega canceled the NV2 but paid **$1 million for a working prototype**." That may be a separate payment, but it conflicts with the $5M figure that Huang and Irimajiri both give. [BOOK] — [ursummary](https://ursummary.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-summary/)

**Layoffs and headcount (numbers conflict)**
- The New Yorker (2023), via Inc.: Huang "laid off half his team." [SECONDARY] — [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/nvidia-founder-jensen-huang-on-how-a-close-call-with-failure-helps-him-dominate-ai.html); [Business Insider](https://markets.businessinsider.com/news/stocks/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo-profile-ai-stock-market-nvda-price-2023-11) ("almost went out of business in 1996... lay off half of its staff... a few weeks away from missing payroll")
- Kim: layoffs of **60% of staff**, leaving about **40 employees**. [BOOK] — [ursummary](https://ursummary.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-summary/); [Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04)
- Witt: Huang cut headcount "from more than 100 to a skeleton roster of **35 engineers**." [BOOK] — [Inc. excerpt of The Thinking Machine](https://www.inc.com/stephen-witt/how-jensen-huang-kicked-off-a-mad-dash-to-save-nvidia-in-its-early-years/91173298)
- SEC anchor: Nvidia had **71 employees as of September 28, 1997**, after RIVA 128 began shipping, and 184 by October 1998. [PRIMARY] — [IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt)

**Vivid detail (Witt)**
- Consultant David Kirk, later Nvidia's chief scientist, arrived in 1996 and "could see that the company was doomed." He insisted on "a hand-delivered paper check at the end of each workweek" because he wasn't sure the company would last longer. The founders were openly shouting at each other over whether to keep Priem's NV2 architecture. [BOOK, single-source] — [Inc. excerpt](https://www.inc.com/stephen-witt/how-jensen-huang-kicked-off-a-mad-dash-to-save-nvidia-in-its-early-years/91173298)

### Inferences
- Sega/Dreamcast wording: many outlets say Nvidia was building "the Dreamcast" chip. More precisely, NV2 was for Sega's next console after the Saturn, which later became the Dreamcast; the Dreamcast eventually shipped with NEC/VideoLogic's PowerVR, based on general industry knowledge not verified in this session. NV1 itself was linked to the Saturn through game ports such as *Virtua Fighter*. Use "Sega's next console" to be safe.
- Irimajiri's title: Huang calls him "CEO," and Japan Times says he was then head of Sega's U.S. arm and later became president. Calling him "a top Sega executive (later its president)" avoids the problem.
- Sega's $5M is best framed as Sega honoring the money even though Nvidia walked away from the work. Whether it was technically a contract payment or a stock purchase is genuinely unclear; Huang and Irimajiri describe it differently.

### Gaps
- No primary document showing Sega's shareholding. Sega is not named among the >5% holders in the 1999 prospectus, so its stake was below 5% or already sold. The sale price and date are unverified.
- The exact layoff number and date (1996) are not in any SEC filing I found. Sources range from "half" to "60%" and from "more than 100 to 35."

## 3. RIVA 128 (1997): the emulator, the "one shot" tape-out, and sales

### Takeaway
With roughly six to nine months of cash, Nvidia designed RIVA 128 (NV3) to fully support Microsoft's Direct3D. It spent about $1M on a hardware emulator from IKOS, a company that was shutting down, so it could test the chip and all its software before any silicon existed. It then sent the design straight to production. RIVA 128 began shipping in August 1997 and passed 1 million units in about four months, which Nvidia announced on January 12, 1998.

### Cited Findings
- RIVA 128 began commercial shipment in **August 1997**. [PRIMARY] — [IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt). A Kim summary says it "launched in April 1997," which is probably the public unveiling, not shipping. — [ursummary](https://ursummary.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-summary/)
- Nvidia press release, **January 12, 1998**: "shipped over one million RIVA 128 3D graphics processor chips." Analyst Jon Peddie called Nvidia "the surprise of 1997" and estimated a **21% share** of the 6-million-unit performance segment "in only 4 months of shipments." Customers included Dell, Gateway, Micron, NEC, Diamond and STB. [PRIMARY] — [Nvidia press release (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20110326212747/http:/www.nvidia.com/object/IO_20020114_4455.html)
- The chip was fabbed by **SGS-Thomson** (0.35 micron), not TSMC. [PRIMARY] — [same press release](https://web.archive.org/web/20110326212747/http:/www.nvidia.com/object/IO_20020114_4455.html). The prospectus says Nvidia "in the past utilized ST and currently utilizes TSMC," and that TSMC had yield problems with the later RIVA128ZX and RIVA TNT. [PRIMARY] — [IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt). **This contradicts** popular retellings in which Huang called TSMC to manufacture RIVA 128. — e.g. [Medium](https://medium.com/@leo.hermansson/the-story-of-nvidia-how-a-few-crucial-decisions-created-a-5-trillion-giant-c788d222b51e)
- Financial turnaround: revenue went from $3.9M (1996) to **$29.1M (1997)**. Nvidia had its first profitable quarter in the quarter ended December 31, 1997. Revenue for the nine months to October 1998 was $92.7M. The accumulated deficit was about $17.1M. [PRIMARY] — [IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt)
- Huang on the emulator: "There was a company called ICOS [IKOS]. On the day that I called them, they were just shutting the company down because they had no customers. I said, hey, look. I'll buy what you have inventory." [PRIMARY] — [Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)
- Huang on why: if they made a test chip, found bugs and re-made it, "we would've been out of business already... if we only had six months and you get to tape out just one time, then obviously you're going to tape out a perfect chip." [PRIMARY] — [Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)
- The team "sat in the lab waiting for Windows to paint." Asked if it was 60 seconds a frame, Huang said: "Oh, easily. I actually think that it was an hour per frame." [PRIMARY; Huang's estimate] — [Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)
- Price of the emulator: **$1 million**, which reportedly cut payroll runway from nine months to six. [BOOK] — [Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04); the IMD business school also cites $1M per [immers.cloud](https://en.immers.cloud/faq/view/?id=195). Conflicting claim: "NVIDIA had $1 million in the bank. They used half of it." [SECONDARY, unreliable] — [Medium](https://medium.com/@leo.hermansson/the-story-of-nvidia-how-a-few-crucial-decisions-created-a-5-trillion-giant-c788d222b51e)
- Witt's scene: the emulator was too big for the computer lab, so staff "shoved aside the Ping-Pong table to make space for it in the break room." Dwight Diercks ran it by day and David Kirk by night. Witt writes, "no firm had ever skipped prototyping before." Treat that last line as an author's claim. [BOOK] — [Inc. excerpt](https://www.inc.com/stephen-witt/how-jensen-huang-kicked-off-a-mad-dash-to-save-nvidia-in-its-early-years/91173298)
- The flaw they shipped anyway: per the Acquired hosts, the chip supported only **8 of the 32** DirectX blend modes. Huang: "The other 24 weren't that important... We should have implemented all 32." [PRIMARY] — [Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)
- Design strategy: build "the biggest chip that anybody had ever imagined building," using the fastest memories, at a price point above what competitors would dare. "Basically, if we built that chip, there could be nothing that could be faster." [PRIMARY] — [Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)
- Other moves: Nvidia licensed a VGA core from rival Weitek and hired its designer Gopal Solanki. [BOOK] — [Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04)
- Huang called 1997 "probably Nvidia's best moment... our backs were up against the wall. We were running out of time, we're running out of money, and for a lot of employees, running out of hope." [PRIMARY] — [Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)
- By launch, Nvidia had about **one month of payroll** left. [SECONDARY, attributed to the New Yorker] — [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/nvidia-founder-jensen-huang-on-how-a-close-call-with-failure-helps-him-dominate-ai.html). Huang reportedly gave it a "50-50 chance." — [Business Insider](https://markets.businessinsider.com/news/stocks/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo-profile-ai-stock-market-nvda-price-2023-11)
- Aftermath, the "bluecoats": when TSMC defects ("titanium stringers") hit the RIVA 128ZX, Nvidia turned its office into a 24-hour manual testing line staffed by contract workers in blue coats. [BOOK, single-source] — [Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04)

### Inferences
- "1 million in 4 months" is well documented by Nvidia's own January 1998 press release and is safe for the script.
- Runway estimates differ by source: "six months" (Acquired hosts and Huang), "nine down to six" (Kim), and "one month of payroll at launch" (New Yorker). These can all be true at different moments, so the narration can walk through them in order.
- The emulator vendor is IKOS Systems. The Acquired transcript spells it "ICOS," a transcription artifact.

### Gaps
- No independent confirmation of the exact emulator price or that IKOS was literally shutting down. Huang's story is single-source. Secondary reporting notes IKOS listed Nvidia as an emulation user in April 1997, which suggests IKOS continued operating.
- I did not find the RIVA 128's launch price or the exact tape-out date.

## 4. "Our company is 30 days from going out of business": origin and meaning

### Takeaway
The line came from the real 1996–97 cash crisis, when Nvidia was weeks from missing payroll. Huang then opened staff presentations with it for years, as a deliberate way to keep people urgent. He still uses it decades later, saying the feeling never leaves him. It is a motto and a mindset, not a literal cash figure after 1997.

### Cited Findings
- The New Yorker (2023), as quoted: "Huang encouraged his employees to continue shipping products with a sense of desperation, and for years to come he opened staff presentations with the words 'Our company is thirty days from going out of business.'" [SECONDARY quoting the New Yorker] — [Business Insider](https://markets.businessinsider.com/news/stocks/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo-profile-ai-stock-market-nvda-price-2023-11)
- It is described as Nvidia's "unofficial motto." [SECONDARY] — [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/nvidia-founder-jensen-huang-on-how-a-close-call-with-failure-helps-him-dominate-ai.html)
- Kim: Huang "utilized fear and anxiety as motivational tools," frequently telling staff "We're thirty days from going out of business." [BOOK] — [Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04)
- Huang to GamesBeat (2020): "I literally feel, every single morning, that we're 30 days from going out of business. I wake up in a panic." [PRIMARY] — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-nvidia-ceo-motivates-himself-with-funny-paranoia/)
- Huang on Joe Rogan (December 2025): "The phrase '30 days from going out of business' I've used for 33 years. The feeling doesn't change." [PRIMARY] — [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-joe-rogan-2025-12)
- Witt quoting Huang: "I do everything I can not to go out of business. I do everything I can not to fail." [BOOK/PRIMARY quote] — [Penguin Random House excerpt](https://penguinrandomhousehighereducation.com/book/?isbn=9780593832707)

### Inferences
- "33 years" (2025 minus 33 = 1992 or 1993) suggests Huang himself dates the phrase to the founding era, not only to 1997. The two stories are broadly compatible.
- For viewers: it began as a literal truth in about 1996–97 and became a culture tool.

### Gaps
- No contemporaneous 1990s document, such as a memo or speech, captures the first use of the phrase.

## 5. RIVA TNT, GeForce 256 ("the world's first GPU"), and the January 1999 IPO

### Takeaway
RIVA TNT shipped in July 1998. Nvidia went public on January 22, 1999 at $12 a share, raising $42M, and the stock closed its first day about 64% higher. On August 31, 1999 it unveiled GeForce 256 and coined "GPU" for a single chip that did both the 3D geometry math (transform and lighting) and the drawing of pixels. Before that, the computer's main processor (CPU) did the geometry.

### Cited Findings
**RIVA TNT**
- RIVA128ZX shipped in March 1998 and RIVA TNT in July 1998. [PRIMARY] — [IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt)
- Huang in 1999: "In 1997, we created the first 128-bit 3D processor, the RIVA 128. In 1998, we delivered the first dual-pipe processor, the RIVA TNT." [PRIMARY] — [GeForce 256 press release](https://mmgik.org/images/almanach/agp/przypisy/NVIDIA%20LAUNCHES%20THE%20WORLDS%20FIRST%20GRAPHICS%20PROCESSING%20UNIT%20GEFORCE%20256.pdf)

**IPO**
- January 22, 1999: 3,500,000 shares at **$12.00**, for $42.0M gross and $39.06M to the company after underwriting. The underwriters were Morgan Stanley Dean Witter, Hambrecht & Quist and Prudential. The ticker was NVDA on Nasdaq. There would be 28,595,976 shares outstanding after the offering. [PRIMARY] — [SEC 424B4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt); [Nvidia IPO press release](https://web.archive.org/web/20190713074208/https:/www.nvidia.com/object/IO_20020108_6739.html)
- First-day close was **$19.6875** (+64%), "giving the company a market value of $626.1 million." [SECONDARY, contemporary] — [WSJ, Jan 25, 1999 (Dean Takahashi)](https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB917224371262043000)
- **Conflicting numbers to avoid:** one blog gives a "$73.5 million market capitalization" and a $21 close, which are wrong. — [Silicon Underground](https://dfarq.homeip.net/nvidias-ipo-on-january-22-1999/). A Kim summary says the IPO was "at $19.69," which confuses the closing price with the offer price. — [ursummary](https://ursummary.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-summary/)

**GeForce 256**
- Unveiled **August 31, 1999** in Palm Springs as "the world's first graphics processing unit (GPU)." [PRIMARY] — [press release](https://mmgik.org/images/almanach/agp/przypisy/NVIDIA%20LAUNCHES%20THE%20WORLDS%20FIRST%20GRAPHICS%20PROCESSING%20UNIT%20GEFORCE%20256.pdf). Retail release was October 11, 1999. [SECONDARY] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeForce_256)
- Nvidia's definition of a GPU: "a single-chip processor with integrated transform, lighting, triangle setup/clipping, and rendering engines that is capable of processing a minimum of 10 million polygons per second." [PRIMARY] — [Nvidia GeForce 256 page (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20060324123500/http:/www.nvidia.com/page/geforce256.html)
- Specs: about 23 million transistors, "more than twice the complexity of the Pentium III"; 15M polygons/s and 480M pixels/s; "50 Gigaflops... equivalent to... a 256-processor Cray T3D." [PRIMARY, marketing claims] — [press release](https://mmgik.org/images/almanach/agp/przypisy/NVIDIA%20LAUNCHES%20THE%20WORLDS%20FIRST%20GRAPHICS%20PROCESSING%20UNIT%20GEFORCE%20256.pdf)
- 3D cards existed before; Nvidia was the first to coin and own the term "GPU." ATI later tried "VPU," without success. [SECONDARY] — [DVHardware](https://www.dvhardware.net/news/65086/nvidia-celebrates-17-years-of-gpus)

### Inferences
- Plain-English version: earlier cards could paint the picture, but the PC's main processor still had to work out where every corner of every 3D shape went and how light hit it. GeForce 256 moved that math onto the graphics chip too. "GPU" was partly real engineering and partly brilliant branding, since Nvidia wrote a definition that only its own chip met.

### Gaps
- I did not find GeForce 256's launch price or first-year unit sales.

## 6. The 3dfx rivalry and asset purchase, the Xbox deal, and the six-month product cycle

### Takeaway
3dfx (Voodoo) was the 3D king of the late 1990s. It slipped on product schedules, bought board-maker STB in a move that alienated its partners, and collapsed. Nvidia agreed on December 15, 2000 to buy its graphics assets for $70M in cash plus 1M shares, and the deal closed April 18, 2001. In March 2000 Nvidia won the Microsoft Xbox graphics contract with a $200M advance. Behind both wins was Nvidia's "three teams, two seasons" structure, which produced a new chip about every six months.

### Cited Findings
**3dfx**
- The IPO prospectus already flagged the threat: "3Dfx's pending acquisition of STB" (STB was 63% of Nvidia's 1997 revenue) "will be reduced significantly." [PRIMARY] — [IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287099000192/0001012870-99-000192.txt)
- December 15, 2000: Nvidia agreed to acquire 3dfx graphics assets (patents, trademarks, brand names and chip inventory) for "$70 million in cash and 1 million shares." Pending patent suits between the two were to be dismissed. [PRIMARY] — [Nvidia press release (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190330171444/http:/www.nvidia.com/object/IO_20010612_6602.html)
- 3dfx's own release valued the deal at **$112M** ($70M cash plus 1M shares at Nvidia's December 14, 2000 close). The "3dfx" and "Voodoo" brands transferred at signing. [PRIMARY] — [3dfx press release (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20010205193000/http:/3dfx.com/rel-15dec_2.htm)
- Closed **April 18, 2001**: $55M net cash ($70M minus a $15M bridge loan). The 1M shares were contingent on 3dfx dissolving. The patent litigation was dismissed April 26, 2001. [PRIMARY] — [Nvidia 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000101287001500581/d8k.txt); [3dfx 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1010026/000089161801500803/f72487e10-k.txt)
- 3dfx's former marketing executive Ross Smith later said the company "wanted perfection in every product that we shipped." Voodoo3 slipped to April 1999, and its successors (Napalm, Rampage) slipped further. [BOOK] — [Américo Dias on The Nvidia Way](https://americo.dias.pt/posts/nvidia-way-lessons/)

**Xbox**
- March 5, 2000: Nvidia entered an agreement with Microsoft to develop and sell Xbox processors. In April 2000 Microsoft paid a **$200M advance** against processor purchases. The Xbox GPU, made by TSMC at 0.15 micron, began commercial shipment in **July 2001**. The advance was fully used by April 2002. [PRIMARY] — [Nvidia 8-K, April 2002](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000095016802001118/d8k.htm); [EE Times, April 19, 2000](https://www.eetimes.com/nvidia-receives-200-million-advance-payment-from-microsoft/)
- If the deal fell through, Nvidia would have had to return half the cash plus shares. [SECONDARY, contemporary] — [The Register, March 16, 2000](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2000/03/16/nvidia-gets-200m-x-box-injection/790645)
- Kim: Huang and sales director Chris Diskin snatched the deal from startup **Gigapixel** "over a weekend," about a week before Bill Gates's announcement. [BOOK, single-source] — [ursummary](https://ursummary.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-summary/); [Joey Zhou notes](https://joeyzhou.com/writings/2026-01-04)
- Postscript beyond 2002: Microsoft took Nvidia to binding arbitration over Xbox chip pricing in April 2002. [PRIMARY] — [Nvidia 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000095016802001118/d8k.htm)

**Six-month cycle**
- Kim, page 94: Huang asked "why is it that in the PC graphics industry, one company can never hold a lead for more than two years?" PC makers refreshed twice a year, but "a new chip design took eighteen months." [BOOK] — [Askeladden](https://askeladdencapital.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-review-notes-and-analysis/)
- Tae Kim, in his own words: PC makers picked vendors in two seasons, "back to school and in the spring." Nvidia made "one 18-month architecture and then created two faster derivatives every six months." [BOOK author interview] — [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2025/01/29/interview-tae-kim-author-of-the-nvidia-way/)
- The structure was "Three Teams, Two Seasons," and it was enabled by Priem's "resource manager" software layer, which could emulate hardware features in software. [BOOK] — [Askeladden](https://askeladdencapital.com/the-nvidia-way-by-tae-kim-book-review-notes-and-analysis/)
- Huang's line about rivals: "The competition will always be shooting behind the duck." [BOOK] — [Américo Dias](https://americo.dias.pt/posts/nvidia-way-lessons/)

### Inferences
- The 3dfx story makes a clean narrative contrast: perfectionism versus cadence. Note that the "bankruptcy" wording is loose. 3dfx sold its assets and dissolved; at signing it was a wind-down, not a court bankruptcy filing. 3dfx later did enter bankruptcy proceedings (general knowledge, not verified here).
- Script framing: the $200M Xbox advance was more money than Nvidia had raised in its entire IPO ($42M).

### Gaps
- I found no primary source on how many 3dfx engineers joined Nvidia.
- No primary documentation of the Gigapixel "weekend" anecdote.

## 7. How many 3D graphics chip companies existed, and how many survived

### Takeaway
There is no hard count. Huang himself has given several figures over the years: about 30, about 50, 60, and about 100. These are rhetorical estimates. The consistent message is "dozens, and Nvidia is the only standalone one left," and it should be presented as Huang's claim.

### Cited Findings
- "Thirty competitors have already shown up" (around 1996). [PRIMARY] — [Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang)
- "The rest of the industry, now some 50 companies" (around 1995). [PRIMARY] — [Time Extension, Sequoia interview](https://www.timeextension.com/features/flashback-how-the-kindness-of-sega-saved-nvidia-from-going-under)
- "When Nvidia first started, there were 60 3D graphics companies... Nvidia would be at the top of that list not to make it." [PRIMARY, via Business Insider] — [Business Insider (LinkedIn post)](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/businessinsider_jensen-huang-explains-why-nvidia-invests-activity-7450414513914331136-hqsK)
- "We were one of a hundred computer graphics companies, and also the last one founded... the largest in the world today and the only one remaining." [PRIMARY] — [Berkeley Haas Dean's Speaker Series (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9hzVdV63scU)
- Witt: "at least thirty five competitors were trying to build one" (in 1993). [BOOK] — [Witt excerpt PDF](https://www.opsjournal.org/DocumentLibrary/Uploads/Jensen%20Huang_final.pdf)
- A secondary analyst essay says "nearly 100... within a few months" and "NINETY graphics companies." [SECONDARY] — [Generative Value](https://www.generativevalue.com/p/nvidia-past-present-and-future). A 2011 talk summary says "about 200," which is a low-reliability AI-generated summary. — [bibigpt](https://bibigpt.co/summary/entrepreneurship/jensen-huang-2011-starting-and-growing-nvidia)
- Huang also contradicts himself on whether Nvidia was "the first" consumer 3D company (Acquired) or "the last one founded" (Berkeley). [PRIMARY] — [Acquired](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/jensen-huang); [Berkeley Haas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9hzVdV63scU)

### Inferences
- A safe script line: "By Jensen Huang's own count, somewhere between 30 and 100 companies were racing to build 3D graphics chips. Nvidia is the only one still standing as an independent graphics chip company." ATI survives inside AMD, so "standalone" or "independent" is the key qualifier.
- "70 to 90" as a specific range did not appear in any source I found.

### Gaps
- No independent industry census, for example from Jon Peddie Research, was found.
