# Jensen Huang: Life Story and Nvidia's Company Culture

Research date: November 20, 2026.

**CRITICAL NEW FACT FOR THE SCRIPT:** Jensen Huang is **no longer Nvidia's CEO**. On November 11, 2026, Nvidia's board accepted his resignation as President, CEO and board member, effective immediately. This came after news reports of extramarital affairs, one of them reportedly with a businesswoman whom US intelligence officials suspect of working for Chinese intelligence. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. Any "he has run Nvidia for 30+ years and still does" framing is now wrong. Details are in the "Long-term thinking, 33-year tenure, and the November 2026 resignation" section below.

Labels used for how well each story is documented:
- **[Documented]** means independent sources, records, or third-party witnesses back it up.
- **[Partly corroborated]** means the basic facts are backed up, but the vivid details come from Jensen.
- **[Jensen only]** means it is known only because Jensen has told it.

## Childhood: Taiwan, Thailand, Oneida Baptist Institute (Kentucky), Oregon

### Takeaway
The basic story is well documented: he was born in Taiwan in 1963, lived in Thailand, was sent to the US around age 9 with his older brother, ended up at a Kentucky reform school by mistake, and later joined his parents in Oregon. The school's leaders and a childhood friend (Ben Bays) back up the rough environment and the bullying. The most emotional details, such as the knife-scarred roommate he taught to read, the toilets, and the 100 push-ups, come mainly from Jensen's own retelling, and the details shift a little between tellings.

### Cited Findings
**Birth and early years [Documented]**
- Born Jen-Hsun Huang on February 17, 1963, in Taipei, Taiwan. He moved to Tainan as a child. His father, Huang Hsing-tai, was a chemical engineer at an oil refinery, and his mother, Lo Tsai-hsiu, was a schoolteacher. The family's native language was Taiwanese Hokkien. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang)
- Relatable detail: his mother picked 10 random English words from the dictionary each day to teach her sons English. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang) (secondary source; the original citation was not checked)
- At about age 5 the family moved to Thailand for his father's refinery job and stayed about four years. He attended Ruamrudee International School in Bangkok. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang)
- Jensen on why they were sent away: "In 1973, there was social unrest and my parents decided that it was probably safer for the kids to go to the United States and then for them to follow." — [NPR](https://thin.npr.org/147162496)
- His father had trained in New York in the late 1960s with an air-conditioning company and resolved to send his sons to the US. His parents reportedly sold nearly all their possessions to afford it. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang) (the "sold nearly everything" detail is secondary and its original source was not checked)

**Sent to the US and to Oneida by mistake [Documented]**
- At 9, he and his older brother (age 10) were sent to the US as unaccompanied minors, to live with an uncle in Tacoma, Washington. The uncle believed Oneida Baptist Institute was a prestigious boarding school, but it was a religious reform academy. — [The New Yorker, Stephen Witt, Nov 2023](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution); [Wired, 2002](https://www.wired.com/2002/07/nvidia/)
- On the Joe Rogan show (Dec 2025), Jensen said his parents contacted an uncle they had never visited and asked him to find a US school that would take two foreign boys with almost no savings. — [Fortune, Dec 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/12/05/jensen-huang-nine-year-old-janitor-before-nvidia-joe-rogan-podcast/) **[Jensen only]**
- Independent confirmation of the school's reputation: former student and later Oneida president Bud Underwood told NPR that "many local folks thought of Oneida as a religious reform school" in the 1970s, and that county schools sent expelled children there. "We wouldn't take some of the students that we took back in the '70s and '80s." — [NPR](https://thin.npr.org/147162496)
- The current president, Larry Gritton, whose parents taught at the school back then, said then-president Dr. Barkley Moore "could have turned the boys away, but didn't, even though they were so young." — [Kentucky Lantern, Nov 9, 2026](https://kentuckylantern.com/2026/11/09/what-do-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-and-clay-countys-oneida-baptist-institute-have-in-common/)
- Clay County, Kentucky, was "one of the poorest counties in the country then and now." — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/05/jensen-huang-nine-year-old-janitor-before-nvidia-joe-rogan-podcast/)
- He was too young for the academy's classes, so he lived in the dorm but attended nearby Oneida Elementary. — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution); [Kentucky Lantern](https://kentuckylantern.com/2026/11/09/what-do-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-and-clay-countys-oneida-baptist-institute-have-in-common/)

**The roommate and the toilets [Partly corroborated: the work duty is documented, the roommate details come from Jensen]**
- Every student had a job. Jensen was assigned to clean the bathrooms. — [NPR](https://thin.npr.org/147162496). Wired (2002) says he scrubbed "all the toilets in his three-story dorm every day." — [Wired](https://www.wired.com/2002/07/nvidia/)
- On Rogan he said he was the youngest boy on campus and cleaned the bathrooms for about a hundred teenage boys, joking, "I just wished they would be a bit more careful." His brother was sent to work in the school's tobacco fields ("kind of like a penitentiary"). He said he tried smoking for a week at age 9. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/05/jensen-huang-nine-year-old-janitor-before-nvidia-joe-rogan-podcast/) **[Jensen only]**
- New Yorker version: his 17-year-old roommate, on their first night, "lifted his shirt to show Huang the numerous places where he'd been stabbed in fights." Jensen: "Every student smoked, and I think I was the only boy at the school without a pocketknife." The roommate was illiterate. In exchange for reading lessons, "he taught me how to bench-press. I ended up doing a hundred pushups every night before bed." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution) **[Jensen only]**
- The details vary between tellings. Wired (2002) describes the roommate as "covered in tattoos and knife scars." On Rogan (2025) the roommate was "wrapped in tape from a recent fight" and "the toughest kid in school." NPR (2012) says Jensen "helped an older dorm-mate in math" and does not mention reading. — [Wired](https://www.wired.com/2002/07/nvidia/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/05/jensen-huang-nine-year-old-janitor-before-nvidia-joe-rogan-podcast/); [NPR](https://thin.npr.org/147162496)
- Jensen to NPR: "The kids were really tough. They all had pocket knives — and when they get in fights, it's not pretty. Kids get hurt." Also: "The ending of the story is I loved the time I was there." — [NPR](https://thin.npr.org/147162496)

**Bullying and the swinging bridge [Documented, with an independent witness]**
- His childhood friend Ben Bays remembered the principal introducing "an undersized Asian immigrant with long hair and heavily accented English. ... He was a perfect target." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- Jensen, "with no apparent emotion": "The way you described Chinese people back then was 'Chinks.' We were called that every day." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- Local boys would grab the ropes of a high, rickety swinging footbridge (old planks, many missing) to try to throw him off. Bays: "Somehow it never seemed to affect him. ... He just shook it off." By the end of the year Jensen was leading those same kids on adventures in the woods. "Actually, it looked like he was having fun." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- Jensen on resilience: "Back then, there wasn't a counsellor to talk to. Back then, you just had to toughen up and move on." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- Other small details: he could pick apples out of his dorm window, and he joined the swim team. — [NPR](https://thin.npr.org/147162496)
- Jensen in 2002, looking at the school's website: "Wow. There it is. I haven't been back since. ... I remember that part of my life more vividly than just about any other." — [Wired](https://www.wired.com/2002/07/nvidia/)

**Giving back to Oneida [Documented]**
- In 2019 he donated $2 million for a girls' dorm and classroom building, Jen-Hsun Huang Hall, and attended its dedication. — [Kentucky Lantern](https://kentuckylantern.com/2026/11/09/what-do-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-and-clay-countys-oneida-baptist-institute-have-in-common/). In his 2019 talk there he spoke fondly of the footbridge, "neglecting to mention the bullies who had tried to toss him off it." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)

**Oregon and table tennis [Documented]**
- After about two years his parents reached the US and settled in Beaverton, Oregon, and the brothers joined them. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang); [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- At Aloha High School he skipped two grades, graduated at 16, was a nationally ranked table-tennis player, and belonged to the math, computer and science clubs. "I did not have a girlfriend," he said. — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- He learned table tennis "with the help of the guy who came to fill the vending machines," rose "to the rank of master," and appeared in Sports Illustrated by age 14. — [Wired](https://www.wired.com/2002/07/nvidia/)
- In 1977 his school bought an Apple II. He used it to play Super Star Trek and wrote his own version of Snake in BASIC. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang)

### Inferences
- The strongest documentary-ready scene is the swinging bridge, because a third party (Ben Bays) describes it on the record. The roommate and bench-press story is beloved but rests on Jensen's own retelling, and its details have shifted (tattoos, then knife scars, then tape). Narration should say "Jensen recalls..." for that one.
- Jensen tends to tell Oneida as a warm, funny story ("I loved the time I was there"), while others describe real hardship, racial abuse and danger. That gap is itself a storytelling beat: his habit of turning suffering into fondness.

### Gaps
- Exact year of arrival: 1973 per [NPR](https://thin.npr.org/147162496) and Wikipedia, 1972 per [Kentucky Lantern](https://kentuckylantern.com/2026/11/09/what-do-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-and-clay-countys-oneida-baptist-institute-have-in-common/). His age is usually given as 9; the [Telegraph review](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/books/non-fiction/review-the-thinking-machine-stephen-witt/) says 10.
- The roommate has never been identified or interviewed, so the reading lessons cannot be independently checked.
- I did not find the original Sports Illustrated item or his exact national table-tennis ranking or event.

## Denny's, Oregon State and Lori, Stanford, AMD and LSI Logic, and founding Nvidia at Denny's

### Takeaway
His job at Denny's (dishwasher, then busboy, then waiter), his Oregon State engineering degree (1984), meeting his wife Lori as a lab partner, a Stanford master's earned at night (1992), and jobs at AMD and LSI Logic are all well documented. The 1993 founding at a San Jose Denny's is real and Denny's itself has celebrated it. The romantic "do you want to see my homework" line is Jensen's own joke.

### Cited Findings
- His first job, at 15, was at a Denny's in Oregon as a dishwasher, busboy and waiter (graveyard shift), from about 1978 to 1983. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang), which cites Fortune, Mar 12, 2024, "How Nvidia's billionaire CEO went from Denny's dishwasher..." **[Documented]**
- To a waitress at the Denny's where Nvidia started (2023): "You know, I used to be a dishwasher here. But I worked hard! Like, really hard. So I got to be a busboy." He ordered seven items, then tipped the waitress $1,000. — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution) **[Documented: witnessed by the reporter]**
- On pressure: "I find that I think best when I'm under adversity. My heart rate actually goes down. Anyone who's dealt with rush hour in a restaurant knows what I'm talking about." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- He chose Oregon State for its low tuition and earned a BS in electrical engineering in 1984 with highest honors. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang) **[Documented]**
- Lori Mills was his lab partner in introductory engineering. "There were, like, two hundred and fifty kids in electrical engineering, and maybe three girls." "I was the youngest kid in the class. I looked like I was about twelve." He called her every weekend to do homework: "I tried to impress her—not with my looks, of course, but with my strong capability to complete homework." After six months of homework he asked her on a date. — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution) **[Jensen only for the romantic details]**
- His pickup line, as he tells it: "I asked her if she wanted to see my homework." — [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-leather-jacket-wife-daughter-style-2023-10)
- They married in 1985. Both worked as chip designers in Silicon Valley ("She actually made more than me"). Lori later left the workforce to raise their two children, Spencer and Madison. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang); [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution). Jensen has said "Lori did ninety percent of the parenting." — [The Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2025/05/thinking-machine-jensen-huang-nvidia-book-review/682122/)
- He designed microprocessors at AMD from 1984 to 1985, then moved to LSI Logic. There he worked with Sun Microsystems engineers Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem on Sun's GX graphics engine, rose to director of LSI's CoreWare division, and stayed until 1993. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang) **[Documented]**
- While working, he took Stanford classes at night and earned an MS in electrical engineering in 1992. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang); [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- He founded Nvidia in 1993, at age 30, with Malachowsky and Priem. They drafted the paperwork at a Denny's in San Jose, which he chose because it was "quieter than home and had cheap coffee." Although he was younger, both co-founders felt he was ready to be CEO. — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution). Priem has recalled saying "Jensen, you're the CEO, right? Done." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang)
- The name: the company was first called "NVision" until they learned a toilet-paper maker had that name. Jensen suggested "Nvidia," riffing on the Latin *invidia* ("envy"), because Priem wanted rivals "green with envy." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- Philanthropy toward his alma mater: in 2022 he and Lori gave $50 million to Oregon State for a research center with a supercomputer. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang), citing OSU Newsroom

### Inferences
- The thread from dishwasher at Denny's to founding Nvidia at Denny's to Denny's honoring him with a plaque in 2023 is a ready-made story loop, and it is documented.

### Gaps
- I did not independently verify the exact dates of his Denny's job (1978–1983) beyond Wikipedia's citation of Fortune.

## His famous quotes on pain, suffering, and "30 days from going out of business"

### Takeaway
All three signature quotes are verified with exact wording. One correction is needed: the "ample doses of pain and suffering" line came from the **Stanford SIEPR Economic Summit (March 1, 2024)**, not the GSB "View From The Top" series. Even Stripe's Patrick Collison mixed the two up on stage.

### Cited Findings
- **Stanford SIEPR, March 2024 (exact):** "People with very high expectations have very low resilience. And, unfortunately, resilience matters in success. I don't know how to teach it to you except for I hope suffering happens to you." ... "Greatness is not intelligence. Greatness comes from character. And character isn't formed out of smart people, it's formed out of people who suffered." ... "For all of you Stanford students, I wish upon you ample doses of pain and suffering." — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2024/03/15/nvidia-ceo-huang-at-stanford-pain-and-suffering-breeds-success.html); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/local/san-francisco/2024/03/18/quote-du-jour-nvidia-s-ceo-wishes-suffering-on-you); [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vOvQSqY7Jgc)
- Same talk: "One of my great advantages is that I have very low expectations." And: "To this day I use the phrase 'pain and suffering' inside our company with great glee. ... I mean that in a happy way, because you want to refine the character of your company." — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/03/13/nvidia-founder-ceo-jensen_huang-stanford-students-genz-grads-low-expectations-successful-pain-career-advice/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2024/03/15/nvidia-ceo-huang-at-stanford-pain-and-suffering-breeds-success.html)
- The event and date: SIEPR Economic Summit, March 1, 2024, moderated by Stanford economist John Shoven. — [Economic Times, citing Stanford SIEPR](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/jensen-huangs-wife-and-daughter-dress-him-who-really-picks-the-nvidia-ceos-famous-leather-jacket/articleshow/135660430.cms). Collison at Stripe: "a remark you made at a Stanford event recently. I was thinking of GSB, I think." — [Stripe Sessions 2024 transcript](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang)
- Expanding on it at Stripe Sessions 2024: "There are no such things that are great that were easy to do. So by definition, I would say therefore I wish upon you greatness, which by my way of saying it, I wish upon you plenty of pain and suffering." — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang)
- **Acquired podcast, October 2023 (exact):** asked what company he would start if he were 30 again in 2023, he answered: "I wouldn't do it." Then: "Building a company and building Nvidia turned out to have been a million times harder than I expected it to be, any of us expected it to be. ... If we realized the pain and suffering, just how vulnerable you're going to feel, and the challenges that you're going to endure, the embarrassment and the shame, and the list of all the things that go wrong, I don't think anybody would start a company. Nobody in their right mind would do it." And: "That's the superpower of an entrepreneur. They don't know how hard it is, and they only ask themselves how hard can it be? To this day, I trick my brain into thinking, how hard can it be?" — [Fortune, Oct 23, 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/10/23/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-tech-startup-entrepreneur/); [Hacker News transcript excerpt](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37952922)
- Note that the user's paraphrase ("if I had known how hard it would be, I would not have started Nvidia") is a fair summary, but it is **not** his exact words. Use "I wouldn't do it" and "a million times harder."
- Reporters noted visible emotion: "You can hear the emotion in his voice and see tinges of pain in his face." — [HotHardware](https://hothardware.com/news/nvidia-ceo-says-he-would-not-have-started-nvidia). Right after, he credited his support system: "I'm surrounded by these people that never one time gave up, and they never one time gave up on me." — [video clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URgncvVxxFU)
- **"30 days from going out of business":** after the near-fatal NV1 failure, he laid off more than half of roughly 100 staff in 1996 and bet the remaining money on the RIVA 128. "It was fifty-fifty, but we were going out of business anyway." When the chip shipped, Nvidia had enough money for only one month of payroll. "For years to come he opened staff presentations with the words 'Our company is thirty days from going out of business.' The phrase remains the unofficial corporate motto." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution) **[Documented]**
- Witt's book says headcount was cut from more than 100 to "a skeleton roster of 35 engineers." A consultant who joined in 1996, David Kirk, insisted on a hand-delivered paper check every week because he wasn't sure the company would last. — [Inc. excerpt from The Thinking Machine](https://www.inc.com/stephen-witt/how-jensen-huang-kicked-off-a-mad-dash-to-save-nvidia-in-its-early-years/91173298). (The New Yorker says "more than half" were laid off, which roughly fits.)
- **Joe Rogan #2422 (December 3, 2025):** "The phrase '30 days from going out of business' I've used for 33 years. The feeling doesn't change. The sense of vulnerability, the sense of uncertainty, the sense of insecurity — it doesn't leave you." Also: "I have a greater drive from not wanting to fail than the drive of wanting to succeed," and he is "always in a state of anxiety." — [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-joe-rogan-2025-12); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/04/nvidia-ceo-admits-he-works-7-days-a-week-including-holidays-in-a-constant-state-of-anxiety-out-of-fear-of-going-bankrupt/)
- On working every day: "Every day. Every single day. Not one day missed. Including Thanksgiving, Christmas." — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/04/nvidia-ceo-admits-he-works-7-days-a-week-including-holidays-in-a-constant-state-of-anxiety-out-of-fear-of-going-bankrupt/)
- Rogan story [Jensen only]: Nvidia spent half of its remaining roughly $1 million on a chip emulator from a company that had already gone out of business, buying the one machine left in its inventory. They then went straight to production at TSMC without a physical test chip. Sega's CEO Shoichiro Irimajiri also gave a $5 million lifeline after Nvidia couldn't fulfill its Sega contract. — [Rosetta transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/joerogan/joe-rogan-experience-2422-jensen-huang); [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-joe-rogan-2025-12)
- On fear at breakfast in 2023: "I do everything I can not to go out of business. I do everything I can not to fail." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- Another line: "I should make sure that I'm sufficiently exhausted from working that no one can keep me up at night." — [The Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2025/05/thinking-machine-jensen-huang-nvidia-book-review/682122/)

### Inferences
- "Pain and suffering" is more than a commencement line. He says he uses the phrase inside Nvidia, and it ties back to Oneida, the 1996 near-death of the company, and the "30 days" motto. That gives the documentary one emotional throughline.

### Gaps
- I did not verify what he said at the actual Stanford GSB "View From The Top" (2024), or anything from the 60 Minutes (2024) interview. I would not quote either without checking the transcripts.

## Management style: 60 direct reports, no one-on-ones, no five-year plans, "Top 5" emails, public feedback, "speed of light," intellectual honesty

### Takeaway
Each of these practices is confirmed, either in Jensen's own on-stage words (Stripe 2024, DealBook 2023) or in reporting (The New Yorker, Tae Kim). Turnover is documented as unusually low (2.5% in fiscal 2025, according to Nvidia's own report), and stock grants have made many employees rich. Bloomberg reports the work remains grueling.

### Cited Findings
- **60 direct reports, no one-on-ones (exact, Stripe Sessions 2024):** "Nvidia's leadership team is 60 people." Asked if they all report to him: "Yeah, they all report to me." He called it "not conventional, but I am certain it's the best practice." "I don't do one-on-ones ... Almost everything that I say, I say to everybody all at the same time." He said 60 direct reports remove "something like 7" layers of management. — [Stripe Sessions transcript](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang)
- At DealBook 2023: "The more direct reports the CEO has, the less layers are in the company. It allows us to keep information fluid." — [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/nvidia-jensen-huang-management-leadership-advice-how-to-run-business-2024-6)
- Fortune put it bluntly: "Huang has 60 direct reports and is known ... for 'reaching down into different layers of the organization' (or, to put it less delicately, micromanaging)." — [Fortune, Nov 2024](https://fortune.com/2024/11/12/jensen-huang-nvidia-ceo-leadership-mpp/)
- **Public feedback (exact, Stripe 2024):** "I give you feedback right there in front of everybody. ... Feedback is learning. For what reason are you the only person who should learn this?" — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang). Also: "Half the time I'm not right, but for me to reason through it in front of everybody helps everybody learn how to reason through it." — [Startup Archive, Stripe clip](https://www.startuparchive.org/p/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-i-really-discourage-1-on-1s)
- **"Torture them into greatness" (exact, Stripe 2024):** "I don't like giving up on people, because I think they could improve. So ... it's kind of tongue in cheek, but people know that I'd rather torture them into greatness." And: "I'd rather torture you into greatness because I believe in you." — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang)
- **"Top 5 things" emails:** employees regularly send a list of the five most important things they are working on or have noticed. These go to their team, their manager, and distribution lists Jensen reads. He reads about 100 or more a day, often on weekends and Sunday evenings, to get unfiltered news. Kim says the practice dates back to Nvidia's early years. — [Tae Kim interview, Asianometry](https://www.asianometry.com/p/nvidias-unique-history-and-culture); [Tae Kim Substack](https://taekim.substack.com/p/nvidias-unique-culture). The New Yorker describes weekly lists that Jensen "surveys ... late into the night." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- Jensen's reasoning: "It's easy to pick up the strong signals, but I want to intercept them when they're weak." — [Tae Kim Substack](https://taekim.substack.com/p/nvidias-unique-culture)
- Conflicting detail: the Telegraph review of Witt's book says the weekly emails have been required "since 2020," while Kim says they go back to the early days. — [Telegraph](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/books/non-fiction/review-the-thinking-machine-stephen-witt/) vs. [Asianometry](https://www.asianometry.com/p/nvidias-unique-history-and-culture)
- **Short emails:** "Huang communicates to his staff by writing hundreds of e-mails per day, often only a few words long. One executive compared the e-mails to haiku, another to ransom notes." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- **No fixed long-term plans:** "Strategy is not words. Strategy is actions. We don't do a periodic planning system. ... the world is a living, breathing organism. We just plan continuously. There's no five-year plan." — quoted in [Founders podcast notes on Tae Kim's book](https://podbrain.app/notes/founders-podcast/403-how-jensen-works) (secondary; I did not find the original interview)
- **"Speed of light":** employees should picture the absolute fastest a task could conceivably be done, physically, then work backward to an achievable goal. A related idea is the "zero-billion-dollar market," meaning products like CUDA that have no competitors and no obvious customers yet. — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- **Intellectual honesty and "No one loses alone":** "Whether the news is good or bad, you must be honest. If someone hides something and it blows up six months later, that's considered the worst thing you can do." There is also a saying, "No one loses alone": if you are falling behind, tell your team and they will help. — [Tae Kim, Asianometry](https://www.asianometry.com/p/nvidias-unique-history-and-culture)
- **"Failure must be shared":** in the early 2000s Nvidia shipped a card with a loud, overactive fan (the New Yorker doesn't name it). Instead of firing the product managers, Jensen had them present every decision behind the fiasco to a few hundred people. Nvidia also released a satirical video showing the card as a leaf blower. — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- **Flat, functional structure:** "no fixed divisions or hierarchy." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution). Nvidia is "functionally organized," which means less internal politics. — [Tae Kim](https://taekim.substack.com/p/nvidias-unique-culture)
- **The parking-lot test:** when he pivoted Nvidia to AI, he told researcher Bryan Catanzaro to "imagine he'd marched all eight thousand of Nvidia's employees into the parking lot" and pick anyone he wanted. An executive recalled: "He sent out an e-mail on Friday evening saying everything is going to deep learning ... By Monday morning, we were an A.I. company." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- **Low turnover [Documented, company-reported]:** overall turnover was 2.5% in fiscal 2025 (versus 2.7% in FY2024 and 5.3% in FY2023), against a semiconductor industry average of 16.4%. One in five employees has been there 10+ years, out of about 36,000 staff. — [Nvidia Sustainability Report FY2025](https://images.nvidia.com/aem-dam/Solutions/documents/NVIDIA-Sustainability-Report-Fiscal-Year-2025.pdf); [Nvidia 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581025000023/nvda-20250126.htm)
- **Rich employees and "semi-retirement":** at a 2023 all-hands, Jensen took a question about long-tenured, stock-rich employees in "semi-retirement" mode. He called working at Nvidia a "voluntary sport" and said each person should be "CEO" of their own time. — [Business Insider, Dec 2023](https://www.businessinsider.com/nvidia-employees-rich-happy-problem-insiders-say-2023-12). Jeff Fisher, an early employee: "Many of us are financial volunteers at this point, but we believe in the mission." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- Bloomberg: Nvidia stock rose 3,776% from the start of 2019, "minting many new multimillionaires," but work hours remain "grueling and high-stress," according to 10 current and former employees who spoke anonymously. — [Bloomberg, Aug 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-08-26/what-s-it-like-working-at-nvidia-you-may-get-rich-but-work-remains-stressful)
- Jensen on tenure: "I don't like working with new people ... I've encoded, I've embodied, I've deposited so much pain, suffering, joy, knowledge ... in all the people that you've worked with. ... That's really the reason why I really deeply believe in tenure." — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang)
- Self-deprecation (contrasted with colleagues): "I only have one superpower—homework." (Head of software Dwight Diercks: "He can master any subject over a weekend.") "I'm quite introverted." (Friend Ben Bays: "He's a great entertainer.") — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)

### Inferences
- These practices all serve one idea: information should flow straight to and from Jensen, with no filters. That makes the culture heavily dependent on him personally, which matters a great deal given his November 2026 resignation.
- The often-repeated claim that "many employees became millionaires" is supported qualitatively by Bloomberg and Business Insider. I found no audited count of how many.

### Gaps
- There is no reliable public figure for the number of Nvidia employee millionaires. Some outlets have cited internal survey numbers that I could not verify.
- I could not find the original source of the "no five-year plan" quote; the secondary source attributes it via Tae Kim's book.

## Long-term thinking, 33-year tenure, and the November 2026 resignation

### Takeaway
Jensen was CEO from the 1993 founding until November 11, 2026, roughly 33.5 years, which made him one of the longest-serving founder-CEOs in big tech. His patience is best shown by CUDA (2006): Wall Street hated it, the stock fell about 70% by the end of 2008, and it only paid off with AI after 2012. **His tenure ended abruptly in November 2026, when he resigned amid an affair scandal with reported national-security concerns.** The board's investigation was still ongoing as of November 13.

### Cited Findings
**Patience and long bets [Documented]**
- CUDA launched in late 2006. "Wall Street reacted with dismay," and by the end of 2008 the stock had fallen 70%. CUDA downloads peaked in 2009, then fell for three years. Board member Jim Gaither said they worked to protect the company from activist investors. Board member Dawn Hudson (joined 2013): "It was a distinctly flat, stagnant company." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- Geoffrey Hinton recalls asking Nvidia for a free GPU in 2009 after telling 1,000 researchers to buy Nvidia cards: "They said no." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution) (a humanizing, funny beat)
- Jensen at Stripe on long, hard bets: "If it also takes a long time to do, you can kind of flounder around for a couple years, nobody notices. So I could be incompetent for several years." — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang)
- On succession in mid-October 2026 (Bloomberg interview): "I'm really hoping that the next CEO of the company is already at the company, but 20 years from now, I hope I stay healthy and I hope I continue to earn my keep." — quoted by [NPR, Nov 12, 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/12/nx-s1-6037833/jensen-huang-nvidia-stepped-down). This was said weeks before his resignation.

**The resignation [Documented: company filing and multiple major outlets]**
- On November 11, 2026, Nvidia's board accepted the resignation of Jen-Hsun Huang as President, CEO and board member, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress (59) was named interim CEO, and Harvey C. Jones (73, a board member since 1993) became the first-ever Chair of the Board. The board is searching for a permanent CEO among internal and external candidates. — [Nvidia SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000082/nvda-20261111.htm); [Nvidia Newsroom, Nov 12, 2026](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition)
- Background, per NPR citing the New York Times: in late October 2026 the NYT reported Huang had a close relationship with a Chinese woman at a Singapore-based company buying Nvidia chips, whom US intelligence officials believed had been recruited by Chinese intelligence. In an email to staff on Sunday, November 8, he admitted cheating on his wife. The NYT later reported a second affair, with an Nvidia executive who reported to him. **NPR said it had not independently confirmed the NYT's reporting.** — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/12/nx-s1-6037833/jensen-huang-nvidia-stepped-down); [AP](https://apnews.com/live/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resignation-news-updates-11-12-2026)
- His email to staff, as quoted by Hindustan Times: "I was unfaithful to my wife. ... My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry." — [Hindustan Times, Nov 13, 2026](https://www.hindustantimes.com/trending/jensen-huangs-wife-lori-and-daughter-madison-are-behind-the-nvidia-co-founders-iconic-black-leather-jacket-look-101794554517785.html) (secondary; I did not find the full email from a primary source)
- Nvidia's independent directors, with a former federal prosecutor, are continuing to investigate him despite the resignation. Nvidia lost $585 billion in market value that week. — [NBC News, Nov 13, 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/nvidia-investigation-jensen-huang-continue-departure-sources-say-rcna609136)
- Stephen Witt, author of *The Thinking Machine*: "There's no obvious replacement. It's not even clear Nvidia can be the same kind of company without Jensen." — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/12/nx-s1-6037833/jensen-huang-nvidia-stepped-down)
- Hindustan Times names the alleged partners and reports a possible third relationship with another staffer, which Huang had not publicly addressed. — [Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/trending/jensen-huangs-wife-lori-and-daughter-madison-are-behind-the-nvidia-co-founders-iconic-black-leather-jacket-look-101794554517785.html). **Treat the second and third allegations, and the names, as unconfirmed reporting.** I recommend not naming individuals in the video.

### Inferences
- The documentary's arc changes completely. A story of 33 years of patience and endurance now ends in a sudden fall. Any statement about his culture, such as "intellectual honesty," "no privileged access to information," or how much the company depends on him personally, will be read in that light.
- The facts are still developing: the investigation is ongoing and allegations are only partly confirmed. The script should say "as of November 2026" and avoid definitive claims about the espionage angle.

### Gaps

- I did not check whether he retains any advisory role or his remaining Nvidia shareholding after resigning.

## Criticism of his style: demanding, intense, public criticism of staff

### Takeaway
Criticism is well documented from multiple reporters. Stephen Witt's book describes "the Wrath of Huang," including a public dressing-down of a chip architect in front of about 150 people that lasted up to two hours. Jensen himself admits to anger and says he is "never satisfied." Many employees nonetheless describe deep loyalty, and he rarely fires people.

### Cited Findings
- From *The Thinking Machine*: an "extraordinarily skilled and dedicated chip architect" was screamed at in front of roughly 150 colleagues: "just nonstop berating him for a good hour and a half. Honestly, maybe it was two hours." Staff call it "the Wrath of Huang." A former chief scientist: "He wants everyone to benefit. He would never just yell at some guy in the hall." — [Telegraph review](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/books/non-fiction/review-the-thinking-machine-stephen-witt/)
- An employee: "I will never forget the first time I saw him erupt. ... Jensen was always so charming and self-deprecating. Suddenly, he's screaming at the top of his voice in front of a hundred people." If a project slipped, the person responsible had to stand up and explain everything that went wrong, followed by "a withering analysis." — [Inc. excerpt from The Thinking Machine](https://www.inc.com/stephen-witt/how-jensen-huang-kicked-off-a-mad-dash-to-save-nvidia-in-its-early-years/91173298)
- Employees quoted in the book: "yelling at people is part of his motivational strategy," and he has "embraced candour to the extreme." In Witt's final interview, Jensen started shouting at Witt himself over a question about AI and jobs: "His anger seemed uncontained, omnidirectional, and wildly inappropriate." — [The Guardian review](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2025/apr/20/the-thinking-machine-stephen-witt-review-nvidia-chip-company-jensen-huang)
- Despite the "terrifying" temper, he was "loath to fire employees." — [Forbes review](https://www.forbes.com/sites/johntamny/2025/06/01/book-review-stephen-witts-take-on-nvidia-the-thinking-machine/). Witt writes that employees "worship him—I believe they would follow him out of the window of a skyscraper if he saw a market opportunity there." — [The Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2025/05/thinking-machine-jensen-huang-nvidia-book-review/682122/)

An employee: "Interacting with him is kind of like sticking your finger in the electric socket." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)

- Analyst Hans Mosesmann: "Typically, in Silicon Valley, you can get away with fudging it. You can't do that with Jensen. He will kind of lose his temper." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- Architect Hao Ko: "He can be very tough. He will undress you." Jensen grilled him about water fountains placed next to bathrooms, which building code requires, at the finished billion-dollar headquarters. Jensen: "I'm never satisfied. No matter what it is, I only see imperfections." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- On public failure reviews, Dwight Diercks: "If someone starts getting defensive, I know they're not going to make it." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- Both of his children worked in hospitality in their twenties and, "following years of paternal browbeating," now work at Nvidia. — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)
- Bloomberg: 10 current and former employees said "a culture problem is brewing" around grueling hours, and they spoke anonymously "for fear of retribution." — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-08-26/what-s-it-like-working-at-nvidia-you-may-get-rich-but-work-remains-stressful)

Jensen's reply: "The more you buy, the more you save." — [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/12/04/how-jensen-huangs-nvidia-is-powering-the-ai-revolution)

### Inferences
- "Public feedback" sounds like a principle when Jensen describes it ("feedback is learning"). Witnesses describe the same practice as hours-long public berating. The documentary can show both views side by side, since the sources agree it happened and differ only on how to read it.

### Gaps
- I found no named former employee publicly accusing him of abuse beyond the anonymous and book-sourced accounts. Formal complaints or lawsuits about his management, if any, were not found.
